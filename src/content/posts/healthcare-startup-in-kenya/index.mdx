This post is about money for a clinic that has not opened yet, or has only just opened. It covers who lends to clinics in Kenya and what each asks for, then what to keep from the first day so that you can apply when you are ready. The terms below were read from each lender's own site on 26 September 2026, and they change, so check them again before you apply.

## Why a new clinic struggles to borrow

Lenders want to see that a clinic can repay before they lend to it. The [Medical Credit Fund](https://www.medicalcreditfund.org/products-services/) asks for at least two years of audited financial statements on its Kenyan term loans. [KCB](https://ke.kcbgroup.com/products/sme-solutions-sme-secured-loan) asks for six months of business takings through a KCB account, or 12 months of statements from the bank you use now. Loans above KES 5 million also need three years of books.

A clinic that has not opened has none of that. So the first money is usually the owner's own: savings and family money, or a partner who puts in cash for a share. Our [Nairobi cost guide](/how-much-does-it-cost-to-open-a-clinic-in-nairobi/) puts a small clinic at about KES 3.2 million if you buy a plot and build, and about KES 835,000 of that is equipment and the first three months of running costs. Start smaller if you have to. A clinic that opens in rented rooms and keeps clean records is a better loan applicant a year later than one that borrowed from friends and kept nothing on paper.

## What the Medical Credit Fund lends

The Medical Credit Fund was established in 2009 as part of the PharmAccess Group, and it [describes itself](https://www.medicalcreditfund.org/who-we-are/) as the only fund dedicated to financing small and medium-sized healthcare businesses in Africa. It lends in Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria, Tanzania and Uganda, and its Kenya office is in Lavington, Nairobi. MCF says its loans run from EUR 100 to EUR 5 million and that it has lent more than EUR 200 million to over 2,200 healthcare businesses.

MCF lends in steps. A less experienced facility starts with a smaller loan and builds a repayment record before it borrows more. Every Kenyan product includes the SafeCare quality improvement programme, and the term loans include help developing a long-term investment plan.

Dr Tony Njuguna, proprietor of Grace Home Clinic in Banana Hill, Nairobi, is quoted on [MCF's site](https://www.medicalcreditfund.org/apply-for-a-loan/): "We have been a beneficiary of MCF’s Cash Advance product in the past years and have been able to purchase and equip our delivery room."

Its [products for Kenya](https://www.medicalcreditfund.org/products-services/) on 26 September 2026:

| Product | What it pays for | What it asks for | Terms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equipment term loan | Medical equipment | Two years of audited accounts | Repayments set per loan; guarantees, land or other security |
| Construction and renovation term loan | Building works on the premises | Two years of audited accounts | Repayments set per loan; guarantees, land or other security |
| Working capital term loan | Running costs | Two years of audited accounts | Repayments set per loan; guarantees, land or other security |
| Digital loan, mobile asset finance | Equipment and technology | Six months of mobile or digital revenues | Up to six months; 3% processing fee; a chattel mortgage on the asset |
| Digital loan | Short-term working capital | Three months of mobile or digital revenues | Six months; 3% processing fee; no conventional collateral |
| Claims advance | Working capital against insurance claims already submitted | Six months of mobile or digital revenues | Seven months; 3% processing fee; no conventional collateral |

Every product also asks for a private company in the health sector, with its registration documents and its licences and permits. If you plan to borrow from MCF, register the clinic as a company from the start rather than changing it later.

The digital loans are the ones a young clinic can reach first, because they are sized on the payments that come in by phone or card. Repayments are deducted from those incoming payments. You apply through a [form on MCF's site](https://www.medicalcreditfund.org/apply-for-a-loan/), and the team contacts you.

## Bank loans

**KCB** publishes its SME terms in full. The [SME secured loan](https://ke.kcbgroup.com/products/sme-solutions-sme-secured-loan) runs up to 36 months for working capital and up to 60 months for capital spending, and it is open to people who bank elsewhere. It is priced at KCB's variable base rate, 15.6% on the day we checked, plus a risk margin, with credit life cover at 0.34% a year. The [SME unsecured loan](https://ke.kcbgroup.com/products/sme-solutions-sme-unsecured-loan) goes up to KES 10 million without security, but only for existing KCB customers, based on KCB's internal scoring. For equipment, KCB's [asset-based finance](https://ke.kcbgroup.com/products/sme-solutions-asset-based-finance) covers up to 80% of the cost of specialised medical equipment over 60 months, secured on the equipment itself.

KCB asks for the same papers on each of these loans: the application form, a passport photo, your business licence or permit, your ID and KRA PIN, the registration or incorporation documents, and six months of takings through KCB or 12 months of statements from your bank.

**Co-operative Bank** lists [business loans](https://www.co-opbank.co.ke/business-banking/business-loans/) including asset finance, business mortgage and construction loans, an MSME term loan and the Msamaria Women's Loan for women who need working or investment capital. The page gives no rates, so ask a branch.

Ask Equity Bank and your own bank for their SME terms too, and compare each offer on the total cost and the security it takes.

## Investors who take a share

Angel investors and venture funds put in money for a share of the business. They want it back, usually when the business is sold or raises more money at a higher value. A single clinic is hard to sell a share of, so equity suits a plan for many sites better than one clinic. If you take a partner's money for a share, put the terms in writing with a lawyer before the money arrives, including what happens if one of you wants to leave.

## Crowdfunding

[M-Changa](https://www.mchanga.africa/mchanga-fees), a fundraising platform that runs fundraisers in Kenya, has a category for medical causes. It charges a platform fee of 4.25% on donations, or 3.5% if a fundraiser raises KES 3 million or more within 30 days. Donations by M-Pesa carry no processing fee, and cards and PayPal carry 2.5%. A bank withdrawal under KES 500,000 costs a flat KES 550.

Crowdfunding suits a clinic with a community cause behind it, such as a maternity room for an area that has none, more than a private business asking strangers for money. It also takes work: someone has to tell the story and thank every donor.

## Records to keep from the first day

Every lender above asks for some version of the same thing: proof of what the clinic takes and what it spends. Start keeping it before you need it.

1. **Bank the takings in the business's name.** KCB reads six months of takings through its account, and MCF's digital loans are sized on mobile and digital revenues. Cash that never reaches an account leaves no record.
2. **Register the business properly.** MCF lends to private companies in the health sector, and every lender asks for the registration documents with the licences and permits.
3. **Keep monthly accounts and have them audited every year.** MCF's term loans ask for two years of audited statements, so the clock starts with the first audit.
4. **Bill every visit.** A bill for each patient, including the ones who pay nothing today, is the record your accountant builds the books from.
