A health camp on a Sunday morning can screen a steady crowd: blood pressure and blood sugar checks, and a few quick consultations under a canopy. Some people are told their readings need a proper check-up. If nobody owns the follow-up, by Wednesday the forms are in a drawer and nobody has called them.

A camp like that has done its job on the day. What it lacks is a plan for the week after. This post is about that plan, written for clinics in India.

## Why camp follow-up breaks

Camps are usually planned for turnout. The banner, the venue, the doctors' time and the queue get organised, and the follow-up is left to whoever has a spare moment afterwards.

After the camp, the information sits in several places at once: paper forms in someone's bag, numbers saved in a WhatsApp chat, photos on a phone and the memory of the staff who were there. Nobody owns the list, so nobody works through it. The person with a high sugar reading was told to visit the clinic, but nothing reminds them, and within a week the advice has faded.

## Plan the follow-up before camp day

Settle four things before the camp:

1. **What the camp is for.** A diabetes and blood pressure camp, a women's health camp, a children's check-up or a specialist camp such as orthopaedics each brings a different group, and each needs a different follow-up slot at the clinic.
2. **One form for everyone.** Name, phone number, age, the reading or finding, what the camp doctor advised, and a box for whether the person agrees to be called or messaged by the clinic about the result. Ask the question in plain words and write down the answer.
3. **One owner for the list.** A named person at the desk takes the forms home from the camp and works through them in the days after it.
4. **Slots at the clinic.** Keep some appointments free in the week after the camp, so a person who agrees to come in can be offered a time on the call.

What each country's data protection law asks of a clinic that messages its patients is set out on the [patient engagement page](/features/patient-engagement/).

## At the camp: write down the result and the next step

Sort each person into a group before they leave the camp, and write the group on the form:

- **See a doctor this week.** A reading or a symptom the camp doctor wants checked soon.
- **Routine check.** Worth a visit in the next month or two.
- **Needs a test.** A lab test or scan before a consultation makes sense.
- **Nothing now.** Readings within range, with advice on when to check again.

Write down anything else a person mentions that needs a doctor. A parent who brought a child to a school camp and says she has been exhausted for months is a follow-up in her own right.

Give everyone a slip with their reading and the clinic's phone number, so they can call even if the clinic does not reach them.

If the camp site has a working internet connection, the desk can register the people who need follow-up as patients in EasyClinic at the camp, so the reading goes on a record and the booking can be made from the appointment screen. EasyClinic is cloud software and does not run fully offline today, so where the signal is poor, use the paper forms and enter those people at the clinic the next morning.

## The week after: call in order of need

On the first working morning after the camp, the list owner sorts the forms by group and starts with "see a doctor this week".

- Say who you are and which camp it was, and remind the person of the reading. Then offer a specific time: "The doctor can see you on Wednesday morning" is easier to say yes to than "come in when you can".
- If the person is busy this week, agree a date to call back and write it on the form.
- Book them into the clinic calendar like any other patient, with a note that they came from the camp and why they were asked to come in.
- Call only the people who agreed to be called, and stop when someone says they do not want to hear from the clinic.

On EasyClinic, a booked patient gets a reminder before the visit: SMS and email reminders come with every plan, and WhatsApp messages from the Premium plan. If someone books and does not come, call once more and offer a new time. Our post on [reducing patient no-shows](/top-strategies-for-reducing-patient-no-shows/) covers what else helps.

For the "routine check" group, one call offering a time in the next month or two is enough. Once someone from the camp becomes a patient and their chart records a condition such as diabetes, they are on the list whenever the clinic runs a recall campaign for that condition. EasyClinic's [patient engagement page](/features/patient-engagement/) describes those campaigns, which message the patients whose chart records the condition and ask them to book, so nobody has to keep a separate camp list.

## Keep the camp within the medical ethics rules

In India, the [Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002](https://nmc.org.in/page/rules-regulations-rules-regulations-of-erstwhile-mci-code-of-medical-ethics-regulations-2002), which the National Medical Commission [adopted in August 2023](https://www.nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/KEpwkMzgUwYNR49OrJaqvLcIpeytGPg7XGcorhTg.pdf) when it held its own 2023 conduct regulations in abeyance, set limits that apply to camps as much as to anything else a doctor does:

- **Soliciting patients.** Regulation 6.1.1 says soliciting patients directly or indirectly, by a physician, a group of physicians, an institution or an organisation, is unethical. It also bars publicity that invites attention to a doctor's skill, qualifications, achievements or specialities, and boasting of cases, operations, cures or remedies.
- **Public health talks.** Regulation 7.11 allows a doctor to give public lectures and talks on public health and hygienic living, and to announce them.
- **Referral fees.** Regulation 6.4.1 forbids a doctor to give or receive any gift, gratuity, commission or bonus for referring, recommending or procuring a patient. Paying a housing society or a pharmacy for each patient it sends after the camp falls under it.

The regulations do not mention health camps by name. Keep the camp's publicity to what the camp offers, and keep the follow-up to people whose results need a doctor. Ask your State Medical Council how it treats camp publicity before you print anything, and check with it for any change to the regulations.

## Count what came out of it

Once the calls are done, count five numbers on one sheet for each camp:

1. People screened.
2. People whose result needed follow-up.
3. People reached by phone.
4. People who booked.
5. People who came.

The gap between each number and the next shows where the follow-up leaks. A drop between screened and needing follow-up is expected: that is the screening doing its job. A big drop between reached and booked may mean the call is not offering a time, and a big drop between booked and came points to reminders. Keep the sheet for each camp, so the next one can be planned on the camp types and places that brought people back.

## What software does not do

Software does not fill the camp or make the call. It keeps the people who need follow-up on a record and sends their reminders. The follow-up still depends on one person owning the list in the week after the camp.
