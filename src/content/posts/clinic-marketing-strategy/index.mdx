Sooner or later a caller asks the front desk, "Do you have any offers?" Perhaps a clinic nearby has cut its fee. A discount is one answer. A marketing plan is a better one.

This post sets out a plan a clinic in India can run with the staff it already has. It has two halves: being chosen by a patient who has never visited, and being chosen again by the one who has. It starts with the rules, because they decide which tactics are open to a doctor at all.

## What the ethics rules allow a doctor in India

Registered medical practitioners in India are bound by the [Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002](https://www.nmc.org.in/page/rules-regulations-rules-regulations-of-erstwhile-mci-code-of-medical-ethics-regulations-2002). The National Medical Commission [made them effective again on 23 August 2023](https://www.nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/KEpwkMzgUwYNR49OrJaqvLcIpeytGPg7XGcorhTg.pdf), when it held its own 2023 conduct regulations in abeyance. For marketing, these clauses matter most:

- **Publicity about the doctor.** Regulation 6.1.1 calls soliciting patients, directly or indirectly, unethical. It forbids publicity that invites attention to a doctor's skill, qualifications, achievements or honours, and boasting of cases, operations, cures or remedies.
- **What may be announced.** The same regulation allows a formal announcement in the press on occasions such as starting practice or changing address, and a public declaration of charges.
- **Signboards and letterheads.** Regulation 6.1.2 treats the doctor's own photograph on a letterhead or signboard as self-advertisement. Regulation 7.13 limits the signboard to the doctor's name, qualifications, titles, speciality and registration number.
- **Articles and talks.** Regulation 7.11 says a doctor should not write articles or give interviews in the lay press on diseases and treatments that have the effect of advertising the doctor. It allows writing under the doctor's own name on public health and hygienic living, and broadcast or internet talks for the same purpose.
- **Institutions.** Regulation 7.12 lets an institution run by a doctor, such as a nursing home or private hospital, advertise in the lay press with no more than its name, the type of patients it admits, the facilities it offers and its fees.
- **Referral payments.** Regulation 6.4.1 forbids giving or receiving any gift, gratuity, commission or bonus for referring a patient. Regulation 7.19 forbids using touts or agents to bring patients in.

A complaint of professional misconduct goes to the appropriate Medical Council, which can hold an enquiry and remove a doctor's name from the register (regulation 8.2). This post follows the text on the National Medical Commission's site on 26 September 2026. Check with your State Medical Council before you rely on it for a particular campaign. What a doctor may post on Reels and Shorts is covered in [doctor personal branding](/doctor-personal-branding/).

## Being chosen by a patient who has never visited

Even a patient sent by a friend may look the clinic up before calling. What they find, and what happens when they call, decides whether they come.

- **The Google listing.** [Google says](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) a Business Profile with complete and accurate information is more likely to show up in local search results. Check the address, the hours for each day, the category and the phone number, and change them the day anything changes.
- **Reviews.** A listing with recent reviews gives a stranger something to go on. Ask every patient in the same way, and read [Google reviews for clinics](/google-reviews-for-clinics/) for what Google does not allow.
- **The phone.** A call that rings out may be a patient you never hear from. Put one person on the phone at the busy hours, and call back every missed number the same day.
- **Directions.** Put the nearest landmark and the entrance in the listing and in the message that confirms the appointment, so a new patient is not calling from the pharmacy next door to ask where you are.

Then there is the fee. A caller who asks for offers may really be asking whether the clinic is worth its fee. Say the consultation fee plainly when asked, and say what the first visit includes. A discount brings patients who came for the price, and it gives them no reason to come back when another clinic cuts deeper.

## Being chosen again

A patient who has visited once already knows the clinic, and is easy to lose by forgetting to ask them back.

- **Book the next visit before the patient leaves.** When the doctor says "come back in two weeks", the desk fixes the date at the counter, and a reminder goes out before it.
- **Recall patients who are due.** A patient with diabetes or high blood pressure needs regular reviews. When reviews fall due, a recall message asks them to book, so the follow-up does not depend on someone at the desk remembering.
- **Keep the notes where every doctor can see them.** A returning patient who sees a different doctor should not have to tell the story again. When the notes sit on one patient record, the second doctor starts where the first one stopped.
- **Tell patients about delays.** A patient told the doctor is running thirty minutes late can decide to wait. A patient told nothing may decide the clinic does not care.

In EasyClinic, recall campaigns message the patients whose chart records a condition, diabetes or hypertension for example, and ask them to book. When the doctor runs late, waiting patients are told of the change to their wait automatically. ## Patient stories, with permission

Patient stories are where the rules bite hardest. Regulation 7.17 says a doctor must not publish photographs or case reports of patients in a way that identifies them without their permission, and that no consent is needed if the identity is not disclosed. Regulation 6.1.1 still forbids boasting of cases or cures, with or without consent.

Read together, a before-and-after result shown as proof of the doctor's skill is the riskiest kind of post. What sits more comfortably is material with no patient in it, on public health or healthy habits, which regulation 7.11 allows a doctor to write and talk about under their own name. A patient's own words about the visit, such as the wait or how clearly things were explained, still promote the clinic, so treat them as publicity too. That is this post's reading of the regulations, and a State Medical Council may read them differently. Whatever you publish about a patient, keep their written permission on file.

## Ask patients what they thought

A short survey after the visit can show you a complaint before it turns up in a review. Ask about the whole visit, from the phone call and the reception to the bill, not only the consultation. Read the answers each month and fix the complaint that comes up most.

EasyClinic sends a feedback survey after the visit that asks the patient to rate it, so an unhappy patient has somewhere to say so and the clinic can call them back. Sending it on WhatsApp needs at least the Premium plan, and the price list does not say which plan includes the survey itself.

## Four numbers to check each month

1. New patients, and where each one heard of the clinic. Ask at registration and write the answer down.
2. Missed calls that nobody called back.
3. Follow-ups booked at the counter, and how many of those patients came.
4. The average feedback score, and the complaint that came up most.

None of these needs an agency. They need someone who looks at them on the same day each month.
