The short answer to how to open a clinic in Ghana is this: register a company, register every clinician with their council, prepare premises that meet HeFRA’s clinic standard, then apply to the [Health Facilities Regulatory Agency (HeFRA)](https://hefra.gov.gh/) for a facility licence under the [Health Institutions and Facilities Act, 2011 (Act 829)](https://extranet.who.int/cpcd/sites/default/files/public_file_repository/GHA_Ghana_Health-Institutions-and-Facilities-Act_2011.pdf). No clinic may open before HeFRA licenses it. After that come tax registration, NHIS credentialing, data protection registration and staff contracts. This guide prices each step in Ghanaian cedi from official fee schedules and shows where a cloud system such as [EMR software in Ghana](/emr-software-in-ghana/) fits once you open.

## Requirements to open a private clinic in Ghana

To open a private clinic in Ghana you need four things: a registered business, registered clinicians, premises that meet HeFRA’s minimum clinic standard, and a HeFRA licence. Everything else, from tax to NHIS, builds on those four.

### How to open a clinic in Ghana: the four pillars

Think of how to open a clinic in Ghana as four pillars that must all stand before HeFRA will inspect you. The company gives you a legal owner. The registered clinicians give you a practitioner in charge. The premises give the assessors something to measure against the clinic standard. The HeFRA application ties the three together. Miss one and the others wait.

### The legal requirement: a HeFRA licence

Section 11 of Act 829 says a person shall not operate a facility unless it is licensed under the Act. The First Schedule lists the facilities covered. It includes medical and dental health centres, clinics and hospitals, eye care clinics, maternity homes, physiotherapy clinics, medical assistant clinics, clinical laboratories, diagnostic imaging clinics, and pharmacies and chemical shops ([HeFRA general requirements](https://hefra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/GENERAL-REQUIREMENTS-VERSION-1.4-18-Jan-2018.pdf)).

HeFRA’s own guidance is blunt: new facilities are not allowed to operate until they have been accredited. The licence must hang in a prominent place that patients can see (section 19).

### What section 12 of Act 829 asks of the applicant

Act 829 sets conditions on the person, not only the building. HeFRA will not grant a licence unless it is satisfied that:

- The applicant, or the managing employee, has at least five years’ post-qualification experience in a recognised institution relevant to the practice.
- The past and present conduct of everyone involved gives reasonable grounds to believe the clinic will follow the law. This covers directors, shareholders and anyone with a beneficial interest.
- The applicant can provide the resources and equipment the practice needs.

### HeFRA’s minimum standard for a clinic

HeFRA publishes a specific requirements document for each facility type. The clinic standard, version 1.3, is the one most private GP practices must meet ([HeFRA specific requirements for a clinic](https://hefra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/3.-SPECIFIC-REQUIREMENTS-FOR-CLINIC-V1.3.pdf)). The main points:

| **Area** | **HeFRA minimum for a clinic** |
| --- | --- |
| Practitioner in charge | One medical doctor with at least five years’ post-qualification experience, in good standing with the Medical and Dental Council |
| Other staff | At least two professional nurses, nurse practitioners or midwives, in good standing with the Nursing and Midwifery Council |
| Core services | Minor ailments, primary health care, health promotion, minor surgery such as suturing, maternal and child health including antenatal and postnatal care, family planning, immunisation and normal deliveries |
| Required rooms | Information desk, waiting and reception area of at least 4 x 3 metres, triage area, nurses’ room, consulting room of at least 4 x 3 metres, treatment room of at least 2 x 3 metres, observation ward with at least two beds, sluice room, dispensary, basic laboratory, medical records area |
| Utilities | Adequate water supply, constant electricity with a working backup supply, washable floors with drains, clean patient toilets and baths |
| Equipment | Thermometer, stethoscope, sphygmomanometer, diagnostic set, glucometer, adult and baby scales, stadiometer, fetoscope, autoclave, dressing trolley, emergency tray, suture kit, resuscitation equipment, delivery tray and bed, adult hospital bed, wheelchair, stretcher |
| Safety | Marked exits that open from inside, fire extinguishers tagged within the last six months, first aid kit, mosquito netting on windows and doors, colour-coded waste handling, sharps disposal |
| Records | Equipment records, NHIS claim forms, inspection records, waste tracking log, accident and injury log, infection log, monthly or quarterly disease data |

The clinic standard also expects maternity space, including a labour room and lying-in room. If you plan an outpatient-only practice, discuss the right category with your HeFRA zonal office before you sign a lease. A dental, eye, ENT or physiotherapy practice has its own standard on the same [HeFRA requirements page](https://hefra.gov.gh/license-requirement/).

## Who can own and run a clinic in Ghana

Anyone can own the company that holds a clinic. Running it is different. HeFRA’s clinic standard requires a medical doctor with five years’ experience as the practitioner in charge. Act 829 also requires that the applicant or managing employee has five years’ relevant experience.

### Can a nurse or physician assistant open a clinic in Ghana?

Not a clinic in HeFRA’s sense, but there are routes. HeFRA’s standard for a health centre allows a physician assistant with five years’ post-qualification experience, in good standing with the Medical and Dental Council, as the practitioner in charge, supported by at least one midwife and two nurses ([HeFRA health centre requirements](https://hefra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2.-SPECIFIC-REQUIREMENTS-FOR-HEALTH-CENTRE-V1.3.pdf)). A health centre may do normal deliveries but no caesarean sections.

A professional midwife with five years’ experience, in good standing with the Nursing and Midwifery Council, can be in charge of a maternity home. HeFRA’s standard for that category also asks for proof of access to a medical practitioner, two nurses and two hospital assistants ([HeFRA maternity home requirements](https://hefra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/13.-SPECIFIC-REQUIREMENTS-FOR-A-MATERNITY-HOME-V1.3.pdf)).

One warning for physician assistants. In 2020 the NHIA said it would stop credentialing privately owned health centres run by physician assistants unless they upgraded their HeFRA status, a move the Ghana Physician Assistants Association called discriminatory ([Citi Newsroom, 18 July 2020](https://www.citinewsroom.com/2020/07/nhia-denies-allegations-of-discrimination-against-physician-assistants/)). Ask your NHIA regional office before you commit to this model.

### Foreign doctors and investors

Section 13 of Act 829 lets a non-citizen own or operate a practice if they hold a valid work permit from the Minister for the Interior, are registered with the relevant Ghanaian professional body, have at least five years’ post-qualification experience, and have a Ghanaian professional in a similar discipline as a business partner. A non-citizen must also have practised in Ghana for at least one year in a recognised institution.

### Registering with the Medical and Dental Council

Every doctor and physician assistant must hold current registration with the Medical and Dental Council and pay the annual retention fee. The Council’s published fees include GH¢658 for a doctor’s retention, GH¢1,366 for full registration including the form, GH¢593 for provisional registration, and GH¢823 for a certificate of good standing. For physician assistants, retention is GH¢494 and full registration is GH¢873 ([MDC fees and charges](https://www.mdcghana.org/fees-and-charges/)). The same figures, minus the GH¢50 form fee, appear in the [Fees and Charges (Miscellaneous Provisions) (Amendment) Regulations, 2025 (L.I. 2512)](https://nca.org.gh/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/FEES-AND-CHARGES-MISCELLANEOUS-PROVISIONS-REGULATIONS-2025.pdf), made on 23 July 2025.

Nurses and midwives renew their Professional Identification Number (PIN) every year. Under L.I. 2512 the Nursing and Midwifery Council charges GH¢71 for an annual PIN renewal and GH¢455 for a practitioner without a PIN.

The documents needed to open a clinic in Ghana come from section 12(2) of Act 829 and HeFRA’s general requirements. Gather them before you buy the application form, because the form must be returned within 90 days with everything attached.

For NHIS credentialing you will later need a single merged PDF of your business registration certificate, HeFRA certificate and, where you dispense, your Pharmacy Council certificate ([NHIA credentialing page](https://nhis.gov.gh/credentialing)).

## Step by step guide to starting a clinic in Ghana

This step by step guide to starting a clinic in Ghana follows the order that saves the most time. Company first, people second, premises third, HeFRA fourth. Do not pay for a lease or equipment until you know which HeFRA category you are applying for.

1. **Register the company.** Most clinics register as a company limited by shares at the [Office of the Registrar of Companies](https://orc.gov.gh/service/incorporation-of-company-limited-by-shares/). You need at least two directors, a company secretary, an auditor’s consent letter, statutory declarations, stated capital details and a beneficial ownership declaration. The incorporation and filing fee is GH¢585, with an optional VIP service at GH¢1,885. Stamp duty on stated capital is 1 percent under L.I. 2512. Your Ghana Card PIN serves as your TIN; non-resident foreigners apply to GRA for a TIN with a passport ([Citi Newsroom, 28 April 2026](https://citinewsroom.com/2026/04/how-to-register-a-business-in-ghana-and-what-foreign-investors-must-know/)).
2. **Confirm every clinician’s registration.** The doctor in charge must show five years’ post-qualification experience and current Medical and Dental Council retention. Nurses and midwives need a current PIN. If you will dispense medicines, your pharmacist or dispensing technician must be registered with the Pharmacy Council.
3. **Choose and fit out premises against the HeFRA clinic standard.** Use the room sizes and equipment list in the table above as your fit-out checklist. Get preliminary planning approval from the Assembly, an EPA permit and a fire certificate. Under L.I. 2512 the Fire Service charges GH¢1,000 for a commercial building fire certificate plus a GH¢250 processing fee and a GH¢31 application form.
4. **Buy the HeFRA application form and submit the package.** HeFRA says the process starts with buying the form from a HeFRA office or an approved office in your area. You have 90 days to return it with every attachment. HeFRA then issues an inspection letter.
5. **Pass the HeFRA inspection.** Assessors visit and check staff, services, premises and equipment against the clinic standard. If findings are unsatisfactory, HeFRA gives you time to fix them and re-inspects. The Registrar may issue a provisional licence within 30 days of receiving the application; the Board issues the full licence at its next quarterly meeting after the zonal committee recommends you (Act 829, section 14).
6. **Register for tax.** Register the company with the [Ghana Revenue Authority](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/corporate-income-tax/). The general corporate income tax rate is 25 percent, paid in quarterly instalments. Medical services performed by or under the supervision of a registered health professional are exempt from VAT under the First Schedule of the VAT Act, 2025 (Act 1151), which took effect on 1 January 2026 ([Tax Law Ghana](https://www.taxlawgh.com/ghana-vat-exempt-zero-rated-relief)). Non-medical sales, such as a shop or canteen, may still be taxable, so get written advice.
7. **Register as an employer with SSNIT.** Take your certificate of incorporation, Ghana Card, TIN, Assembly permit and staff details to a SSNIT branch. Total contributions are 18.5 percent of basic salary: 13 percent from the employer and 5.5 percent from the employee, split 13.5 percent to SSNIT and 5 percent to a Tier 2 scheme, due within 14 days of the following month ([SSNIT employer registration](https://www.ssnit.org.gh/become-an-employer/)).
8. **Get your Assembly business operating permit.** Each Metropolitan, Municipal or District Assembly sets its own fee in a yearly fee-fixing resolution. As an example, Tamale Metropolitan Assembly’s 2023 resolution charged GH¢1,210 a year for an outpatient hospital or clinic and GH¢2,178 for in- and outpatient facilities ([Tamale Metropolitan Assembly 2023 fee-fixing resolution](https://tma.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/TMA-2023-Fees-fixing-4th-pdf.pdf)). Accra’s Assembly has agreed in principle to a 25 percent rise in permit fees for 2027 ([Rainbow Radio, 3 August 2026](https://rainbowradioonline.com/2026/08/03/ama-okaikoi-south-businesses-agree-on-proposed-25-increase-in-bop-fees-for-2027/)).
9. **Apply for NHIS credentialing.** Once the HeFRA certificate is in hand, request an account on the [NHIA credentialing portal](https://nhis.gov.gh/credentialing). The application processing fee for a clinic is GH¢2,000. A maternity home or health centre pays GH¢1,200 and a primary hospital or polyclinic pays GH¢3,000. If you want to understand how insurance billing works day to day, see how a clinic system handles [insurance billing and claims](/payor-management/).
10. **Register with the Data Protection Commission.** Your clinic processes health data, which is a special category under the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843). All data controllers must register on the [DPC portal](https://dpc.gov.gh/registration/). Under L.I. 2512 the fee is GH¢156 for a small data controller with turnover of GH¢90,000 or less and up to 50 staff or customers, and GH¢1,170 for a medium controller with turnover above GH¢90,000 but below GH¢5 million. Renewal requires a compliance report.
11. **License the dispensary.** If the clinic will keep and dispense medicines, apply to the Pharmacy Council. L.I. 2512 sets a GH¢650 processing fee, a GH¢600 registration fee and a GH¢600 renewal fee for a private clinic pharmacy.
12. **Set up records and reporting before day one.** Section 20 of Act 829 requires every licensee to send health service data to the Ministry every three months. HeFRA also inspects at least once a year and checks your registers. Choose your record system before you open, not after. Our guide to the [best clinic management software in Ghana](/best-clinic-management-software-ghana/) explains what to look for.

## Common mistakes

- **Opening before the HeFRA licence arrives.** This is the offence HeFRA prosecutes most often. In October 2025 HeFRA closed 17 facilities nationwide for expired licences or unsafe conditions, with police alongside its teams ([Citi Newsroom, 9 October 2025](https://citinewsroom.com/2025/10/hefra-shuts-down-17-unlicensed-health-facilities-nationwide/)). Treat the licence as the start date.
- **Letting the three-year licence lapse.** Failing to renew and continuing to practise is a separate offence under section 23. HeFRA gives administrative fines and a two-week grace period before it closes a facility ([Ghana News Agency, 8 October 2025](https://gna.org.gh/2025/10/hefra-crackdowns-on-unlicensed-health-facility-operators/)).
- **Signing a lease before checking the HeFRA category.** A consulting room smaller than 4 x 3 metres, or no space for an observation ward, means a failed inspection or a costly refit.
- **Adding services without approval.** Section 18 requires Board approval for any service not in your original application. Starting ultrasound, a lab or deliveries without it is an offence.
- **Treating EPA, fire and Assembly permits as optional.** They are attachments to your HeFRA application, and the EPA ordered all clinics without permits to regularise in January 2026 ([GBC Ghana, 10 January 2026](https://www.gbcghanaonline.com/news/epa-orders-businesses-to-regularise-permits-as-enforcement-intensifies/2026/)).
- **Hiring staff whose council registration has lapsed.** Practising without the appropriate registration is an offence for the clinic as well as the clinician. Check PIN cards and retention receipts every January.
- **Skipping Data Protection Commission registration.** Patient records are sensitive personal data under Act 843. Registration is cheap compared with an enforcement visit. Our guide to [data privacy laws in Kenya](/data-privacy-laws-in-kenya/) shows how a neighbouring regulator applies similar rules.
- **Counting on NHIS cash flow too early.** The NHIA paid GH¢1.64 billion in claims across public, private and mission facilities in 2026 and the Private Health Facilities Association of Ghana welcomed the more regular payments, but it also asked for monthly reimbursement and a claims adjudication committee ([MyJoyOnline, 3 October 2026](https://www.myjoyonline.com/gh%C2%A21-64bn-nhis-claims-paid-in-2026-as-private-health-facilities-welcome-improved-reimbursements/)). Keep a cash buffer.
- **Forgetting the quarterly data return.** Section 20 is an offence clause too. Build the report into your records system from the first patient.

If you remember one thing about how to open a clinic in Ghana, make it this: the licence comes before the first patient, and renewal comes before expiry.

## How to open a clinic in Ghana: common questions

### Who licenses private clinics in Ghana?

The Health Facilities Regulatory Agency (HeFRA) licenses all public and private health facilities under the Health Institutions and Facilities Act, 2011 (Act 829). The Ministry of Health oversees HeFRA, so the first step in how to open a clinic in Ghana is a HeFRA application.

### How long is a HeFRA licence valid?

A provisional licence from the Registrar lasts six months. A full licence from the HeFRA Board lasts three years and must then be renewed.

### Can a nurse open a clinic in Ghana?

Not a clinic under HeFRA’s clinic standard, which requires a doctor in charge. A professional midwife with five years’ experience can be in charge of a maternity home, and a physician assistant with five years’ experience can be in charge of a health centre.

### Do I need to be a doctor to own a clinic in Ghana?

No. Any person or company can own the business, but the practitioner in charge must be a doctor with at least five years’ post-qualification experience, and the owners must pass HeFRA’s good-conduct test under section 12.

### Can a foreigner open a clinic in Ghana?

Yes, under section 13 of Act 829, if they hold a work permit, are registered with the Ghanaian professional body, have five years’ experience, have practised in Ghana for a year, and have a Ghanaian professional in a similar discipline as a business partner.

### What is the penalty for running an unlicensed clinic in Ghana?

Up to 5,000 penalty units (GH¢60,000 at GH¢12 a unit), up to ten years’ imprisonment, or both, plus 10 penalty units a day for a continuing offence, and the court may close the facility.

### How much does company registration cost in Ghana?

The Office of the Registrar of Companies charges GH¢585 to incorporate a company limited by shares, plus stamp duty of 1 percent of stated capital. VIP service costs GH¢1,885 extra.

### Are medical services subject to VAT in Ghana?

No. Medical services performed by or under the supervision of a registered health professional are exempt under the First Schedule of the VAT Act, 2025 (Act 1151), in force since 1 January 2026. Confirm non-medical income with GRA.

### How much does NHIS credentialing cost for a clinic?

The NHIA lists an application processing fee of GH¢2,000 for a clinic, GH¢1,200 for a maternity home or health centre and GH¢3,000 for a primary hospital or polyclinic.

### Does my clinic need to register with the Data Protection Commission?

Yes. All data controllers must register under the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843). A small controller pays GH¢156 and a medium controller pays GH¢1,170 under L.I. 2512.

### Can I add a laboratory or ultrasound later?

Yes, but you must apply to the HeFRA Board first under section 18 of Act 829. Imaging equipment also needs Nuclear Regulatory Authority certification.
