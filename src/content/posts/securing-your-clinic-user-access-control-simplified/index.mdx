On paper, anyone who can reach the file shelf can read the file. Clinic software can do better than that, but only if the clinic sets it up. A system where the whole front desk shares one password is a file shelf with a lock that nobody uses.

This post is about who in a clinic should open what, and the habits that keep it that way after the first week.

## What role-based access means in a clinic

Every person who uses the system gets a login of their own, and each login carries a role, such as doctor, nurse, receptionist or pharmacist. The role decides which screens the login can open. You decide the access once for each job, and each new person gets the role for their job, so a new receptionist starts on day one with the same access as the last one.

The principle is need to know. The receptionist books appointments and takes payments. The pharmacist dispenses what the doctor prescribed. Neither needs the consultation notes to do the job, and a login that cannot open the notes cannot leak them.

## Who needs to see what

This table is a starting point for a small clinic or a polyclinic, not a rule. Adjust it to how your clinic works and write down what you decide, so the next manager does not have to guess.

| Role | Needs to open | Does not need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receptionist | Registration, appointments, bills and payments | Clinical notes and prescriptions |
| Nurse | Vitals and the notes of the patients in their care | The till and the financial reports |
| Doctor | The full chart of the patients they see, and prescribing | The day's accounts, unless they own the clinic |
| Pharmacist | The prescriptions to dispense, and the stock | Consultation notes beyond the prescription |
| Accountant | The bills and the financial reports | Clinical notes |
| Owner | Reports across the clinic | Clinical notes, unless they also treat patients |

The receptionist row matters most, because the front desk sees every patient who walks in. On Easy Clinic the reception login comes with the price. It is not allowed into the EMR and cannot issue prescriptions. Prescriptions are written on a doctor's login, and each doctor has a licence of their own.

## One login for each person

A shared login undoes everything above. It starts for a sensible reason: the desk is busy, and logging out and in again between patients feels slow. Then an appointment disappears or a payment is recorded twice, and the log says only that "reception" did it.

A shared login breaks two things. The audit log records the login, not the person, so it cannot tell you which receptionist made the change. And when one of them leaves, the password has to change for everyone, which is easy to put off.

The same goes for a doctor's login. If a nurse prints the prescriptions on the doctor's login to save time, the doctor's name is on prescriptions the doctor did not write. Visiting consultants and locums should get logins of their own too, closed when the arrangement ends.

On Easy Clinic, logins for your staff come with every plan.

## Keep sensitive notes to a smaller circle

Some notes need a tighter circle than the rest of the chart: psychiatric history, HIV status, sexual health, a disclosure of violence at home. A patient who tells the counsellor expects the counsellor to keep it, not the dermatologist down the corridor who shares the same record.

Decide which kinds of note your clinic treats as sensitive, and write down which roles may read them. If your software has a setting called confidential notes, ask exactly which roles can still read a confidential note before you rely on it. The [mental health page](/mental-health/) covers how access works for a psychiatry or counselling practice on Easy Clinic.

## Keep a record of who did what

An audit log is the record of what each user did in the system. Easy Clinic keeps audit logs for each user, and the cash till and stock adjustments carry audit trails of their own.

An audit log does not stop anyone opening a record their role allows. It tells you afterwards, and only if someone looks. Pick a day each month to read it. If your log shows who opened which record, start with records opened by people who were not involved in that patient's care. Tell your staff that the log exists and that it is read.

## When staff come and go

Settings are the easy part. Access drifts as the staff list changes.

- **Joining:** create the login on the first day, with the role for the job. Nobody starts work on a colleague's login while theirs is being set up.
- **Changing job:** when a nurse moves to the front desk, change the role. Do not add the new role on top of the old one.
- **Leaving:** close the login on the last working day, before the person leaves the building, and change any clinic password they knew, such as the clinic's email account.
- **Every quarter:** go through the list of active logins and close any that belong to people who have gone.

## If you run more than one branch

With two branches, the question becomes which branch a person can see as well as which screens. On Easy Clinic a doctor who works at two branches signs in with the same login at both, and a role's access can be set location by location. A chain can also give its owned clinics and its franchisees different levels of access. ## What the law asks, with India as an example

In India, [rule 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf) lists the minimum security safeguards a data fiduciary must take for the personal data in its possession or under its control. They include measures to control access to the computer resources used to process the data, and logs that show who accessed it, monitored and reviewed so that unauthorised access is found. The logs are kept for one year unless another law requires otherwise.

A data fiduciary is whoever decides the purpose and means of processing personal data, under [section 2(i) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf). For patient records that is usually the clinic, and rule 6 also covers data that a software vendor handles on the clinic's behalf. As notified, rule 6 comes into force eighteen months after the Rules were published on 13 November 2025, under [rule 1 of the same Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf). Our [guide to patient data privacy laws in India](/patient-data-privacy-laws-in-india/) covers the rest of the law. Other countries have their own data protection laws, so ask a lawyer which apply to your clinic.

## Habits that matter as much as the settings

The settings do not help if the habits at the desk undo them.

- Lock the screen when you walk away from the computer, even for a minute.
- Keep passwords off sticky notes on the monitor and out of the WhatsApp group.
- If your system offers a second step at login, such as a code sent to a phone, turn it on for the doctors and the owner first.
- Show every new member of staff what their role can and cannot open, on their first day.
