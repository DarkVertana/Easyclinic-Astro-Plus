This post is for a clinic that tells patients with diabetes or high blood pressure to come back in three months, and then has no way of knowing who did. The plan below uses four messages over about five weeks. It works on WhatsApp or by SMS, and the wording is yours to change.

## Why chronic patients go quiet

The review date is written at the foot of the prescription, and the patient takes the paper home. When they feel well, the date slips past. Nobody at the desk has a list of who was due last week, so nobody calls, and on a busy day the calls to overdue patients are the first job dropped.

A patient who misses a review has not necessarily decided to leave the clinic. The date may simply have gone by. A message on the right day gives them a reason to book. A message on the wrong day, or the fifth one in a week, gives them a reason to block the number, and [WhatsApp's business messaging policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy) says that asking for a separate opt-in for each kind of message lowers the risk of people blocking a business for messages they did not expect.

## Start from the review date

Group patients by where they stand against the review date the doctor set, not by how long ago they last came. Each group gets one message, and a patient moves to the next group only if they have not booked.

| Where the patient stands | What the clinic does |
| --- | --- |
| Review due in 7 days | First reminder |
| Review due today | Second reminder, with an easy way to book |
| 7 days overdue | Third message, with a way to ask a question first |
| 30 days overdue | Last message, then stop |
| More than 30 days overdue | No more messages; the doctor decides whether a call is worth making |

The list needs a review date for every patient on long-term care. If your doctors write "review in three months" on the prescription but not in the record, start there, because nothing else in this plan works without it.

## Four messages, and then stop

The sample wording below is written for this page. Words in round brackets are for your clinic to fill in.

| When | What the message does | Sample wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 days before the review | A plain reminder | "This is (clinic name). Your review with Dr (name) is due on (date). Reply BOOK and we will send you a time, or reply STOP to stop these reminders." |
| On the day it is due | Makes booking easy | "Your review with Dr (name) is due today. If today does not suit you, reply with a day that does and we will find a slot." |
| 7 days overdue | Removes the reasons not to come | "We have not seen you for your review yet. If someone in your family books your visits, you can forward this message to them. If you have a question first, reply CALL and we will ring you during clinic hours." |
| 30 days overdue | The last message | "This is our last reminder about your review with Dr (name). Whenever you are ready, reply BOOK or call us on (number), and we will start from your last visit." |

Four messages in five weeks is slower than a message a day, and that is deliberate. Each one has a single job and a single action. The third one allows for a family member who books the patient's visits. The last one tells the patient the messages are ending, so a patient who is embarrassed about missing visits has a way back that does not feel like being chased.

Someone has to read the replies. A reply that says BOOK at 9pm is a patient ready to come in, and it needs an answer the next morning. If the clinic sends on the WhatsApp Business Platform, a reply also opens a [24-hour customer service window](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing), during which the clinic can answer in its own words; outside that window, a message the clinic starts has to be on a template Meta has approved. In EasyClinic, Cura AI answers the standard questions when a patient writes back on WhatsApp, so fewer of them turn into phone calls.

## What to leave out of a reminder

Leave out the diagnosis and any result, unless the patient has said they are happy to see them. "Your review with Dr (name) is due" tells the patient what they need to know. "Your diabetes review is due" also tells anyone who glances at the lock screen. WhatsApp's [business messaging policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy) also says not to send or ask for health information on WhatsApp where the law requires a system held to a higher standard for it.

Leave out pressure. Words like "urgent" or "you have missed" read as a telling-off, and a patient who feels told off can simply not reply. If the doctor thinks a patient is at real risk, that patient needs a phone call from the clinic, not a sharper message.

## What a blue tick tells you, and what it does not

Meta's reference for business messages defines the [read status](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/reference/messages/status) as the message being displayed in an open chat on the patient's phone, shown as two blue ticks. One tick means it was sent, and two ticks that have not turned blue mean it reached the phone.

That is less than it looks. Two blue ticks do not tell you who was holding the phone or whether the patient means to book. Use the ticks only to decide the next step:

- One tick that never becomes two means the message has not reached the phone. Try SMS or a call, because the number may have changed.
- Read with no reply means the patient gets the next message in the plan, on its day, and nothing sooner.
- A reply or a booking is the signal that counts. Take that patient off the list.
- A STOP, on WhatsApp or said across the desk, ends the messages for good.

For booked appointments, EasyClinic tracks which patients have acknowledged their reminder, so the desk can call only the ones who have not.

## Consent before the first reminder

WhatsApp's [business messaging policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy) lets a business message a person only after the person has given it their number and agreed to receive its messages. How to ask is left to the business, within the law of its country. The policy suggests asking separately for each kind of message, so ask at registration whether the patient wants reminders about their visits, and keep that apart from any offers the clinic sends. Write the answer down with the patient's details.

Stopping has to be as easy as starting. WhatsApp's policy requires a business to respect every request to opt out, whether it arrives on WhatsApp or anywhere else. In India, [section 6(4) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf) gives a person the right to withdraw consent at any time, as easily as they gave it, and the DPDP Rules notified in November 2025 [phase the Act in over 18 months](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014). The [consent table on our patient engagement page](/features/patient-engagement/#consent-by-country) sets out what the law asks in India, Kenya, the UAE and Nigeria, in general terms.

## What EasyClinic does here, and what it does not

EasyClinic sends a reminder for the review without anyone at the desk remembering to. The doctor sets the follow-up in the consult, and the patient gets an automated reminder by SMS and email on every plan, or on WhatsApp on Premium. A recall campaign picks the patients whose chart records a condition, such as diabetes or hypertension, and sends each of them a message asking them to book.

We do not claim that it builds the overdue groups in the table above by date or sends the later messages on its own, because neither is confirmed. In this plan the later messages are the desk's job, worked from the calendar and the recall list. We also have no measured figure for how many overdue patients come back after a plan like this, so this post gives none.
