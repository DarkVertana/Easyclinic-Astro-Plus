This checklist is for a clinic that is already open. The licences for opening are in [how to start a clinic in India](/clinic-in-india/), and patient privacy has its own [guide](/patient-data-privacy-laws-in-india/). Each rule below links to its source. Rules differ from state to state, so treat this as a list of things to check, and ask a lawyer where your state's rules are unclear.

## Registration under the Clinical Establishments Act

The [Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/) provides for the registration and regulation of clinical establishments, with minimum standards for their facilities and services. It does not apply everywhere. The Act's website says it has been adopted in 19 states and union territories: Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Mizoram, Rajasthan, Sikkim, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and every union territory except Delhi.

If your clinic is in one of them, it registers under the Act, and the website issues provisional and permanent certificates. If it is not, ask the state health department which law registers clinics in your state.

For a running clinic, the check is simple. Keep the certificate current with the renewal date in the diary, and re-read the registration whenever you add a service or move premises.

## What the doctors' ethics regulations ask

The [Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002](https://www.nmc.org.in/page/rules-regulations-rules-regulations-of-erstwhile-mci-code-of-medical-ethics-regulations-2002), published on the National Medical Commission's website, put several duties on each doctor that land on the clinic's paperwork:

- **Registration number.** Every physician displays the registration number given by the State Medical Council or the Medical Council of India in four places: the clinic, prescriptions, certificates and money receipts (1.4.1).
- **Records of indoor patients.** They are kept for three years from the start of treatment (1.3.1). This rule is for indoor patients, and the regulations set no period for outpatient records.
- **Requests for records.** When the patient or an authorised attendant asks for the records, the request is acknowledged and the documents are issued within 72 hours (1.3.2). The same applies to a request from the legal authorities.
- **Medical certificates.** The doctor keeps a register of the certificates issued, with a copy of each one (1.3.3).
- **Computerised records.** The regulations ask that efforts be made to computerise medical records for quick retrieval (1.3.4).
- **Generic names.** Every physician should, as far as possible, prescribe drugs with generic names (1.5).

The 72-hour rule is easy to miss at a busy desk. Decide who at the desk handles a request for records, and where the copies come from, before a patient asks.

Before you destroy any old record, check the retention period with your state medical council or a lawyer. The three-year rule covers indoor patients only, so these regulations do not settle how long to keep an outpatient's file.

## Biomedical waste

Every clinic produces biomedical waste, from used gloves to needles. The [Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016](https://hspcb.org.in/uploads/laws/BMW_Rules.pdf), as amended, apply to it. The authority a clinic deals with is the state pollution control board, or the pollution control committee in a union territory.

**Authorisation.** Every occupier handling biomedical waste applies for authorisation in Form II, whatever the quantity (rule 10). For a clinic without beds the authorisation is one-time, and it is deemed granted if the board has not objected within 90 days of a complete application.

**Segregation and storage.** Waste is separated into coloured bags or containers at the point where it is produced, as Schedule I sets out (rule 8(2)), and kept in a secure, ventilated place until it is collected (rule 4(b)). The four colours:

| Colour | Examples from Schedule I |
| --- | --- |
| Yellow | Human anatomical waste; soiled waste such as dressings, plaster casts and cotton swabs contaminated with blood or body fluids; expired or discarded medicines; and the other yellow categories in the schedule |
| Red | Contaminated recyclable waste: tubing, catheters, urine bags, syringes without needles, gloves |
| White (translucent) | Sharps: needles, syringes with fixed needles, scalpels, blades |
| Blue | Broken or discarded contaminated glass, including medicine vials and ampoules |

**Other duties of the clinic under rule 4:**

- pre-treat laboratory and microbiological waste, blood samples and blood bags on site by disinfection or sterilisation before they leave (4(c));
- stop using chlorinated plastic bags, apart from blood bags, and chlorinated gloves (4(d));
- never hand treated biomedical waste over with municipal solid waste (4(f));
- train every health care worker who handles the waste when they join and at least once a year after that (4(g));
- immunise those workers against hepatitis B and tetanus (4(h));
- put a bar code on bags or containers sent out of the premises (4(i));
- give those workers a health check when they join and at least once a year, and keep the records (4(m));
- report major accidents to the board, and tell it at once if the treatment facility operator does not collect on time (4(o) and 4(q)).

**Paperwork.** A clinic with fewer than thirty beds names a qualified person to monitor its waste handling and submit the annual report (4(r)). The annual report goes to the board in Form IV on or before 30 June each year (rule 13). Records of how the waste was generated, stored, handed over and disposed of are kept for five years (rule 14). The daily register and monthly website display in rule 4(n) apply to bedded units.

## Patient data and the DPDP Act

On 14 November 2025 the government [announced](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014) that it had notified the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, with an 18-month phased compliance timeline. The same release says the Rules require consent notices that stand on their own and explain, in plain words, the purpose for which personal data is collected.

The [patient data privacy guide](/patient-data-privacy-laws-in-india/) covers which duties touch a clinic and when, and has its own checklist. For a running clinic, four habits are worth building while the Rules phase in. The consent notice at registration says what the data is for. Each member of staff sees only the records their job needs. Reports go to patients through channels the clinic controls, not a personal phone. Someone is named to handle a breach before one happens.

## Teleconsultations

If the doctors see patients by video, phone or chat, the [Telemedicine Practice Guidelines](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf) of 25 March 2020 apply, as Appendix 5 of the 2002 ethics regulations. What they ask of the record:

- The doctor verifies the patient's identity, by name, age, address, email, phone number or a registered ID (3.2.2). Before prescribing, the doctor asks the patient's age, and a minor is seen only with an adult whose identity is confirmed (3.2.3).
- Consent is implied when the patient starts the teleconsultation. When the doctor, a health worker or a caregiver starts it, the patient's explicit consent is needed, and it can be recorded in any form, such as an email, a text, an audio message or a video message (3.4).
- The doctor keeps a log of the teleconsultation, such as the phone log, email record, chat record or video log, and keeps the reports and images used in it. A prescription shared with the patient is kept as it would be for a clinic visit (3.7.2).
- A teleconsultation is treated like a clinic visit for fees, and the patient gets a receipt or invoice (3.7.3).

The [telehealth page](/features/telehealth/) covers the prescribing rules in the guidelines, including which medicines can be prescribed remotely.

## Before you add a service

A running clinic's approvals can change when it adds something new: an X-ray unit, a sample-collection point, a pharmacy counter or a procedure room. Each can bring its own approval.

- An X-ray unit falls under the [Atomic Energy Regulatory Board](https://www.aerb.gov.in/english/), which licenses X-ray facilities through its eLORA e-licensing system.
- Laboratory waste and blood samples have to be pre-treated on site under the biomedical waste rules, so a new collection point changes your waste handling.
- For anything else, such as a pharmacy counter or an ultrasound machine, ask the state authority what approval it needs before you buy the equipment.

Re-read your Clinical Establishments Act registration at the same time, and ask the registering authority whether the new service changes it.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Clinic registration current, with the renewal date in the diary | Before the renewal date, and when you add a service or move | Clinical Establishments Act, where adopted |

| Register of medical certificates kept, with copies | Every certificate | 2002 regulations, 1.3.3 |

| Waste handling training for new and existing staff | At joining, and at least yearly | BMW Rules, rule 4(g) |
