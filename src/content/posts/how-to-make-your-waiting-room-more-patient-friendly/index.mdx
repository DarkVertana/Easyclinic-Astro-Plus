A patient meets the waiting room before the doctor, and on a busy day may sit in it for longer than the consultation lasts. Much of what makes it patient-friendly needs no building work: a clean room with the right chairs, and someone who tells patients what is happening.

This post covers the room itself, whether you are opening a clinic or renovating one. Cutting the wait is a separate job, covered in the post on [reducing patient waiting time](/smarter-scheduling-happier-patients-elevating-healthcare-operations-through-optimized-appointments/).

## Keep it clean, and keep coughs contained

Waiting to see a doctor is stressful for some patients, and an untidy room makes it worse. A full bin or a leaking water dispenser also tells patients something about your hygiene in the rooms they cannot see. Give one person the job of checking the waiting room through each session, not only at opening time, and clear the clutter of old leaflets and broken chairs.

Then plan for the patients who arrive coughing. The US [CDC's core infection control practices](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/core-practices/index.html) ask facilities to prompt patients and visitors with respiratory symptoms to cover their coughs and clean their hands, by providing tissues, masks, hand hygiene supplies and instructional signs at the entrance and through the building. When space permits, they ask for patients with respiratory symptoms to be separated from others as soon as possible, for example on entry. In a small clinic that can mean a few chairs by a window or the door, with a box of masks and a bin with a lid beside them. During periods of higher respiratory virus spread in the community, the same page suggests facilities consider asking everyone to wear a mask on entry.

## Seat every kind of patient

Count your seats against your busiest hour, not an average one. If patients stand on Monday mornings, you need more chairs or a different booking pattern, and your own check-in times will show which.

Then look at who sits in them:

- **Older patients and anyone in pain** need chairs of a normal height with armrests to push up from. Keep a few near the consulting room doors so they do not have to cross the room when called.
- **Wheelchairs and prams** need a clear space beside the chairs and a clear path to the desk and the consulting rooms.
- **Children** do better with a small play corner. Choose toys that can be wiped clean, and wipe them.
- **Patients who want quiet**, to read or to rest, need seats away from the television and the play corner.

Arrange the furniture so patients can choose where to sit, rather than in one long row facing the desk.

## Tell patients how long they will wait

Patients can find it annoying when they do not know how long they have to wait, and each question at the counter takes the receptionist away from the next patient. Tell people roughly how long they will wait when they check in, and tell the room when the doctor is running late and by about how much. A board by the desk with each doctor's current delay does the same job without anyone having to ask.

India's 2002 ethics regulations put the doctor's side of this plainly: regulation 2.1.1 asks a physician to endeavour to add to the comfort of the sick by making visits at the hour indicated to the patient.

In Easy Clinic, waiting patients are told automatically when the doctor's delay changes their wait, so the update does not depend on the receptionist finding a quiet moment. To cut the wait itself, start with the two weeks of measurement in the post on [reducing patient waiting time](/smarter-scheduling-happier-patients-elevating-healthcare-operations-through-optimized-appointments/).

## Give people something to do

Something to do makes the time pass, and patients watch the clock less.

- **A television** with health programmes or the news, at a volume that lets people talk and hear their name called.
- **Your own information screen or leaflets** about the clinic's services and timings. In India, keep anything about the doctor to plain facts: regulation 6.1.1 of the [2002 ethics regulations](https://www.nmc.org.in/page/rules-regulations-rules-regulations-of-erstwhile-mci-code-of-medical-ethics-regulations-2002) forbids publicity that invites attention to a doctor's skill or achievements, and the [clinic marketing strategy post](/clinic-marketing-strategy/) covers what the rules allow.
- **Newspapers and health magazines**, replaced before they are torn.
- **Tablets fixed to the furniture** with videos or reading, if you can keep them charged and clean.
- **A place to charge a phone**, for patients who wait long.

## Water, and something to eat

Keep drinking water where patients can reach it without asking, with cups. A patient who fasted for a blood test will want something to eat once the sample is taken. Depending on your space, a small counter with bottled water and snacks can be self-service or run by someone, and a nearby shop pointed out on a sign does the job in a small clinic.

## What goes on the wall

In India, regulation 1.4.1 of the [2002 ethics regulations](https://www.nmc.org.in/page/rules-regulations-rules-regulations-of-erstwhile-mci-code-of-medical-ethics-regulations-2002), which the National Medical Commission [adopted in August 2023](https://www.nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/KEpwkMzgUwYNR49OrJaqvLcIpeytGPg7XGcorhTg.pdf), says every physician shall display their registration number in the clinic, as well as on prescriptions, certificates and receipts. Regulation 1.8 says a physician should announce fees before rendering service, and a fee list for routine consultations and procedures in the waiting room is one plain way to do that. Your state's rules for registering a clinic may ask for other notices, so check with the authority that registered yours.

Add the practical notices patients look for, such as each doctor's days and hours and how walk-ins are seen.

## Keep private things private at the desk

Where the reception desk sits in the waiting room, whatever is said at the counter is said to the room. Ask for phone numbers and addresses quietly, or on a form. Call patients by name only, never with the reason for their visit or the test they are waiting for. Hand reports over in an envelope, not across the counter for everyone to read.

Forms filled in on a clipboard in the waiting room add to the time and to what others can see. The [digital intake forms post](/simplifying-clinic-workflows-digitally-streamlining-patient-intake-forms/) covers sending the registration form before the visit instead. In Easy Clinic you set up your clinic's own registration form and send it as a link by SMS, or by WhatsApp on Premium, and the answers go on the patient record. The [patient data privacy guide](/patient-data-privacy-laws-in-india/) covers the law on patient records in India.
