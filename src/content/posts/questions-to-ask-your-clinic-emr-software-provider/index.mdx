A demo shows you the screens. The questions below are about what the demo does not show, from what you pay as the clinic grows to what happens to your records if you leave. Ask them in the demo and get the ones that matter in writing. Under each one is what a straight answer sounds like, then Easy Clinic's own answer, including the ones we cannot give in full yet.

## What is included in the price, and what costs extra?

EMR software ranges from a chart and a prescription pad on a screen to a system that also runs the front desk, the bill, the pharmacy and the lab. Ask the vendor to list what the plan you are quoted includes, and which parts are add-ons with a price of their own. A straight answer is a list per plan, in writing.

**Easy Clinic's answer:** every plan includes the EMR and e-prescriptions, Cura AI to draft the note, billing and payments, appointment scheduling, dashboards and reports, SMS and email reminders, logins for your staff and unlimited support and training. Premium adds specialty templates, your own forms and print layouts, WhatsApp reminders, lab reports in the chart and more than one location, among other things. Pharmacy, lab management, insurance claims and integrations come with Enterprise, and a clinic on Professional or Premium can add them. The [pricing page](/pricing/) has the full list.

## How is the price counted?

Some vendors charge per doctor and others per login, and a second branch can carry a price of its own. A quote that looks cheap for one doctor can double once the receptionist needs a login. Ask what you would pay for your clinic as it is today, and again for the day you add a second doctor or a second branch. Ask how often it is billed, and what the discount is for paying ahead.

**Easy Clinic's answer:** Professional and Premium are priced per doctor per month, billed annually or quarterly. Billed annually, Professional is $79 and Premium is $99 per doctor per month, or ₹1,499 and ₹1,999 in India, and quarterly billing costs more per month. A login for your receptionist is included in the price, and there is no setup fee. A second branch needs Premium or Enterprise, and Enterprise, for chains and hospital OPDs, is quoted for your network.

## Is there a contract, and what do we take with us if we leave?

Ask how long the contract runs and what it costs to leave early. Then ask the question that matters most on the way out: if we leave, what do we get back, and in what format? Ask too how long the vendor keeps a copy afterwards. A straight answer names a file format and a number of days. Get it in writing before you sign, because it is hard to negotiate once your records are in the system.

**Easy Clinic's answer:** there is no binding contract and no setup fee, so you use Easy Clinic for as long as it works for your clinic. The Kenya section of our [privacy policy](/privacy/) covers exporting your data when the contract ends. For clinics in other countries, what you get back if you leave, and in what format, is not on our website yet. Ask us for it in writing before you sign, and hold us to the same standard as any other vendor.

## Can we see it working on our own clinic's day before we pay?

A demo run on the vendor's sample patients shows the software at its best. Ask to see your own day instead: your five commonest prescriptions typed in front of you, and a bill with a part-payment raised from a visit. If the vendor offers a free trial, ask who sets it up and whether your staff are trained first, because an untrained trial mostly tests how patient your receptionist is.

**Easy Clinic's answer:** the demo shows Easy Clinic working with your clinic's own workflows, before you commit to anything. Bring the question that matters most to you, such as how a walk-in is registered or how the day's takings are closed. If you would rather not pay for a year up front, quarterly billing lets you pay a quarter at a time.

## Who trains the staff, and how long until we are live?

Ask for the training plan in days and minutes, and ask who it is for. A plan that trains only the doctor leaves the receptionist learning from the doctor between patients. Ask how long it takes from signing to the first patient on the system, and whether the clinic has to close for any of it.

**Easy Clinic's answer:** training is personalised for you and your clinic staff, in four sessions over four days. Day 1 is 40 minutes of basic training, day 2 is 40 minutes of EMR scenario training, day 3 is 20 minutes of practice exercises and day 4 is a 20-minute session the programme calls the road to expertise. That is about two hours in all, at times that suit the clinic, so you keep seeing patients while you learn. A clinic can be live in as little as 3 days after the demo, which is the quickest case, and your patient list is moved across for you.

## How do we reach support once training is over?

Ask how you reach support and whether it costs extra after the first year. Ask who your contact is, by name. A vendor that can only point you to a help page is telling you how the first bad morning will go.

**Easy Clinic's answer:** support is included on every plan with no limit, and you can reach it by phone or in writing. Premium and Enterprise clinics also have a named account manager.

## Can the templates and the prescription be set up our way, and at what cost?

Every specialty writes a different note, and every doctor has a way of writing a prescription. Ask whether the vendor sets up your templates and your prescription layout, and what it charges for the work. Keep the options already in the product apart from a feature built only for your clinic, and ask how that is priced.

**Easy Clinic's answer:** the customisation options already in the product come without charges, and our support team helps you set them up to fit your clinic. Specialty EMR templates and your own clinical forms and print layouts are part of Premium and Enterprise. A feature built only for your clinic is scoped and priced as custom development.

## Which systems does it connect to today?

Lab machines, accounting software, payment providers, insurers and national health systems all come up in demos. Ask which connections are live at clinics today and which are planned, and for each planned one, a date. A connection on the roadmap is not one you can use on your first day.

**Easy Clinic's answer:** integrations come with Enterprise, and a clinic on Professional or Premium can add them as a project scoped for its own setup, such as a link to its lab machines or its accounting system. Ask us about each connection you need by name, and ask for its status and date in writing.

## Who can see our patients' records, and do you sell the data?

Ask what each login can see. A receptionist who books appointments does not need to read clinical notes. Ask where the data is hosted, and whether the vendor sells or shares it in any form, and ask where that answer is written down.

**Easy Clinic's answer:** the reception login included in the price is not allowed into the EMR and cannot issue prescriptions, so the notes stay with the doctor and the clinical staff you give access to. What we may and may not do with your data is set out in our [privacy policy](/privacy/). Read it before you sign, and get our answers on hosting and on selling data in writing with the rest.

## What happens when the internet goes down?

Cloud software needs a connection. Software installed in the clinic needs a server that someone looks after. Ask which one you are buying, and what is kept of a note that was being typed when the line dropped.

**Easy Clinic's answer:** Easy Clinic is cloud software and does not run fully offline today, so the clinic needs a working internet connection. If your line drops often, keep a second connection, such as a mobile data hotspot. Our post on [running a cloud EMR with unreliable power and internet](/cloud-based-emr-for-nigerian-clinics/) sets out the backup a clinic needs.
