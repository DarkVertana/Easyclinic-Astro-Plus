A clinic rarely loses money to a billing mistake on the day it is made. The patient was seen and the work was done. Weeks later the payment arrives short, or does not arrive at all. By then the patient has gone home and nobody at the desk remembers the visit.

The six mistakes below matter most to clinics that bill an insurer, a TPA, an HMO or a company for some of their patients. A clinic that only takes payment at the desk can skip to the duplicate bill, which can happen anywhere. Each section gives the mistake and the check that catches it, with a short note on where that check sits in Easy Clinic.

## Wrong details or the wrong plan at registration

A patient hands over an insurance card, and the receptionist types the name the patient says rather than the name on the card, or picks the right insurer and the wrong plan. The payer cannot match the claim to a member, or pays it at another plan's rates. Either way, the reply comes weeks after the visit.

A patient with two covers, such as an employer scheme and a personal policy, adds one more way to get it wrong: the claim goes to the cover that was not meant to pay for this visit.

The check:

- Ask for the card at every visit, and ask whether the cover has changed since the last one.
- Before the consult, compare the name, date of birth, member number and plan on screen with the card.
- For a patient with two covers, ask which one pays for this visit and note it on the record.
- Correct a mistake while the card is still in the patient's hand.

In Easy Clinic, reception links the patient to their payer and plan category at registration, and the plan's cover and price list come with the link, so the desk can check the cover against the card before the consult. When a patient's cover changes, reception updates the payer or plan and the next bill follows it. Insurance claims come with the Enterprise plan, or as an add-on on Professional and Premium.

## Notes that do not back up the bill

The bill says a wound was dressed and an ECG was done. The note says "wound review" and nothing about the ECG. When the payer asks for the record behind a claim, the note is all the clinic has to show, and a line the note does not support is a line the payer can refuse.

The reverse also happens. The dressing is in the note and on no bill, and the clinic did the work for nothing. That is a leak rather than a rejection, and the [revenue management page](/features/revenue-management/) covers it.

The check:

- Every billed procedure has a line in the note saying what was done and why.
- Once a month, take ten claimed visits and read each note beside its bill.

In Easy Clinic, lab orders and procedures become lines on the patient's invoice as they are processed, so the bill and the chart start from the same orders. Writing down why the procedure was needed is still the doctor's job.

## A service the plan does not cover

The doctor orders a scan the plan excludes, or a branded medicine where the plan pays only for the generic. Nobody finds out until the payer rejects that line. By then the patient has left, and the clinic is chasing money the patient was never told they owed.

The check is to keep each payer's exclusions and limits where the doctor and the desk can both see them. When an order falls outside the cover, tell the patient before the service and ask whether they will pay for it themselves. A patient who agreed at the desk pays at the desk. A patient who hears about it by phone three weeks later is harder to collect from.

In Easy Clinic, each plan category lists what it covers, with its exclusions and limits, and orders are checked against the plan's cover at the time of care. An item the plan will not pay for shows while the patient is still in the clinic, when the doctor can change it or the patient can agree to pay.

## A pre-authorisation nobody raised

Some payers want to approve a test or procedure before it is done, and each payer keeps its own list of what needs approval. The doctor thought the desk had asked. The desk thought the doctor had. The claim goes out without the approval, and the payer does not pay for work it never approved, however well it was done.

The check:

- Keep one sheet per payer listing what needs approval first, at the desk and in the consulting room.
- Record the approval reference against the visit before the procedure is done.

In Easy Clinic, pre-authorisation is a step of its own in the claim, between the order and the invoice, and the approval is recorded against the visit. A claim that needs an approval and has none shows at the billing desk before it goes out. The decision itself stays with the payer.

## The same service billed twice

This mistake catches cash patients too. The desk adds the consultation, then the nurse adds it again along with the dressing. A patient comes back for a report and a second bill is opened for the same visit. A cash patient who is overcharged asks for a refund, if they notice. A payer that finds a duplicate line may query the whole claim.

The check:

- One person closes each bill, even when several people add lines to it.
- Before the desk closes, look for two bills for the same patient on the same day.

In Easy Clinic, the bill opens from the appointment screen with the doctor's orders already on it as lines, so nobody types the orders in a second time. A bill raised in error is voided with authorisation, and the till keeps an audit trail of every transaction, so the owner can see later which bills were voided.

## A claim sent after the deadline

Check each payer contract for the time limit on sending a claim. Where there is one, a claim that misses it can be refused however clean it is. One way to miss it is a claim that sits in a tray until month end, waiting for a missing signature.

The check:

- Write each payer's time limit on one sheet and keep it beside the claims.
- Send claims on a fixed day every week, oldest first, rather than in one batch at month end.

In Easy Clinic, the claims dashboard shows the status of every claim, and the billing desk submits claims one at a time or in a batch. The ageing report sorts the unpaid claims by how long each has waited, so a slow payer shows up before the month closes.

## A monthly check on last month's bills

You can run this check whether or not you use software. All it needs is last month's bills and claims, with the notes behind them.

1. List every claim a payer sent back last month with the reason the payer gave, and sort the list by reason. Two or more with the same reason may trace back to one of the six mistakes above.
2. Take ten claimed visits and read each note beside its bill.
3. Look for two bills for the same patient on the same day.
4. List the claims not yet sent, oldest first, against each payer's time limit.

We give no figure for what this check usually finds, because Easy Clinic has not published a measured rejection or leakage rate across the clinics that use it. The first month's results become your own starting figure.

## What software does not fix

Software can put the plan on the screen and show a claim that has no approval before it leaves. It cannot make the receptionist ask for the card, and it does not decide what a payer covers. The number to watch is your own: the claims each payer sent back, month by month.
