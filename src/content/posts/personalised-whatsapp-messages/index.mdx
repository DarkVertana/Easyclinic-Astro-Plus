A follow-up can slip without anyone deciding against it. The symptoms ease, and nobody reminds the patient. A short message at the right moment gives them a reason to return, as long as it reads as if the clinic remembered them and not as if a system sent it.

## What makes a WhatsApp message personal

- **It says who is writing.** The patient's name and the doctor's name are in the first line, so the patient knows who it is from before they open it.
- **It is about this patient.** It refers to the review the doctor asked for, or the medicines prescribed at the last visit, and not to "your health" in general.
- **It asks for one thing.** "Reply with a day that suits you" is easy to do on the bus. A message that asks the patient to open a link and also call the desk asks for too much.
- **It arrives at a moment that makes sense to the patient.** The day before the medicines run out, or two days after a procedure, and not whenever the desk has a free minute.

A generic message with a name pasted in is not personal. "Dear Mrs Sharma, your follow-up is due" still reads like a notice.

## Before you send: consent and the two kinds of message

WhatsApp's [Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) lets a business message a person only after the person has given it their number and agreed to receive its messages. For a clinic, the natural moment to ask is registration. Ask about two kinds of message separately: messages about the patient's visits, such as reminders and reports, and greetings or news from the clinic. Meta's own advice is to get a separate opt-in for each category, because people block businesses that send them messages they did not expect.

The split matters because Meta sorts every template into a category. A utility message follows up on something the patient already has with the clinic and contains nothing promotional. Meta's [template guidelines](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/template-categorization) give a birthday wish and a Diwali invitation as examples of marketing messages, and a visit reminder with an offer added to it counts as marketing too. Meta reviews each template and decides its category from the exact wording, and the charge for a delivered template message depends on its category. How templates get approved, and what Meta charges, is on the [WhatsApp page](/features/whatsapp/).

When a patient asks you to stop, stop. The policy requires a business to honour every request to opt out, whether it comes on WhatsApp or anywhere else, across the desk included. Your country's data protection law applies as well, and the [patient engagement page](/features/patient-engagement/#consent-by-country) sets out what the laws of India, Kenya, the UAE and Nigeria ask, in general terms.

## Five moments worth a message

| Moment | When to send | Who can receive it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Birthday | On the day | Patients who agreed to greetings from the clinic |
| Festival | A few days before | Patients who agreed to greetings from the clinic |
| Refill review | A few days before the medicines run out | Patients who agreed to messages about their visits |
| Check-in after a visit | A day or two after a procedure or a new treatment | Patients who agreed to messages about their visits |
| Overdue follow-up | A few weeks after the follow-up was due | Patients who agreed to messages about their visits |

The samples below were written for this post, with made-up patients and doctors. Change the wording to your clinic's own voice.

### A birthday wish

> Happy birthday, Mrs Sharma. Everyone at Dr Rao's clinic wishes you a healthy year ahead. If your yearly check-up is due, reply with a day that suits you and we will find a time.

This is a marketing message by Meta's own example, so it goes only to patients who said yes to greetings. If you would rather a greeting asked for nothing, drop the last sentence.

### A festival message

> Wishing you and your family a happy Diwali from Dr Rao's clinic. We are closed on Friday and Saturday, and open as usual from Monday.

A greeting that also gives the clinic's festival hours is more useful than a greeting alone. It is still a greeting, so the same opt-in applies. For a clinic in Nairobi the festival might be Christmas or Eid, and the same rule holds.

### A refill review

> Mr Otieno, the medicines Dr Mwangi prescribed on 3 March will run out soon. The doctor would like to see you before the next prescription. Reply with a day that suits you.

Name the visit, not the condition. Keep the message about the doctor's review and not about buying the medicines, because WhatsApp's policy does not allow a business to sell or promote prescription drugs on WhatsApp.

### A check-in after the visit

> Dr Mwangi's clinic here, checking in after your visit on Tuesday. How are you feeling? If anything about your treatment is unclear, reply here and our nurse will call you.

When the patient replies, the clinic can answer in its own words for the next 24 hours without a template. Only invite replies if someone will read them that day. If replies are answered automatically, WhatsApp's policy asks for a clear way to reach a person, such as a phone number or a handover to staff.

### An overdue follow-up

> Dr Rao's clinic here. Our records show your follow-up was due last month. If you are feeling better, we are glad to hear it. If not, reply with a day that suits you and we will book you in.

No scolding and no alarm. The patient decides, and a reply is the only thing you ask for.

## What to leave out of a WhatsApp message

- **The diagnosis and the test values.** Someone else may read the patient's phone, and WhatsApp's policy says not to send health information on WhatsApp where the law requires a system held to a higher standard for it.
- **Offers on medicines.** They are not allowed on WhatsApp, as above.
- **Requests for card or ID numbers.** The policy says not to ask people to share full card numbers or ID card numbers, and a clinic has no need for them in a chat.
- **Anything about another patient.** The policy does not allow a business to pass information from one customer's chat to another customer.

## How often to message a patient

Only when there is a reason the patient would recognise. Each of the five moments above is about the patient who receives it. A weekly health tip nobody asked for is the kind of unexpected message that WhatsApp's policy says leads people to block a business.

Count what each kind of message brings back, replies and booked visits, for a month. Keep the messages patients answer and drop the ones nobody does. Easy Clinic has not published a measured change in repeat visits from messages like these, so this post gives no figure.
