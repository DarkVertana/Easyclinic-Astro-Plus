On Google Maps and Search, a clinic's reviews [show next to its Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en), so a patient looking for a skin clinic nearby can read them before opening any website. What those reviews say may be the first thing a new patient learns about the clinic, and nobody at the clinic wrote it.

A US survey gives a sense of how much people rely on them. In [BrightLocal's 2026 survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) of 1,002 adults in the US, 68% said they would only use a local business rated four stars or more, up from 55% a year earlier, and 74% said they only care about reviews written in the last three months. It asked about local businesses in general, not clinics and not India, so read it as a direction rather than a figure for your city.

## What reviews do on Google

Reviews also count toward where the clinic appears in local results. One of the factors [Google names for local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) is prominence, how well known a business is, and Google says it is based partly on how many reviews a business has, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help. The same page says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Reviews are not the only thing on that list. Google says profiles with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up in local results, so check the address, the hours for each day, the category and the phone number before you ask anyone for a review.

## Ask every patient, the same way

1. **Get the link and the QR code.** In your Business Profile, open Read reviews, then Get more reviews, and copy the link or save the QR code. [Google says](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en) the QR code can only be made from a computer browser, not a phone.
2. **Put them where the visit ends.** Google suggests putting the link on receipts and displaying the printed QR code, and says review requests can also go by email or WhatsApp. At a clinic, that means the bill at the counter and the message after the visit.
3. **Ask everyone.** Use one sentence, said by the same person at the counter to every patient, whether or not the visit went well. For example: "If you have a minute later, a Google review helps other patients find us. The QR code is at the counter."
4. **Ask, then let go.** [Google's policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) says a business should not require or pressure patients to write a review on the premises, or ask for particular content. The patient reviews later, on their own phone, in their own words.
5. **Expect some patients to need help.** [Google says](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) a reviewer must be signed in to a Google Account, which can be set up with an email address that is not Gmail.

## What Google does not allow

[Google's content policy for Maps](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) is specific about what a business may not do when it asks for reviews:

- **No incentives.** No payment, discount, free test or gift in exchange for posting a review, or for changing or removing a negative one. Google removes reviews posted for an incentive.
- **No filtering.** A business may not discourage negative reviews or selectively ask for positive ones. If the clinic runs its own feedback survey, do not use it as a filter by sending the Google link only to patients who scored the visit highly. That is asking selectively for positive reviews.
- **No targets or scripts.** No asking staff to collect a set number of reviews, and no asking patients to name a staff member or mention anything in particular.
- **No reviews from inside.** Google removes reviews based on a conflict of interest, which includes current and former staff and family members.

## Replying to reviews

Only a verified business can reply. [Google says](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en) a reply is public and appears under the review as the business, not under your name. The reviewer is told about it and can still change their review. Google checks each reply against its policies first, which usually takes up to 10 minutes and can take up to 30 days.

For a clinic, the hard rule is confidentiality. Regulation 7.14 of the [2002 ethics regulations](https://www.nmc.org.in/page/rules-regulations-rules-regulations-of-erstwhile-mci-code-of-medical-ethics-regulations-2002) forbids a doctor to disclose a patient's secrets learnt in practice, except in the situations it lists, such as a court order. So no reply confirms that the reviewer was a patient or mentions a diagnosis.

Any review can be reported, but [Google says](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en) it removes only those that break its policies, such as spam or profanity, and that it does not get involved in disagreements between a business and its customers. [Responding to negative patient reviews](/maintaining-trust-how-to-handle-negative-reviews-in-healthcare-professionally/) has sample replies and more on when to report one.

## Fix what the reviews keep saying

Once a month, read the reviews from the last three months and sort the complaints by kind: the wait, the bill, the front desk, the follow-up. Fix the one that comes up most, then watch whether it stops appearing.

- **The wait.** Tell waiting patients about a delay, so nobody has to queue at the counter to ask. In EasyClinic, when the doctor runs late, waiting patients are told of the change to their wait automatically.
- **The bill.** A bill the patient does not understand can read as overcharging. Put each test and procedure on its own line, and explain the total before the patient pays. In EasyClinic, the tests and procedures the doctor ordered arrive on the invoice as lines.
- **The front desk.** Give the desk the same few sentences for the moments that go wrong, such as a delay or a missing report, so the answer does not depend on who is on shift.
- **The follow-up.** A patient who leaves unsure what happens next may say so in a review. Book the next visit at the counter, and send the date in a message.
