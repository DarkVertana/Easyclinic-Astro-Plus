## 3\. The Everyday Problems Faced by Allergy Clinics in India

Every speciality clinic faces its share of operational challenges, but allergy clinics experience a unique set of issues due to the nature of their services. Based on extensive industry insights, Easy Clinic has observed the following recurring pain points faced by Indian allergy specialists.

### Overbooked appointments and confusing scheduling

Most allergy clinics experience peak hour clusters. Walk-in patients often arrive unexpectedly, causing delays. Manual scheduling increases the chances of double booking and long waiting room congestion.

### Paper-based patient records leading to lost or incomplete data

Allergy care is detail-oriented. Notes on triggers, reactions, seasonal patterns, and treatment responses are critical. Paper files make it difficult to consolidate, analyse, or retrieve information quickly.

### Administrative overload is reducing doctors’ focus.

Doctors often spend hours recording notes, reviewing immunotherapy cycles, signing off reports, and coordinating follow-up reminders. This decreases the actual time spent on consultations.

### Immunotherapy and inventory tracking challenges

Immunotherapy requires strict dosing schedules and vial tracking. Manual recording increases the risk of misplaced vials, missed dose cycles, or incorrect records.

### Compliance pressure from health authorities

With India’s shift toward digital records and structured documentation under health digitisation guidelines, clinics must maintain accurate EMRs, secure patient data, and ensure adherence to reporting standards.

To illustrate the impact, imagine a busy allergy clinic in Pune. The doctor conducts 30 prick tests daily but still uses handwritten notes. Staff must track which vials are used, which patients missed their dose, and which batch expires soon. At the same time, the clinic owner wants to review billing patterns and patient return rates, but lacks the data. These inefficiencies accumulate and eventually restrict growth.
