An endocrinology practice runs on follow-up. Many patients are not seen once and discharged. They come back every few months with a new report and a dose to adjust, and the value of each visit depends on what the doctor can see from the visits before it. A general clinic system that stores the last prescription and the bill is not enough for that. This post sets out the four jobs to test before you choose software for an endocrine practice.

## Lab trends in front of the doctor during the consult

For a patient on levothyroxine, the doctor wants to know more than today's TSH: whether it is moving, and how it moved after the last dose change. The same goes for HbA1c in diabetes and for weight in a weight-management programme.

On paper, the doctor lays out reports from several visits and reads the numbers across, in the middle of a consult. The software should do that work: keep each lab value on the patient's record visit after visit, and show it as a trend on the screen the doctor already has open.

In Easy Clinic, lab values stay on the record and are drawn as lab charts beside the vitals, so the TSH or HbA1c trend is one look instead of a pile of reports. Lab reports in the chart come with the Premium plan, and abnormal values are flagged when the report comes in.

Test how outside reports get in before you buy. If most of your patients test at a lab you do not run, the trend is only as good as the path those results take into the record. In Easy Clinic, a report from an outside lab is uploaded to the patient's record as a file. A direct feed from that lab's own system would be integration work, scoped as a project for your clinic.

## Protocol tests, ordered in one step

Every endocrinology clinic has its own list of tests that fall due on a schedule. If your protocol says that a patient with long-standing diabetes has a urine albumin test once a year, the test gets missed when nobody remembers to order it on a busy day.

Two things help. First, group each protocol's tests so the doctor orders them in one step rather than writing each one out. In Easy Clinic, the lab's test catalog can hold health check-up packages that you put together, so an annual diabetes panel or a thyroid profile can be a single item on the chart. The test catalog is part of lab management, which comes with Enterprise or as an add-on on Professional and Premium.

Second, put the protocol where the doctor looks. Premium includes your own clinical forms, so a diabetes review form can carry a line for the date of the last urine albumin test, and a PCOS follow-up form can carry the cycle history your doctors ask about at every visit. A form with the right fields is more likely to be filled in than a free-text note that relies on memory.

## Follow-up at the interval each condition needs

A patient whose thyroid dose changed today may need a recheck sooner than a patient who has been stable on the same dose for years. The interval is the doctor's decision. The software's job is to make sure the decision turns into a visit.

The simplest way is to book the next review before the patient leaves. Once it is on the calendar, Easy Clinic sends the reminder on its own: by SMS and email on every plan, and on WhatsApp as well on Premium. A patient who leaves without a booking is easy to lose, so ask the receptionist to book the review while the bill is being raised.

For patients who have already drifted, the clinic needs a list of everyone with a given condition who has not been seen for too long. That list is only as good as the diagnosis recorded in each chart, so agree how your doctors record each diagnosis, such as hypothyroidism or type 2 diabetes, and use the same terms every time. How Easy Clinic reaches patients between visits, including recalls, is on the [patient engagement page](/features/patient-engagement/).

## Thyroid and PCOS programmes billed as packages

Some endocrine clinics sell care as a programme rather than a visit: a set number of consults and tests over three or six months. Billed visit by visit, a programme is easy to undercharge, because an extra counselling session or a repeat test slips through without a bill.

On Premium and Enterprise, Easy Clinic sets up a course of care as a package of several items at a package price. The package can be billed in one visit or across several, with the payment spread over the visits, so a patient paying monthly for a PCOS programme does not need a new invoice written by hand each time. How the bill itself works is on the [billing page](/features/billing/).

## Between visits: reports and teleconsults

A patient on long-term care may travel a long way for a result and a short conversation. Two things can save that trip. On Premium, a lab report can go to the patient on WhatsApp, and a follow-up can run as a video consult. The doctor and the patient join from a web browser, and the prescription from the teleconsult can be sent to the patient as soon as the call ends. Whether a visit can safely be remote is the doctor's call, patient by patient.

## Where Cura AI fits, and where it does not

Cura AI drafts the consult note and the prescription in the chart for the doctor to confirm, and the note drafting comes with every plan. It saves typing. The dose and the review interval stay with the doctor, and a draft the doctor has not read is not a finished note.

## Questions to put to any vendor

Take these to every demo, including ours, and ask to see each one on screen with a patient like yours:

- Show me one patient's TSH over the last two years on one screen.
- Show me how a report from an outside lab reaches the record, and whether it joins the trend.
- Build me a PCOS follow-up form with our fields. How long does it take, and who does it?
- Set up a six-month programme as a package, and bill the second month's instalment.
- Book a three-month review at the desk, and show me the reminder the patient receives.
- Show me what the receptionist's login can and cannot open.

In Easy Clinic, the reception login included in the price books appointments and raises bills, and it cannot open the EMR or issue prescriptions, so a patient's endocrine history stays with the clinical staff.
