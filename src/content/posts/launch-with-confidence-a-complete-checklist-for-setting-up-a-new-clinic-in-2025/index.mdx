This checklist follows the order the work comes in when you open a clinic. It is not tied to one country, so where a step depends on local rules it tells you what to ask and links to our guide for your country. Tick each step off before you start paying for the next one.

## 1. Plan the clinic and the budget

Before you look at a single property, write down four things:

- The services you will offer on the first day, and the ones that can wait.
- Who the patients are, and where they go for care now.
- Who else serves them nearby, and what those clinics do not offer.
- What you will charge, and how patients will pay.

Then build the budget in two columns: what you pay once before opening, and what you pay every month after it. Keep at least three months of running costs in the bank on opening day. For worked examples, Easy Clinic's line-by-line budgets come to about [₹17.55 lakh for a small clinic in Mumbai](/how-much-does-it-cost-to-open-a-clinic-in-mumbai/) and about [KES 3.2 million in Nairobi](/how-much-does-it-cost-to-open-a-clinic-in-nairobi/) if you buy a plot and build, and the [clinic setup cost in India](/clinic-setup-cost-in-india/) post explains where the money goes.

If you need to borrow, find out now what lenders will ask for. The lenders in our post on [funding a clinic in Kenya](/healthcare-startup-in-kenya/), for example, ask for a record of takings or audited accounts that a new clinic does not have yet.

## 2. Choose the premises

The location decides many of your patients, and it is hard to change once the lease is signed. Check each site for:

- **Access.** Can patients reach it by public transport or road, and can an elderly patient manage the entrance?
- **Parking.** Is there somewhere for patients who drive to stop?
- **Visibility.** Will a sign be seen from the street?
- **Neighbours.** Is there a pharmacy or a diagnostic centre nearby, and is another clinic already on the same street?

Visit each site at the hours your patients would come, on more than one day. Read the lease for the length, the notice period, the yearly rent increase and who pays for repairs. Ask the local authority whether the unit can be used as a clinic before you sign.

## 3. Apply for the licences

Which licences a clinic needs depends on the country, and often on the state or county. Ask the health regulator before you commit money, and ask about each of these:

- the registration of the clinic itself as a health facility
- each clinician's practising licence or council registration
- storing and dispensing medicines
- clinical waste, and who will collect it
- fire safety and the signboard
- tax registration for the business

Our guides list the licences in order, with the fees, for [India](/clinic-in-india/), [Kenya](/the-ultimate-guide-to-starting-a-clinic-in-kenya/), [Nigeria](/clinic-in-nigeria/), [Uganda](/clinic-in-uganda/) and [Ethiopia](/clinic-in-ethiopia/). Keep a copy of every licence in one file with its renewal date, and put the dates in the diary.

## 4. Lay out the rooms and buy the equipment

Plan the rooms around the route a patient takes: in at reception, into the waiting area, through the consultation, and out past the bill or the pharmacy. Give the consultation and examination rooms real privacy. A patient in a wheelchair should be able to reach every room they need.

Buy equipment for the services you will offer on the first day:

- clinical equipment for your specialty, such as an examination couch and a BP monitor
- sterilisation, if you reuse instruments
- a computer and a printer at the desk, with a card or mobile payment device
- power backup for the desk and the fridge that holds vaccines or medicines

Leave the expensive diagnostics until patient numbers justify them.

## 5. Hire and train the team

Ask the regulator what minimum staff your type of clinic needs, then hire for the services you will offer on the first day. Check every clinician's licence before you make an offer.

Write short procedures for the desk, for hygiene, for emergencies and for keeping records, and train everyone on them before the first patient. Train the desk on the records system too. A receptionist who learns the software on opening day makes the queue longer.

## 6. Set up the front desk and the till

The desk decides whether patients wait and whether the bill is right. Before opening day, set up:

- **Appointments.** Slots for each doctor with room for walk-ins, and reminders by SMS or WhatsApp the day before.
- **Billing.** A printed bill for every visit, and the payment methods patients use in your area, including insurance if you accept it.
- **Stock.** Medicines and disposables with their expiry dates, and a reorder level for each.
- **Records.** Paper files or software. If it is software, choose it before the first patient.

Put these questions to any software vendor before you sign:

1. Does the patient record fit my specialty, or do I have to work around it?
2. Can billing and the pharmacy run in the same system as the record?
3. What does a second doctor, or a second branch, add to the bill?
4. What happens at the desk when the internet goes down?
5. How do I get my data out if I leave, and in what format?
6. Is there a contract, and what does it cost to stop?

## 7. Tell patients you are opening

- Put up the sign and give the clinic a name patients can say and spell.
- Create a Google Business Profile with the address, the opening hours, the phone number and a photo of the entrance.
- Build a simple website that loads on a phone, with the services and how to book.
- Tell nearby pharmacies and labs that you are open, and what you treat.
- Consider an opening day with free health checks to bring the first patients in.

## 8. Rehearse before opening day

Run one full day with the staff playing patients. Book them, see them, bill them and dispense to them. Every problem you find on that day is one a real patient does not meet.

Check the safety basics at the same time: the sterilisation routine, the waste bins and the collection arrangement, clear fire exits with working extinguishers, and an emergency kit that everyone can find. Then put the paperwork in one place, ready for an inspection: the licences, each clinician's registration, equipment service records, and the procedures with the training record.

## 9. Review the numbers every month

Pick a few numbers and look at them on the same day each month:

- patients seen each day
- missed appointments
- how long patients wait
- what was billed against what was collected
- stock that expired or ran out
- patients who came back

Meet the team once a month to go through complaints and change the procedures that are not working. Add a service or a second location when the numbers say you can, and not before.
