**Digital prescriptions** are picked from lists, saved to the patient’s record, and printed or sent to the patient. A paper prescription is handwritten and leaves with the patient. This guide explains what really changes for doctors, patients and pharmacy staff, what a good digital prescription should contain, and how to switch over four weeks without slowing your OPD.

## Key points

The biggest change is not the paper, it is the record that stays with the clinic. Expect the first week to feel slower while you build saved sets. Fix your prescription format first, start with your top 10 conditions, and send the PDF on WhatsApp from day one.

## Paper and digital prescriptions side by side

Both formats give the patient a prescription. Only one keeps a record the clinic can use again at the next visit.

| **Area** | **Paper prescription** | **Digital prescription** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Writing it | Handwritten every time | Picked from lists, saved sets and shortcuts |
| Reading it | Depends on handwriting | Printed clearly |
| Patient copy | One copy, easy to lose | Printed copy plus a PDF on WhatsApp |
| Next visit | Doctor asks what was given | Last visit shows on screen |
| Pharmacy | Reads the paper the patient brings | Sees the prescription in its own queue |
| Patient language | Usually English | Medicine instructions can print in the patient’s language |
| Clinic records | Stored in files, if at all | Saved on the patient’s record |
| Reports | Counted by hand | Top diagnoses and medicines in a few clicks |

## What a good digital prescription contains

Before you choose a system, agree what your prescription must show. If you are new to clinic software terms, our guide to the [difference between EMR and EHR](/difference-between-emr-and-ehr/) is a good starting point. Use this list to check any sample a vendor gives you.

| **Section** | **What it should show** |
| --- | --- |
| Clinic header | Clinic logo, name, address and contact number |
| Doctor details | Doctor name, qualification, registration number and digital signature |
| Patient details | Name, age, sex, patient ID and visit date |
| Complaints and findings | Chief complaints, duration and key examination findings, if you print them |
| Diagnosis | The working or confirmed diagnosis |
| Medicines | Name, strength, dose, frequency, timing with food and duration |
| Tests | Investigations advised |
| Advice | Diet, care instructions and warning signs, in plain words |
| Follow-up | The date or number of days for the next visit |

Some details are for the doctor only. Internal notes and severity markers should stay on screen and never print. Check this with the vendor. A [smart EMR](/smart-emr-for-clinics/) keeps these on screen only.

## What changes for the doctor

The first week feels slower. After that, most of the work is clicking, not writing. These are the tools that make the difference.

- **Saved medicine sets.** For a common condition, one click adds the usual medicines with dose and duration. You still edit each dose for the patient in front of you.
- **Text shortcuts.** Save a phrase like “complaints of fever and chills” once. Next time, type a short code and press space.
- **Saved test bundles.** Group the tests you order together for a condition. One click orders them all.
- **Saved advice.** Diet and care instructions are added in a click, so patients leave with them in writing.
- **The last visit on screen.** No need to ask the patient what they took last time.

### A worked example: a sore throat visit

A patient comes in with fever, cough and a red, swollen throat. The doctor picks the complaints from favourites and types a 3-letter shortcut for “chills with fever”. They confirm upper respiratory tract infection as the diagnosis and order one blood test from a bundle.

Next, they add a saved medicine set for this condition. The set has the usual antibiotic for 5 days. The doctor changes it to 7 days for this patient. They add saved advice, such as warm gargles and steam, and set a follow-up after 5 days. The prescription prints and goes to the patient’s phone.

Nothing in this visit needed long typing. The doctor still made every clinical decision.

## What changes for the patient

- **A clear, printed prescription.** No guessing at handwriting at home or at the pharmacy.
- **A copy on their phone.** The PDF on WhatsApp means it is not lost before the next visit.
- **Instructions in their language.** Medicine instructions can print in a regional language, such as Hindi. Read more on [multilingual](/multilingual-digital-prescriptions/) [**digital prescriptions**](/multilingual-digital-prescriptions/).
- **Advice in writing.** Diet, care and follow-up are written down, so fewer patients return to the desk with questions. This is a simple first step in [patient engagement](/patient-engagement-at-easyclinic/).

## What changes for the pharmacy and front desk

The pharmacist reads a printed list, not handwriting, which removes one common cause of [prescription errors](/ai-prescription-errors/). In a clinic with an in-house pharmacy, the prescription can reach the pharmacy before the patient does. The pharmacist can start preparing the medicines while the patient walks over.

The front desk can find any old prescription from the patient’s record. When a patient calls to ask what they were given, the answer is on screen in seconds.

## Common worries about digital prescriptions

| **Worry** | **What usually happens** |
| --- | --- |
| It will slow me down | The first week is slower. Once saved sets and shortcuts are built, most prescriptions need only a few clicks. |
| It will not look like my pad | A custom format can match your layout, logo and sections, and print on pre-printed paper. |
| My patients prefer paper | You still print and hand it over. The WhatsApp copy is an extra. |
| My medicines are not in the list | Good systems let you add a missing brand during the visit and save it for next time. |
| Every patient needs a different dose | Saved sets are a starting point. Every dose stays editable. |

## A 4-week plan to switch without slowing your OPD

| **Week** | **Focus** | **What to do** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Set up | Agree your prescription format. Build saved medicine sets for your top 10 conditions. Save your 20 most common phrases as shortcuts. |
| Week 2 | New patients only | Use **digital prescriptions** for new patients. Keep paper for follow-ups. Note what you had to type and turn it into a shortcut. |
| Week 3 | All patients | Move all patients to digital. Start sending the PDF on WhatsApp to everyone who agrees. |
| Week 4 | Review | Check which saved sets you edit most and update them. Add test bundles and saved advice for your common conditions. |

Keep a pad in the drawer for the first month, until the whole team is comfortable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a digital prescription slower to write than a paper one?

**Digital prescriptions** are slower only at the start. Once your common medicine sets and shortcuts are saved, most prescriptions are a few clicks. The doctor then edits doses for the patient. Expect the first week to feel slower while you build your saved sets.

### Can a digital prescription look like my current pad?

Yes, in most systems. In Easy Clinic, the team builds the format from your sample. It can print on plain paper with your logo, or on your pre-printed pad with the margins set to fit.

### Do patients still get a printed copy?

Yes. You can print the prescription and hand it over as usual. With Easy Clinic, you can also send it to the patient’s WhatsApp as a PDF, so they keep a copy on their phone.

### Can I write weight-based or tapering doses digitally?

Yes. Saved medicine sets are only a starting point. In Easy Clinic every dose stays editable for each patient, and a medicine can have several dose steps, such as twice a day for 7 days and then once a day.

### What should I digitise first?

Start with your top 10 conditions by volume. Build one saved medicine set and one advice set for each. Then save the phrases you write most often as shortcuts. This covers most of an average OPD day.

### Can the prescription print in Hindi or another language?

Medicine instructions can print in regional languages such as Hindi in Easy Clinic. This helps patients follow dose and timing at home. Ask in your demo which languages are available for your region.
