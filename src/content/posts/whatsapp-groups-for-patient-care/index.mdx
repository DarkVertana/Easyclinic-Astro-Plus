Patients with diabetes and pregnant patients have questions between visits, and many of them are the same questions. Do I come fasting for tomorrow's test? Is this swelling normal at this stage? A clinic group lets the doctor answer the common ones once, for everyone, and keeps the one-to-one conversations for the patients who need them.

This post covers what such a group is for and how to set one up so that it stays useful.

## What a clinic group is for

A group works for the things that are true for everyone in it. For a diabetes group that is food, and the routine around testing and medicines. For a pregnancy group it is what to expect at each stage and how to prepare for each scan or test.

- Education on a schedule, written or approved by the doctor, one topic at a time.
- Notices that apply to everyone, such as a holiday closure or a screening day.
- Answers to the questions the desk hears most often, posted once for the whole group.
- A place where a newly diagnosed patient can see that other people ask the same things.

A group is not for diagnosis or for emergencies, and it does not replace the next consultation. Once a group has become a place where patients post their reports and wait for the doctor to reply, it is doing a consult's job badly and in front of everyone.

## What stays out of the group

Every member of a group sees every message in it. A patient who posts a sugar reading or a scan report has shared it with everyone in the group, and a doctor who replies in the group has discussed that patient's care in front of them all.

WhatsApp's own guide for [health groups](https://www.whatsapp.com/communities/learning/health), written for community health programmes, says a group's rules should discourage members from sharing their own or anyone else's personal information, and case-related information. Where members share sensitive experiences in a support group, it suggests they use disappearing messages. WhatsApp's [business messaging policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy) adds that a business should not send or ask for health information on WhatsApp where the law requires a system held to a higher standard for it.

When a patient posts something personal anyway, an admin moves the conversation to a private message or a booked visit and removes the post from the group. WhatsApp's guide for community admins says they can [delete members' messages for up to 2.5 days](https://www.whatsapp.com/communities/learning/buildingasafecommunity) after they are sent, so the admins need to look at the group every clinic day.

## Settings to switch on before the first member joins

These settings come from WhatsApp's guides for [health groups](https://www.whatsapp.com/communities/learning/health) and for [keeping a community safe](https://www.whatsapp.com/communities/learning/buildingasafecommunity).

- **At least two admins.** WhatsApp's health guide suggests at least two admins for every group. One should be clinical, so every health message is written or approved by someone who can stand behind it.
- **Only admins can post, if you want it one-way.** The "Send messages" setting can be changed to "Only admins". A clinic that wants questions can open the group for one set hour a week and close it again.
- **Join requests on, and invite links sent privately.** Turn on group join requests so an admin approves each new member. Send the invite link to each patient in a private message, and never post it on a website or a poster.
- **Hours in the description.** WhatsApp's [guide to setting boundaries](https://www.whatsapp.com/communities/learning/establishingboundaries) suggests stating your online hours. Put the clinic's hours in the group description, next to the rules.

A clinic with several groups can link them into a WhatsApp Community, with one announcement group that reaches everyone, for example one group for each trimester. WhatsApp's guide warns that @mentioning a member in the announcement group exposes that member's phone number to the entire community, so admins should not do it. The same guide rules out using a Community for bulk or automated messaging, so reminders and results still go to each patient on their own.

## Rules to post on the first day

WhatsApp's guide suggests putting the rules in the description and posting them again regularly. A starting set, to be changed by your doctors:

1. This group is for health education from (clinic name). The doctors do not answer individual questions here.
2. Please do not post your own reports, readings or medicines. Send them to the clinic on (number) or bring them to your next visit.
3. If you feel unwell or think something is wrong, call the clinic or go to the nearest emergency department. Do not wait for a reply in this group.
4. What others share in the group stays in the group.
5. The group is read on clinic days, between (hours).
6. You can leave at any time, and we will not add you back.

## A weekly plan for two groups

Start with two posts a week. The topics below are examples. Each post should be written or approved by the clinician who runs the group, in their own words.

| When | Diabetes group | Pregnancy group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early in the week | One food idea that fits the plan the doctor gives, such as a snack | The week's topic for each stage, written by the obstetrician |
| Later in the week | A reminder to log readings before the next review | Which symptoms are expected at this stage and which mean calling the clinic, in the doctor's words |
| Before a festival or a holiday | Meals and medicine timing on the day, as the doctor advises | Travel and rest over the holiday, as the doctor advises |
| Once a month | Answers to the questions the desk heard most that month | Answers to the questions the desk heard most that month |

A pregnancy group is easier to run split by stage, so a patient in her first trimester is not reading about labour. Each stage can be its own group inside one Community.

## Consent before you add a patient

Being in a pregnancy group or a diabetes group says something about a patient to everyone else in it. So ask first and add only the patients who said yes, with their answer written down beside their details. If a patient leaves the group, WhatsApp's guide says to [honour that decision](https://www.whatsapp.com/communities/learning/buildingasafecommunity), so do not add them back.

WhatsApp's [business messaging policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy) lets a business message a person only after the person has given it their number and agreed to hear from it. In India, [section 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf) asks for consent that is free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative action for a specified purpose, and lets the person withdraw it as easily as they gave it. The DPDP Rules notified in November 2025 [phase the Act in over 18 months](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014). Our guide to [patient data privacy laws in India](/patient-data-privacy-laws-in-india/) covers the rest of what a clinic owes its patients there.

## Where EasyClinic fits

We do not claim that EasyClinic creates or runs WhatsApp groups, because none of our product pages describes a group feature. What it does cover is the one-to-one side that the group should not. On Premium, it sends each patient's reminders, prescriptions, lab reports and receipts to that patient on WhatsApp from the clinic software, and every plan sends reminders by SMS and email. A recall campaign picks the patients whose chart records a condition such as diabetes and asks each of them to book a review.

For an obstetric practice, the pregnancy record tracks gestational age and trimester progress, so whoever runs a trimester group can check a patient's stage on her record before adding her. The antenatal visits and the follow-ups between them stay on that record, not in the group.
