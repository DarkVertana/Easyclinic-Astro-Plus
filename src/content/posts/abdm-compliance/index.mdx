ABDM, the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, is run by the National Health Authority (NHA). It gives each patient a health ID and lets records move between providers with the patient's consent. This post looks at it from a private clinic's side, starting with whether a clinic has to join at all.

An earlier version of this post said ABDM was no longer optional for clinics. ABDM's own answer is that it is voluntary, and the sections below link to the sources.

## What ABDM is, from a clinic's side

ABDM has a few parts a clinic deals with directly. The definitions below are from [ABDM's FAQ](https://abdm.gov.in/FAQ).

- **ABHA number.** A randomly generated 14-digit number that identifies a patient and links their health records across providers, with their consent.
- **ABHA address.** A name the patient chooses, in the style of an email address (the FAQ's example is ajay@abdm), that is easier to remember than the number.
- **Health Facility Registry (HFR).** The registry of health facilities, public and private, including clinics, labs, pharmacies and hospitals, across modern and traditional systems of medicine.
- **Healthcare Professionals Registry (HPR).** The registry of doctors and other healthcare professionals.

One point is easy to miss: ABDM does not hold your patients' records. The FAQ says medical records are created and stored by healthcare providers under their own retention policies, and ABDM only carries the exchange between them after the patient consents. Only the registry data is stored centrally.

## Is ABDM compulsory for a private clinic?

No. [ABDM's FAQ](https://abdm.gov.in/FAQ) says participation is voluntary for citizens and for healthcare facilities, and that the facility's management takes the decision. Enrolment on the HFR is voluntary. Once a facility decides to join, its healthcare professionals need to register on the HPR for the facility to be fully part of the network. A doctor who also works at a clinic that has not joined uses their HPR ID only at the one that has.

Patients do not have to have an ABHA either. The FAQ says it is not mandatory for citizens, and that a private hospital can give a patient who does not want to use ABHA another number from its own system.

The direction of travel is set out in a 2022 paper on ABDM's website, [Accelerating Digitisation in Healthcare Delivery](https://abdm.gov.in/strapicms/uploads/Accelerating_Digitisation_in_Healthcare_Delivery_v3_fad6fe700f.pdf). It proposes that facilities in public schemes, including hospitals empanelled under PMJAY, join ABDM at the earliest, and that registered clinical establishments hold an ABDM facility ID and keep records in ABDM-enabled systems, with other facilities following in a graded manner. Those are proposals in a paper, not rules. Check the current position with your state before you plan around them.

## Registering the clinic and the doctors

Both registries now sit on one portal, the [National Healthcare Providers Registry](https://nhpr.abdm.gov.in/), and the old Health Facility Registry and Healthcare Professionals Registry addresses lead there. The clinic's registration is done by a facility manager. [ABDM's FAQ](https://abdm.gov.in/FAQ) sets out the steps:

1. Go to the portal as a facility manager, authenticate, and generate a Healthcare Professional ID.
2. Log in with that ID and fill in the facility's application form.
3. Submit it. On submission, a Facility ID is generated.
4. Wait for verification. The details count as self-declared until State or UT government officials verify them.

The FAQ puts enrolment at 20 to 30 minutes if the information is ready. Have two photographs to upload, one of the building and one of the board, and an address proof. For a private facility the FAQ lists documents such as the Clinical Establishments Act registration certificate, a utility bill, a rent agreement or a property record.

Each doctor registers on the Healthcare Professionals Registry through the same portal, and the doctor's professional council does the verification, not the district. Neither registry charges a fee.

## What the milestones M1 to M3 mean at the clinic

The milestones describe what the clinic's software can do on the ABDM network. The clinic does not pass them itself; its software vendor does. ABDM's FAQ says every integrating entity has to submit a functional testing report and certificate, and meet the NHA's information security policy before it gets production keys.

| Milestone | What the software does | What changes at the clinic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | Creates or verifies a patient's ABHA number | At registration, the desk creates or verifies the patient's ABHA |
| M2 | Acts as a Health Information Provider: links the clinic's records to the patient's ABHA address and shares them on request | With the patient's consent, the prescription or report from the visit can appear in the patient's own health records app |
| M3 | Acts as a Health Information User: gets records from other facilities and shows them, with the patient's consent | With the patient's consent, the doctor can see records that other ABDM-linked facilities created |

The sources: M1 is "Creation and Verification of ABHA Number" in the NHA's [M1 document](https://sandboxcms.abdm.gov.in/uploads/ABHA_M1_API_document_V1_R1_bab8b1bd_fb943e3e71.pdf). The [incentive scheme's Corrigendum 7](https://abdm.gov.in/strapicms/uploads/DHIS_corrigendum_7_c1d7e09cc2.pdf) ties M2 to the Health Information Provider role and M3 to the Health Information User role. What each role does is from the [2022 paper](https://abdm.gov.in/strapicms/uploads/Accelerating_Digitisation_in_Healthcare_Delivery_v3_fad6fe700f.pdf) above.

Separately from the milestones, ABDM's Scan and Share, described in [ABDM's FAQ](https://abdm.gov.in/FAQ), lets a patient share their profile at the desk by scanning the clinic's QR code. The FAQ says it is meant to cut the wait at registration, and the details are used to generate the OPD token.

Consent works record by record. The FAQ says a patient can share selected records rather than everything, and for a set time, such as 48 hours or 7 days. When the time runs out, the clinic loses access. For the front desk, that means consent becomes part of registration, and someone has to explain it to the patient in plain words. The FAQ also says ABDM's Health Data Management Policy asks for the patient's consent before a record is linked. The [patient data privacy guide](/patient-data-privacy-laws-in-india/) covers the DPDP Act side.

## What the incentive scheme pays a clinic

The Digital Health Incentive Scheme has offered facilities payments for ABDM transactions since 1 January 2023. [Corrigendum 7](https://abdm.gov.in/strapicms/uploads/DHIS_corrigendum_7_c1d7e09cc2.pdf), dated 9 April 2026, set the terms for April to September 2026, subject to funds:

- Corrigendum 7 does not cover October 2026 onwards, so check whether the NHA has issued a further corrigendum before you count on the money.
- For a health facility or clinic, a transaction counts only if it is done on a system integrated with ABDM up to M3.
- Nothing is paid at 100 eligible transactions a month or fewer. Above that, the facility is paid for each eligible record beyond the first 100: Rs 10 for a diagnostic report or discharge summary, and Rs 5 for other records, such as a prescription or an OP consultation.
- Records count only if they are linked to a KYC-verified ABHA, and Scan and Share registrations are not paid.
- At most one transaction per ABHA a day, and five a month, counts.
- From July 2026, incentives are paid only where the software is fully on version 3 of ABDM's interfaces for M1 to M3.

For a small clinic, the threshold matters most. A clinic that sees few patients with KYC-verified ABHAs may not cross 100 eligible transactions a month.

## Questions to ask a software vendor about ABDM

Put the same questions to every vendor on your shortlist, and ask for the answers in writing, with dates.

1. Which milestones is your software live on in production, as opposed to the sandbox, and since when?
2. Is it on version 3 of ABDM's interfaces for M1 to M3?
3. What does my front desk do at registration to create or verify an ABHA, and what does the patient see?
4. How is the patient's consent recorded, and what happens in the software when it expires?
5. If we leave, how do we take our records with us?
