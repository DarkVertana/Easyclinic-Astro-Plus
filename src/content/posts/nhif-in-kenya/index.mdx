  - question: Does a clinic apply to SHA to be empanelled?
    answer: >-
      No, a clinic applies to the body that licenses and accredits it, such as the KMPDC or the Clinical
      Officers Council, and SHA empanels every licensed and certified facility on the list that body sends
      it, under section 33 of the Social Health Insurance Act as
      [SHA's empanelment notice](https://www.sha.go.ke/empanelment-of-health-care-providers-and-health-facilities/)
      quotes it. What the clinic does with SHA directly is the contract, which it signs on SHA's
      [e-contracting portal](https://econtracting.sha.go.ke/).
  - question: Is our old NHIF contract still valid?
    answer: >-
      No, NHIF transitioned to SHA on 1 October 2024, and apart from emergency treatment SHA pays claims
      only to facilities it has empanelled and contracted, so a clinic needs an SHA contract.
      [SHA's transition notice](https://www.sha.go.ke/public-notice-to-all-employers-transition-to-the-social-health-insurance-fund-shif-and-employer-portal-guidance/)
      gave 30 September 2024 as the last date for NHIF admissions.
  - question: How long does SHA take to contract a clinic after empanelment?
    answer: >-
      SHA's [onboarding page](https://www.sha.go.ke/onboarding-facilities/) says it starts contracting
      within 30 days of publishing the list of empanelled facilities in the Kenya Gazette. The licensing
      step before that sits with your regulator, and SHA gives no timeline for it.
  - question: Can a clinic run by a clinical officer be contracted by SHA?
    answer: >-
      Yes, SHA's [contracted-facilities page](https://www.sha.go.ke/contracted-facilities/) names the
      Clinical Officers Council alongside the KMPDC as a regulator of the facilities it contracts. The
      [proposed 2026 to 2029 contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part1/)
      asks for licensing by the KMPDC or the Clinical Officers Council, whichever applies to the facility.
  - question: Does a clinic need an electronic system to work with SHA?
    answer: >-
      Under the [proposed 2026 to 2029 contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part1/),
      yes: a contracted clinic must run a working electronic medical record or an approved claims system
      that sends claims electronically, and the
      [Primary Healthcare Fund contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-b1-primary-healthcare-fund-contract/)
      asks for a system certified by the Digital Health Agency. SHA's
      [notice of 31 August 2026](https://www.sha.go.ke/notice-to-healthcare-providers-extension-of-hmis-compliance-period/)
      set 30 September 2026 as the deadline for its HMIS requirements, and the Digital Health Agency
      lists certified systems in its [public registry](https://certification.dha.go.ke/registry).
  - question: How fast must we send SHA claims, and how fast does SHA pay?
    answer: >-
      SHA's [FAQ](https://www.sha.go.ke/faqs/) says claims must be submitted within 7 days of discharge
      and that SHA processes and pays them within 21 working days. The
      [proposed contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part2/) counts the
      7 days from the date of service or of discharge, whichever is later, and gives 14 days to fix and
      resend a claim SHA sends back.
  - question: How does SHA pay a primary care clinic?
    answer: >-
      Under the Primary Healthcare Fund SHA pays by capitation, which its
      [FAQ](https://www.sha.go.ke/faqs/) describes as a fixed, pre-paid amount for each enrolled patient
      over a set period, whatever the number of services. The proposed
      [Primary Healthcare Fund contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-b1-primary-healthcare-fund-contract/)
      covers facilities at KEPH Level 2 or 3 and says services under it are free to the patient at the
      point of use.
  - question: Will SHA pay what NHIF still owes our clinic?
    answer: >-
      SHA is paying verified NHIF claims of KSh 10 million and below in batches, and for larger claims
      it has started with a verification exercise for Level 4 facilities. In
      [July 2026](https://www.sha.go.ke/settlement-of-outstanding-claims-from-the-defunct-national-health-insurance-fund-nhif-ksh-10-million-and-below/)
      SHA said KSh 4 billion had been released for the smaller claims and listed 3,527 eligible
      facilities, each of which signs an agreement confirming its verified NHIF claims are fully settled
      before the payment is released. For Level 4 facilities owed KSh 10 million or more, SHA
      [said in September 2026](https://www.sha.go.ke/notice-on-nhif-defunct-payment-of-10m-and-above-in-level-4/)
      that a verification exercise would start on 21 September 2026, and that Level 3 facilities would
      hear separately.

This page used to explain NHIF empanelment. NHIF transitioned to the Social Health Authority (SHA) on 1 October 2024, and the last NHIF admissions were on 30 September 2024, according to [SHA's transition notice](https://www.sha.go.ke/public-notice-to-all-employers-transition-to-the-social-health-insurance-fund-shif-and-employer-portal-guidance/). The old NHIF website now returns an error.

What follows is the SHA route. It comes from SHA's own notices and from the proposed contracts SHA published for comment in September 2026, for the [contracting cycle](https://www.sha.go.ke/notice-on-new-contracting-cycle/) that runs from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2029. A proposed contract can change before it is signed, so where this post quotes one, check the version SHA sends you.

## What changed when SHA replaced NHIF

The old version of this page walked through an application at an NHIF branch and approval by the NHIF board. None of that applies now.

Under section 33 of the Social Health Insurance Act, a facility that wants to be empanelled applies to the body responsible for accreditation for quality of care, not to SHA. SHA then empanels every licensed and certified facility on the list that body sends it, as [SHA's empanelment notice](https://www.sha.go.ke/empanelment-of-health-care-providers-and-health-facilities/) quotes the Act and Regulation 31. In that notice SHA said it had received the KMPDC's list of licensed health facilities, and it asked every facility to check that its licences were up to date with its regulator.

Empanelment does not happen in rounds. SHA's [onboarding page](https://www.sha.go.ke/onboarding-facilities/) says it empanels continuously, updating the list as it goes, and starts contracting within 30 days of publishing the list of empanelled facilities in the Kenya Gazette.

The contract is what gets you paid. Apart from emergency treatment, SHA pays claims only to facilities that are both empanelled and contracted, under Regulation 31 as quoted in the same [empanelment notice](https://www.sha.go.ke/empanelment-of-health-care-providers-and-health-facilities/).

## Step 1: get licensed by your regulator

Your clinic reaches SHA's list through its regulator, so the licences come first.

- The KMPDC registers and licenses health facilities as well as doctors and dentists, [by its own description](https://kmpdc.go.ke/). SHA's [contracted-facilities page](https://www.sha.go.ke/contracted-facilities/) names the KMPDC and the [Clinical Officers Council](https://clinicalofficerscouncil.org/) as the regulators of the facilities it contracts, and the Clinical Officers Council issues private practice licences.
- Every clinician needs their own registration and a current licence, which the [proposed contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part1/) makes a condition. Doctors register with the KMPDC, and our [KMPDC registration guide](/how-do-i-get-approval-from-the-kmpdc-in-kenya/) lists the documents.
- A pharmacy or a laboratory inside the clinic needs its own approvals. The [proposed contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part1/) counts the Pharmacy and Poisons Board and the KMLTTB among the regulators, and asks for the extra approvals behind any pharmacy or laboratory service the clinic declares. The [KMLTTB](https://kmlttb.org/) renews laboratory licences every year.
- The facility must be on the Kenya Master Health Facility List with a facility ID (FID) and a KEPH level, at Level 2 or above, under clause 5.1.5 of the [proposed contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part1/).

If your clinic is licensed but missing from SHA's list, start with the regulator that licensed it, because SHA empanels from that regulator's list.

## Step 2: sign the contract on the e-contracting portal

SHA's [e-contracting portal](https://econtracting.sha.go.ke/) sets out five steps:

1. Register the facility and its administrator.
2. Fetch the facility's details by its HFR ID or licence number.
3. Complete the contract, including the clinic's tariffs and bank details.
4. Submit it and have it co-signed by a witness.
5. Track the contract and renew it from the dashboard.

The [onboarding page](https://www.sha.go.ke/onboarding-facilities/) adds that the contract is signed electronically in the Signatory and Witness sections, and must be fully executed before the clinic can formally work with SHA. The [proposed contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part1/) goes further: no contract exists until both sides have signed and SHA has issued a Contract Reference Number, and an application or an assessment on its own creates none.

SHA contracts a clinic only for services its licence allows and SHA has verified it can deliver. Under the same [proposed contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part1/), a service the licence covers but the clinic cannot actually offer is recorded as a service gap and cannot be claimed.

For the 2026 cycle, SHA scheduled online briefings on the new e-contracting process for 9 and 10 September 2026, with Level 2 and Level 3 facilities on the 10th, and it takes contracting questions at contractmanagement@sha.go.ke, according to its [notice of 8 September 2026](https://www.sha.go.ke/notice-on-new-contracting-cycle/). If you object to the terms of your e-contract, SHA's [FAQ](https://www.sha.go.ke/faqs/) says to write to customerservice@sha.go.ke or call 0800 720 601.

## Documents to have ready

The proposed contract ([part 1](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part1/) and [part 2](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part2/) of its general terms) asks a clinic to hold these, and to keep them current for as long as the contract runs:

- Facility registration and an operating licence, from the KMPDC or the Clinical Officers Council.
- The registration and current licence of every clinician.
- The licences for anything else you offer, such as a pharmacy or a laboratory, and, where they apply, approvals such as fire safety and radiation.
- Ownership documents, with a declaration of the clinic's beneficial owners down to the individual people. The contract does not name the documents. Have a current official search from the [Business Registration Service](https://brs.go.ke/fee-schedule-companies-registry/): its fee schedule lists the search for a limited company as the CR12, and for a registered business name as the CR13.
- A Kenya Revenue Authority tax compliance certificate.
- A bank account in the clinic's official name, with a bank confirmation letter.
- Professional indemnity and medical malpractice insurance from an insurer licensed in Kenya.
- A signed anti-fraud and anti-bribery undertaking on SHA's own form, which also covers conflicts of interest.

The same contract asks for the clinic's name, facility ID, KEPH level and location to match across the registration, the licence, SHA's records and the bank documents. A clinic registered under one name and banking under another should fix that before it applies.

## The electronic record SHA now expects

Under the [proposed contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part1/), a contracted clinic must run a working electronic medical record or an approved claims system that can send claims electronically within SHA's deadlines, with internet-connected devices at every point of service. The proposed [Primary Healthcare Fund contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-b1-primary-healthcare-fund-contract/), which covers facilities at KEPH Level 2 or 3, asks for an electronic health information system certified by the Digital Health Agency, one that can report to SHA's Centralized Digital Platform and to the Kenya Health Information System.

On 31 August 2026 SHA [extended the deadline](https://www.sha.go.ke/notice-to-healthcare-providers-extension-of-hmis-compliance-period/) for its HMIS requirements by one month, to 30 September 2026, and said the extension was for one month only. It pointed providers with system problems to the Digital Health Agency's helpdesk at helpdesk@dha.go.ke.

The Digital Health Agency lists the systems it has certified in a [public registry](https://certification.dha.go.ke/registry). Before you rely on any system for SHA work, look the vendor up there. This post does not state EasyClinic's certification or SHA status.

## Keeping the contract

- **Licences.** Under the [proposed contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part1/), re-upload every licence and insurance policy at least seven days before it expires, and tell SHA within seven working days if a licence lapses or is suspended. A lapsed licence counts as revoked until the renewal is uploaded and verified, and payment is withheld for services given in that gap.
- **Claims.** SHA's [FAQ](https://www.sha.go.ke/faqs/) says claims must be submitted within 7 days of discharge and that SHA processes and pays them within 21 working days. The [proposed contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part2/) counts the 7 days from the date of service or of discharge, whichever is later, gives 14 days to correct and resend a claim SHA sends back, and lets an uncorrected one lapse.
- **The patient's bill.** The same [proposed contract](https://www.sha.go.ke/part-a-master-general-terms-conditions-part2/) bars the clinic from passing on to the patient the amount of a claim SHA rejected, sent back, reduced or withheld.
- **Suspension and appeal.** SHA's [onboarding page](https://www.sha.go.ke/onboarding-facilities/) says it will suspend or end the contract of a facility that falls short of the criteria and standards in the Social Health Insurance Regulations, 2024, and breaches its contract terms. A facility aggrieved over its empanelment application can appeal to the Dispute Resolution Tribunal within 30 days of the Board's decision, under section 33(4) of the Act as quoted in SHA's [empanelment notice](https://www.sha.go.ke/empanelment-of-health-care-providers-and-health-facilities/).

How the claim itself is put together, from the plan checked at registration to the payer's payment, is on the [insurance claims page](/features/insurance-claims/).

## Unpaid NHIF claims

Some clinics are still owed money by NHIF. In a [notice of 4 July 2026](https://www.sha.go.ke/settlement-of-outstanding-claims-from-the-defunct-national-health-insurance-fund-nhif-ksh-10-million-and-below/), SHA said the government had released KSh 4 billion to settle verified claims of KSh 10 million and below owed by the defunct NHIF. Eligible facilities sign an agreement confirming that their verified NHIF claims are fully settled, and SHA listed 3,527 of them on its website and at its county offices.

SHA publishes those payments in batches on its [claim payments page](https://www.sha.go.ke/claim-payments/). If your clinic is on the list, compare the verified amount with your own records before you sign, because the agreement confirms the claims are settled in full.

Larger debts are at an earlier stage. In [September 2026](https://www.sha.go.ke/notice-on-nhif-defunct-payment-of-10m-and-above-in-level-4/) SHA told Level 4 facilities owed KSh 10 million or more by NHIF that a verification exercise would start on 21 September 2026, and asked them to have their invoices, service delivery records, claim submission records and any other documents they sent NHIF ready. It said Level 3 facilities with outstanding claims would hear separately.

SHA published the 2026 to 2029 contracts as proposals for comment, and this post was checked against SHA's site on 26 September 2026.
