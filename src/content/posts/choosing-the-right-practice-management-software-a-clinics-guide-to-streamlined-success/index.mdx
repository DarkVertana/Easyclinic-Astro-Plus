Every clinic software demo is built to look good. The way to choose well is to walk in knowing what your clinic needs, and to test exactly that. This guide turns your clinic's day into 12 checks to take to each demo, and ends with how Easy Clinic answers each one.

## Start from your clinic's day

Before you look at any software, write down one ordinary day, from the first patient at the desk to the cash counted at closing time. For each step, note who does it and what they write on. That list is what you test every system against, and it keeps the demo on your clinic instead of on the vendor's favourite screens.

The size of the clinic changes the list:

- A doctor on their own with a receptionist needs the note and the prescription done in minutes, so the evenings are not spent on paperwork. See [software for a solo clinic](/solutions/solo-clinic/).
- A polyclinic needs one record shared by every doctor, with roles that decide who sees what. See [software for a polyclinic](/solutions/polyclinic/).
- A chain needs central control, so the branches follow the same protocols and their numbers sit side by side. See [software for a clinic chain](/solutions/clinic-chain/).
- A hospital OPD needs the queue to keep moving, and claims that go out complete. See [software for a hospital OPD](/solutions/hospital-opd/).

## The 12 checks

Take these to every demo, and ask the vendor to show each one on screen rather than describe it.

### 1. Finding a patient and booking the visit

The receptionist should find a returning patient by name or phone number straight away, and put walk-ins and booked patients on the same list for the doctor. Ask which channels the appointment reminders go out on, and who pays for each message. Then register a new patient and book a follow-up while you watch.

### 2. The note and the prescription

This is where the doctor's day goes. Ask the vendor to type your five commonest prescriptions in front of you, using the shortcuts the system offers. Check that the templates fit your specialty and that the printed prescription looks the way you want it to.

### 3. Who can see what

Each person should see what their job needs and no more. The receptionist books and bills, and the clinical notes stay with the clinical staff. Ask the vendor to log in as the receptionist and show you what that login can open.

### 4. The bill, raised from the visit

The bill should come from the visit, so a dressing added in the consulting room reaches the bill without anyone having to remember it. Ask to see a bill with a part-payment, and the day-end close that adds up what was collected.

### 5. Insurance claims, if you take insurance

If some of your patients pay through insurance, ask how a claim is built from the visit and the bill, and what happens when one is rejected. Ask which insurers or claim systems in your country the software sends claims to today, rather than which are planned.

### 6. Pharmacy and lab, if you run them

A pharmacy module should dispense from the doctor's prescription and track each batch to its expiry date. A lab module should take orders from the chart and send results back to the patient's record. If you run neither, do not pay for them.

### 7. The owner's numbers

Ask what the owner sees at the end of the day and at the end of the month, such as the day's takings and revenue by doctor. If the reports have to be exported to a spreadsheet before they make sense, ask who in your clinic will do that every week.

### 8. A second doctor or a second branch

Ask what happens to the price when you add a doctor, and what happens to the records when you open a second branch. A patient who visits both branches should have one record there, not two.

### 9. Internet and power

Cloud software keeps the records off the clinic's own machines and needs a working internet line. Software installed in the clinic can run without the line but needs a server that someone looks after and backs up. Ask which one you are buying, and what happens to a half-written note when the line drops. If your power or your line is unreliable, read [running a cloud EMR with unreliable power and internet](/cloud-based-emr-for-nigerian-clinics/).

### 10. Your country's rules

Every country has its own rules for health data and for claims, such as [ABDM](/glossary/#abdm) in India, [SHA](/glossary/#sha) in Kenya, [NABIDH](/glossary/#nabidh) in Dubai and the [NDPA](/glossary/#ndpa) in Nigeria. For each one that applies to your clinic, ask the vendor where it stands today, with the certificate or integration named and a date. "Ready" or "compliant" without a date is not an answer.

### 11. Training and support

Ask for the training plan in days and minutes, and who it covers. Ask how you reach support after training, whether it costs extra, and who your named contact is. The [questions to ask an EMR vendor](/questions-to-ask-your-clinic-emr-software-provider/) go through this conversation in more detail.

### 12. The price, and what happens if you leave

Compare the price for your clinic as it will be in two years, with the doctors and branches you expect, and not only as it is today. Then ask about the exit: how long the contract runs, and what data you get back, and in what format, if you leave.

## Four mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing without the receptionist.** The receptionist is on the software from the first patient to the last. If staff feel it adds steps, they stop using it, so bring them to the demo.
- **Choosing on price alone.** The cheapest quote per month can cost more once the extra logins and add-ons are counted, and the dearest is not always the best fit.
- **Forgetting the records you already have.** Ask what moves across from paper or your old system, and what stays behind.
- **Buying for today only.** A second doctor or a second branch may be a year away. Find out what each one costs before you need it.

## Rolling it out

- Go live in a quieter week, and expect the desk to be slower for the first few days.
- Name one person in the clinic who owns the switch. They gather the staff's questions and take them to the vendor.
- Train on your own cases, so the first real patient is not the first time anyone has used the screen.
- If the vendor allows it, go live in stages, starting with the appointment book and adding the chart once the desk is settled.
