If the front desk decides message by message whether a patient gets an SMS or a WhatsApp, some patients get both and some get neither. It works better to decide once, by the kind of message, and to ask each patient at registration which channel they want. This post sorts the messages a clinic sends into the two channels, and sets out what each channel's rules ask of you.

## How SMS and WhatsApp differ at a clinic

| At the clinic | SMS | WhatsApp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which patients it reaches | Anyone whose phone can receive a text, including a basic phone with no internet | Patients who use WhatsApp on that number; the message arrives when the phone is next online |
| What it can carry | Text. A link works only if the phone can open it | Text, and files such as a PDF of a lab report or a prescription |
| Whether the patient can reply | Usually not, when it comes from a named sender, so the message needs a number to call | Yes, in the same chat, and the clinic can answer in its own words for 24 hours after the patient writes |
| What you need before the first message | In India, a registered sender header and registered message templates | The patient's agreement, and a template Meta has approved for any message the clinic starts |
| Who sets the price per message | The telecom operator or the SMS provider | Meta, by the kind of message and the country code of the patient's number |
| On Easy Clinic | SMS and email reminders on every plan | WhatsApp reminders and messages from the Premium plan |

The short version: SMS is the channel that reaches everyone, and WhatsApp is the channel patients can answer. This post quotes no price per message, because rates depend on the country and the provider, and Meta can change its WhatsApp rates on the first day of each quarter.

## Which messages to send by SMS

Send by SMS the messages that must reach every patient, whatever phone they carry.

- **The appointment reminder.** A reminder sent only on WhatsApp misses the patients on basic phones, and the patients who never agreed to WhatsApp messages from you.
- **A change everyone must hear about.** The clinic is closed on Friday, or the doctor's Tuesday evening session is cancelled.
- **The first message to a new number.** Until you know whether the patient uses WhatsApp on that number, SMS is the channel you can count on.
- **Every message to a patient who said no to WhatsApp.** Their reminders and notices go by SMS, or by email if they gave you an address.

Write each SMS so it stands alone. Put the clinic's name and the appointment time in the first line, and end with a phone number to call, because the patient probably cannot reply to it.

## Which messages to send on WhatsApp

Send on WhatsApp the messages that carry a file or invite a reply, and only to the patients who chose WhatsApp.

- **The prescription after the consult.** The dosage instructions are on the patient's phone when they reach the pharmacy counter.
- **The lab report when it is ready.** The report itself, rather than a photo of a printout taken at the desk.
- **A reminder the patient can answer.** Ask the patient to reply if they cannot come, so the desk can offer the slot to someone else.
- **A question after the visit.** A feedback request, or a check-in two days after a procedure.
- **The registration form before a first visit.** A link the patient fills in on their own phone, so nobody types it in at the desk.

A patient can answer any of these in the same chat, so someone at the clinic has to read the replies the same day. If replies are answered automatically, WhatsApp's policy asks the business to give the patient a clear way to reach a person, such as a phone number or a handover to staff.

## What to keep out of both

- **Diagnoses and test values in the message text.** A phone can be read by whoever picks it up. WhatsApp's [Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) also says not to send or ask for health information on WhatsApp where the law requires a system held to a higher standard for it, so check your country's rules before a report or a result goes out.
- **Card numbers and ID numbers.** The same policy says not to ask people to share full card numbers or ID card numbers, and an SMS is no safer a place for them.
- **One patient's details in front of another.** WhatsApp's policy does not allow a business to pass information from one customer's chat to another customer. A patient group is no place for anything personal either, because every member sees what is posted there.
- **Offers on medicines.** WhatsApp's policy does not allow a business to sell or promote prescription drugs on WhatsApp, so a refill message is about booking the doctor's review, never about a price.

## The rules each channel sets

### SMS in India

In India, business SMS fall under TRAI's [Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations, 2018](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-09/RegulationUcc19072018.pdf). A business has to register as a sender with a telecom operator before it sends commercial messages. The messages go out under a registered header, the sender name of up to eleven characters that the patient sees, and the wording is registered as a content template. Since the [February 2025 amendment](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf), a message that mixes promotion into a service or transactional message counts as promotional, and a promotional message has to carry a way to opt out in the same message.

For a clinic, that means a reminder with an offer tacked on is treated as a promotional message. If you send SMS through clinic software or an SMS provider, ask whose registered header and templates your messages go out under.

### WhatsApp, in every country

WhatsApp's [Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) lets a business message a person only after the person has given it their number and agreed to receive its messages, and the business must honour every request to stop, whether it arrives on WhatsApp or across the desk. On the WhatsApp Business Platform, which clinic software such as Easy Clinic sends through, any message the clinic starts goes out on a template Meta has approved. A reply to a patient who wrote in the last 24 hours can be written freely.

Meta also puts each template in a category, and a message that mixes a visit update with a promotion counts as marketing under its [template guidelines](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/template-categorization). Meta charges for the template messages it delivers, at a rate set by the category and the patient's country code, and its [pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing) says that from 1 October 2026 it also charges for free-form replies inside the 24 hours, after the first 1,000 a month on each business number. How templates get approved, and what that means for a clinic, is on the [WhatsApp page](/features/whatsapp/).

### Data protection law, for both

Both channels use the patient's phone number, so your country's data protection law applies whichever you choose. The [patient engagement page](/features/patient-engagement/#consent-by-country) sets out, in general terms, what the laws of India, Kenya, the UAE and Nigeria ask of a clinic that messages its patients.

## How to ask each patient

Ask at registration, while the patient is at the desk anyway.

1. Do you use WhatsApp on this number, and may we send you messages about your visits there, such as reminders and reports?
2. Separately, would you like greetings or news from the clinic?

The best practices in WhatsApp's [Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) advise a separate opt-in for each kind of message. Asking this way lets a patient say yes to reminders and no to everything else. Write the answers where the desk will see them at the next visit. A patient who says no to WhatsApp, or has a basic phone, gets SMS.

On every plan, Easy Clinic sends appointment reminders by SMS and email, days or hours before the visit at the times the clinic sets. On the Premium plan, reminders and other messages also go out on WhatsApp from the clinic software, including the prescription after the consult and the lab report once it is on the record. A patient without WhatsApp, or one who has asked you to stop, can still get SMS or email reminders if they agreed to those.
