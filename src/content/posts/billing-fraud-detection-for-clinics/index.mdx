On the day it happens, billing fraud at a clinic looks like an ordinary bill. A regular patient gets 20% off. A consultation is billed at last year's price. A refund goes back in cash, and a bill is voided at six in the evening. Each of these examples can be honest, and the owner's problem is that the honest ones and the dishonest ones look the same until somebody lines them up.

This post is for the owner of a single clinic and for the finance head of a chain, where the problem grows with every counter. The controls are the same in both. A chain applies them branch by branch.

## Where money goes missing at the counter

- A discount given at the counter that nobody approved.
- Cash taken and not recorded, or recorded for less than the patient paid.
- A refund handed back without anyone checking why.
- A price typed over the list price by hand.
- The same service charged differently at two branches.

Without controls, an honest mistake and a deliberate one leave the same gap in the takings, and the owner cannot tell them apart. That is why the controls below are about the system rather than about any one person. When prices are unclear, staff improvise. When the cash is checked only at month end, nobody remembers the day it went missing.

## Prices the desk picks from a list

The first control takes one decision away from the counter: what to charge. Every service has one price on a list, and the desk picks the line instead of typing an amount. A category of patient that pays less, such as your own staff or the members of a scheme you run, gets its price from the list the owner set, and not from whoever is on duty that day.

A chain adds one rule. Each branch charges from the list that applies to it, so a scan does not cost one thing at one branch because a receptionist remembers an old price.

In EasyClinic, each service and item has its price on the service list, and a price changed on the list shows on the next bill. Each branch can have its own price list, and prices and discounts can be set for a category of patients. Running more than one location needs Premium or Enterprise.

## Approval before money comes off a bill

The second control is an approval for anything that reduces what the clinic collects: a discount, a voided bill, a write-off or a refund. The approval does two jobs. It makes the person asking stop and say why, and it leaves a record that someone other than the cashier agreed.

Ask for the reason in words. "Regular patient" is a reason you can review later, and a blank is not.

Set the limit where it catches what matters. If the owner has to approve every small rounding, approvals turn into a rubber stamp and stop catching anything.

In EasyClinic, discounts, write-offs, refunds and voided invoices go through authorisation. On the Enterprise plan, a workflow rule can set a discount limit, so a larger discount waits for approval from the person head office names.

## A cash count against the system every evening

The third control is the day-end close. At closing, the cash in the drawer is counted against the cash the system says was taken, and each other payment mode is checked against its own statement. A gap is written down that evening, with the counter and the people on it. It is never quietly adjusted into the next day's takings.

A single short day is usually a mistake. The same counter short every week is worth a conversation.

In EasyClinic, access to the cash register is set by role, so only the staff you allow can take money on it. Cash coming in and going out is tracked through the day, and the till is balanced across payment modes. The end-of-day report gives a snapshot of the day's cash transactions to count the drawer against. Tracing a gap between the drawer and the report is covered on the [revenue management page](/features/revenue-management/).

## A weekly review of the exceptions, by user

A day's total can look normal while one person's pattern does not. The fourth control is looking at the patterns. Once a week, list for each user:

- discounts given, as a share of what that person billed
- refunds, and how many of them were paid out in cash
- bills voided after the payment was taken
- days their counter was short, and by how much
- prices typed over the list, if your system allows it

Compare people who do the same job on the same shifts. A receptionist who gives far more discounts than the others has a reason, and it may be a good one: a doctor who tells patients to ask for a discount, or a scheme price nobody told the rest of the desk about. Ask before you conclude.

Look only at the flags. Reviewing the exceptions keeps the review short, and it keeps suspicion off the staff whose bills are in order.

EasyClinic keeps an audit log for each user, and the till keeps an audit trail of every transaction, so a discount or a refund can be traced to the login that made it. Reports export to Excel and PDF for the review. Someone still has to read the list and ask the questions.

## Test the controls in a two-week pilot

A pilot lets the staff see the controls working before they apply everywhere, and it gives you a starting figure to compare against.

1. **Choose the scope.** Two weeks, at one branch or on the services with the most discounts and refunds.
2. **Measure where you start.** Before changing anything, record a week of discounts and refunds by user, with the daily cash gap for each counter.
3. **Switch the controls on.** Load the price list and set the approval limit. From the first evening, count the cash against the system.
4. **Review and adjust.** At the end of each week, compare with the starting week and move any limit that catches too much or too little. Train the staff on what the review found, using real cases.

Tell the staff about the pilot before it starts. The controls are there so that an honest person can show their bills were right.

## Signs a clinic needs these controls now

- Margins fall while patient numbers hold steady.
- Bills are changed after they are raised more often than they used to be.
- Changing a price by hand at the counter has become routine.
- The accountant keeps asking about the same kind of entry.

## What the controls cannot catch

Cash taken with no bill leaves nothing to count against. One defence is a receipt from the system for every patient, because a patient who is used to getting one notices when it does not come. EasyClinic can send the receipt to the patient's phone by SMS or email, or on WhatsApp with Premium.

Two people who approve each other's discounts can defeat any approval rule. In a chain, the person who approves should not be the person who bills, and the weekly review should go to someone outside the branch.

The controls also slow the desk a little on the bills that need an exception. A bill at the list price needs no approval and moves through the desk as before.
