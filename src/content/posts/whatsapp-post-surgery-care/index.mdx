This is a message plan for the first week after an operation or a day-care procedure, for clinics that send patients home soon afterwards. The surgeon writes what goes in each message. This post covers how the week is laid out and who does what.

## Why the first week at home goes wrong

Discharge instructions arrive all at once, when the patient is tired and the family is thinking about getting them home. By the next morning, much of it can be forgotten. Then the questions start, from the patient or a relative and often in the evening: is this swelling normal, can I have a bath yet. The doctor ends up answering from a personal phone, because nobody else at the clinic knows the answers.

A short message each day puts the instructions back in front of the patient in small pieces, on the day they apply. It also gives the clinic a daily check on each patient without anyone picking up the phone.

## What the surgeon decides before the first message

The plan only works if the clinical content is settled before the first patient goes home. For each procedure, the surgeon decides:

1. **The warning signs, in plain words.** For a surgical wound, Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust lists [the signs of infection](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/surgical-wounds-and-preventing-infections) as skin around the wound that is red or sore, or hot and swollen; liquid, often green or yellow pus, coming from the wound; the wound opening; and feeling generally unwell or having a temperature. It tells patients with any of these to contact their hospital doctor or GP straight away.
2. **Who answers a YELLOW reply,** and by what time on a working day.
3. **What a RED reply sets off,** and which number the patient calls out of hours.
4. **Whether the clinic wants photos.** Some surgeons do and some would rather see the patient. Decide before anyone asks.
5. **Which number the messages go to.** Often a relative reads them for the patient. Use the number the patient chooses at discharge, and write down that the patient agreed.

## The seven-day plan

The sample wording below is written for this page. Words in round brackets are for the surgeon to fill in, procedure by procedure.

| Day | What the message does | Sample wording | Reply asked for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0, a few hours after discharge | Sets expectations | "You are home. Today is day 0 of your recovery. What is normal today: (surgeon's list). Call us on (number) at once if (warning signs). Reply HOME when you get this." | HOME |
| 1 | The first night and morning | "Take your medicines at the times on your prescription, and look after the dressing the way the nurse showed you. Reply OK when you have read this." | OK |
| 2 | Movement | "Today: (what to do) and (what to avoid). Reply WALK if you managed the walking the doctor advised, or REST if you did not." | WALK or REST |
| 3 | The check-in | "How are you today? Reply GREEN if you feel stable, YELLOW if something is bothering you, or RED if you feel worse. If you feel very unwell, do not wait for our reply: call (number) or go to the nearest emergency department." | GREEN, YELLOW or RED |
| 4 | Booking the review | "Dr (name) would like to see you between (dates). Reply with the day that suits you." | A day |
| 5 | One instruction, repeated | "One thing to keep doing: (the instruction patients most often drop). Reply YES to confirm." | YES |
| 6 | Questions | "If you have a question about your recovery, reply QUESTION and we will answer during clinic hours." | QUESTION |
| 7 | Closing the first week | "Your first week is done. Keep watching for (warning signs) until the wound has healed, and call us if you see any of them. Your review is on (date). Reply DONE." | DONE |

Each message has one job. The one-word replies make the patient do something with the message, and they show the clinic who did. A patient who has not replied HOME by the evening of day 0 may have a wrong number on file, and that is worth a call before the night.

The structure stays the same from one procedure to the next, but the words do not. An eye surgeon's day 1 message is about the drops the surgeon prescribed. An orthopaedic surgeon's is about the exercises, and how much weight the patient may put on the leg.

If the clinic sends these messages on the WhatsApp Business Platform, a message sent more than 24 hours after the patient last wrote has to be on a template Meta has approved. Meta [reviews each template](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/overview) when it is created and again when it is edited, and it does not translate them, so a clinic writing in two languages needs each version approved. Each reply opens a new [24-hour customer service window](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing), in which the clinic can answer a YELLOW in its own words.

## The GREEN, YELLOW, RED rule

| Reply on day 3 | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| GREEN | The next day's message, and nothing else |
| YELLOW | A nurse or the doctor calls the patient the same working day |
| RED | The surgeon's escalation rule, at once. The message has already told the patient not to wait for a reply |
| No reply by the evening | A call from the desk. Silence is not GREEN |

Day 3 sits early in the stretch that matters for the wound. Guy's and St Thomas' says a wound infection can happen [at any time from 2 to 3 days after surgery until the wound has healed](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/surgical-wounds-and-preventing-infections). The wound may not have healed by day 7, which is why the day 7 message repeats the warning signs instead of telling the patient all is well. If the surgeon wants a second check-in, send the day 3 message again on day 6.

## Someone has to read the replies

Name the person who reads the replies on clinic days, and the doctor who takes a RED. Put the clinic's hours in the day 0 message, so a QUESTION at 11pm is not expected to get an answer before morning. Out of hours, set an automatic reply that gives the emergency number. The [WhatsApp Business app](https://business.whatsapp.com/products/business-app), for example, can answer messages that arrive while you are away.

Photos need a decision of their own. WhatsApp's [business messaging policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy) says not to send or ask for health information on WhatsApp where the law requires a system held to a higher standard for it. Check what the law where you practise says before you ask patients to send pictures of a wound. A photo is also no substitute for seeing the wound, so a worrying one should end in a visit.

## Consent at discharge

WhatsApp's [business messaging policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy) lets a business message a person only after the person has given it their number and agreed to receive its messages, and it requires the business to respect every request to stop, made on WhatsApp or anywhere else. Ask at discharge, in plain words: "Can we send you one message a day on WhatsApp for the first week after your operation?" Write the answer on the patient's record.

A patient who says stop gets no more messages. If the surgeon still needs to hear from that patient, it becomes a phone call. ## What EasyClinic does in this plan

EasyClinic covers the parts of the plan that sit in the record and the calendar. The procedure is written on the patient's record. On Premium, the discharge prescription can go to the patient on WhatsApp, so the medicine times in the day 1 message match what is on the patient's phone. The review is booked in the calendar before the patient leaves, and the patient gets a reminder by SMS and email on every plan, or on WhatsApp on Premium. The desk can see which patients have acknowledged that reminder. When a patient writes back on WhatsApp, Cura AI answers the standard questions, though a YELLOW or a RED needs a person.

We do not claim that EasyClinic sends the daily recovery messages on its own or sorts the GREEN, YELLOW and RED replies. In this plan the daily messages and the replies are the desk's job, worked from a list of patients by discharge date. We also have no measured figure for fewer calls or better attendance at the review, so this post gives none.
