## 2\. Key Tax Benefits for Medical Startups in Kenya

The Kenyan government offers several tax benefits for medical startups in Kenya to encourage growth in the **health sector in Kenya**. The Tax Laws (Amendment) Act, assented to on 25th April 2020, introduced changes to the Income Tax Act, including a new framework for capital allowances called the “Investment Allowance.” Here’s how these benefits apply to medical startups in Kenya:

**Investment Allowance Overview**  
The Investment Allowance replaces the previous capital allowances framework, offering deductions for capital expenditures. Key features include:

- A maximum deduction rate of 100% for certain investments.
- Claims are made on a reducing balance basis.
- Decelerated claims: 50% in the first year of investment, with the residual claimed at 10% or 25% on a reducing balance.

These allowances are particularly beneficial for investing in buildings, equipment, and technology.

## 3\. Capital Allowances for Medical Startups in Kenya

The Investment Allowance provides specific deductions for medical startups in Kenya based on the type of capital expenditure. Here’s a breakdown of the allowances relevant to healthcare:

**Hospital Buildings**  
If you’re building or renovating a hospital or clinic, you can claim:

- 50% in the first year of use: This applies to hospital buildings, such as a new **healthcare clinic in Kenya**.
- 25% per year on reducing balance: The remaining value is deducted over subsequent years.

For example, if you spend KES 10 million on a new clinic building, you can deduct KES 5 million in the first year and KES 1.25 million annually thereafter (on the remaining balance).

**Hospital Equipment**  
For equipment purchases, such as diagnostic machines or telemedicine tools, the allowance is:

- 50% in the first year of use: This applies to hospital equipment, including devices used in your medical startup in Kenya.

If your **healthcare business in Kenya** spends KES 500,000 on computers and software, you can deduct KES 125,000 annually on a reducing balance basis.

**Furniture and Fittings**
