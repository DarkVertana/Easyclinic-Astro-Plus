Most of the tax relief a new clinic in Kenya can claim comes from one table: the investment allowance in the Second Schedule to the [Income Tax Act](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf). This post goes through the lines of that table a clinic is likely to use, as the Act stands at 1 July 2026, and the paperwork that makes a claim hold up. It is not tax advice. Have an adviser who already files for clinics check your return before you rely on any of it.

## The rates a clinic will use

The investment allowance is deducted when you work out the clinic's taxable profit, at a fixed rate for each kind of capital purchase. These are the lines a clinic is most likely to meet, from the table in the [Income Tax Act as KRA publishes it](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf):

| What you spend on | Rate in the Act |
| --- | --- |
| Hospital buildings | 50% in the first year of use, then the residual value at 25% a year, in equal instalments |
| Hospital equipment | 50% in the first year of use, then the residual value at 25% a year, in equal instalments |
| Computer hardware and software | 25% a year, in equal instalments |
| Furniture and fittings | 10% a year, in equal instalments |

Apart from the first-year 50%, every rate in the table is claimed in equal instalments. An earlier version of this post said the residual value was claimed on a reducing balance, but the table in the Act says equal instalments. The [Finance Act 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Finance-Act-2026.pdf) changed the rows for petroleum storage and industrial buildings, not the ones above.

The [Income Tax Act](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf) attaches conditions to these rates, and four of them matter to a clinic:

- **The building has to be licensed.** The Act allows the hospital building rate only where the building is licensed by the competent authority, so get the clinic licensed before you count on the 50%. The guide to [starting a clinic in Kenya](/the-ultimate-guide-to-starting-a-clinic-in-kenya/) covers the facility licence.
- **Neither term is defined.** The Act does not say what counts as a hospital building or as hospital equipment. Whether a two-room clinic's premises or its BP machines and examination couch qualify at 50% is a question for your adviser, and it is worth asking before you plan the year around the higher rate.
- **The 100% rate is not for clinics.** The Act allows 100% for some large investments outside Nairobi City County and Mombasa County, or in a special economic zone, but it limits that rate to hotels and to buildings and machinery used for manufacture. A clinic in Kitui gets the same 50% as a clinic in Westlands.
- **A converted building starts from what is left.** Where a building changes use, the Act limits the deduction to its residual value, or the amount not yet claimed, at the rate that applies. If you turn an existing building into a clinic, ask your adviser what that leaves you to claim.

## The rates worked through in shillings

Here is what the table gives on some round numbers. It is arithmetic on the rates, so check the timing with your adviser; the first-year figure is claimed in the year the item is first used, not the year you paid for it.

| Purchase | Cost | First year of use | Each later year | Until |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clinic building (licensed) | KES 10,000,000 | KES 5,000,000 | KES 1,250,000 | four more years |
| Medical equipment | KES 2,000,000 | KES 1,000,000 | KES 250,000 | four more years |
| Computers and software | KES 500,000 | KES 125,000 | KES 125,000 | four years in all |

These are deductions from taxable profit, not cash back. What you save depends on the rate you pay, and in a first year with more deductions than profit, the clinic shows a loss for that year. ## Keep the invoices KRA will check

A deduction is only as good as the paper behind it.

**Ask for an eTIMS invoice every time.** Section 16 of the [Income Tax Act](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf) refuses a deduction for any expenditure whose invoice was not generated through an electronic tax invoice management system, unless the Tax Procedures Act exempts the transaction. Get the eTIMS invoice from the equipment supplier when you pay, and file it with the asset register.

**Keep an asset register.** For each building, machine, computer and piece of furniture, write down the supplier, the cost, the eTIMS invoice number and the date it was first used. The date decides which year gets the 50%.

**File on iTax.** The annual income tax return is filed on iTax, in the window that [KRA's filing guide](https://www.kra.go.ke/file-my-returns) gives for each year of income. The same guide says KRA validates the income and expenses you declare against the records it holds, including electronic tax invoices where they apply, so keep the eTIMS invoice for every expense you claim.

## Check whether turnover tax applies to you first

The investment allowance only helps if the clinic pays income tax on its profit. Under section 12C of the [Income Tax Act](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf), a resident business with a turnover above KES 1 million and up to KES 25 million pays turnover tax instead, at 1.5% of gross receipts. Turnover tax is worked on receipts, so the investment allowance does not reduce it.

Two parts of [section 12C](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf) may take a clinic out of it. Turnover tax does not apply to professional fees, or to management and training fees, and a business can elect out of it by writing to the Commissioner. Whether a clinic's consultation fees count as professional fees is the first thing to settle with your adviser, because it decides whether the rest of this post applies to you.
