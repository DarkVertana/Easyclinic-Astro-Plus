This guide covers the licensing route for a private clinic in Rwanda, from registering the business to the Ministry of Health's licence to operate. It uses the government's own pages and documents, checked on 26 September 2026: the Rwanda Development Board's registrar, the IremboGov guides to health facility licensing, the Rwanda Medical and Dental Council, and the Ministry of Health's instructions on private health facilities and on health service tariffs. It gives no figures for setup or running costs, because we found no official Rwandan source for them.

## Decide what kind of facility you are opening

The Ministry of Health licenses a private facility by category, and the category decides what it may do. The [Ministerial Instructions governing private health facilities](https://www.moh.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Moh/Publications/Laws/new_law/MINISTERIAL_INSTRUCTIONS_2022_compressed.pdf), signed in October 2022, group the categories by level of care:

| Level of care | Categories in the 2022 instructions |
| --- | --- |
| Primary | Nursing home, home care, dispensary (renewals only), antenatal and postnatal clinic, cabinet, mental health clinic, paramedical clinic, institution clinic, general clinic, digital health facility |
| Secondary | Polyclinic, specialized clinic, specialized centre, diagnostic centre without super-specialized diagnostic services |
| Tertiary | Private hospital, specialized hospital, diagnostic centre with CT, MRI or other super-specialized diagnostic services |

Two rules in the same instructions shape the choice. Existing dispensaries may renew their licences, but the Ministry no longer accepts new applications for dispensaries. And each service must have at least the minimum staff it needs, with the head of each clinical service a permanent employee. The instructions put the standards for each category in their annex.

The main text of the instructions does not define the categories. For a description, the Ministry's 2017 document on [health service packages for private facilities](https://www.moh.gov.rw/index.php?eID=dumpFile&t=f&f=47025&token=7373f8b9333f62e5db302a7c33626662ca8a5e38) is the most detailed public source we found. In 2017 it described a medical practice as run by doctors for consultations, diagnostics, prescriptions and emergency laboratory tests, with stays of no more than four hours, and a medical clinic as run by a general practitioner or a specialist, with a maximum of 7 beds and night duty mandatory. Its names are older than the 2022 list, so match your plans to a 2022 category when you apply. The document also lists the services, staff and equipment it expects of each type, and in 2017 it noted that most private specialist services were in Kigali.

## Register the business with the RDB

Business registration is handled by the Office of the Registrar General at the RDB. Its [business registration page](https://org.rdb.rw/business-registration/) says registration is 100% online, free of charge and done within six working hours, and the certificate can then be downloaded from your account.

- Choose the category: an individual enterprise, which is a single owner paying personal income tax on the profit, or a limited liability company.
- Give a local, physical address as the registered address.
- Register with the tax administration within seven days of starting business. You need the TIN for the Ministry of Health's first step.

The registrar runs a support desk at the RDB building in Kigali for applicants who get stuck online.

## Check every doctor's RMDC licence

The [RMDC](https://rmdc.rw/spip.php?page=article&id_article=508) registers a medical or dental practitioner once, after checking their qualifications. The licence to practise is annual. Doctors renew it online, and the renewal needs evidence of 50 CPD credits along with the expired licence and the fee slip.

The rule that matters to a clinic owner is the date. From 1 July each year, a doctor without a valid annual licence, and anyone who employs that doctor, is contravening Law No 44/2012 of 14/01/2013, and the RMDC says such cases go to court. Put every doctor's renewal in the diary well before July, and keep a copy of each current licence on file.

For a foreign doctor, the hosting institution, meaning the clinic where the doctor will work, files the registration and licence application with the RMDC. For a licence of one year or more, the RMDC asks the Ministry of Health for clearance, which it says has taken one to two months.

## Apply for the Ministry's licence on IremboGov

The Ministry's licensing runs through IremboGov, the government's online services platform, in three applications. The [IremboGov licensing FAQ](https://support.irembo.gov.rw/en/support/solutions/articles/47001261990-frequently-asked-questions-about-health-facility-licensing-) says each is free, each has a processing time of 30 days, and you can apply with or without an IremboGov account. The [2022 instructions](https://www.moh.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Moh/Publications/Laws/new_law/MINISTERIAL_INSTRUCTIONS_2022_compressed.pdf) list a receipt of payment among the documents for a licence, so check whether a fee applies when you apply.

### Step 1: provisional authorization

[Provisional authorization](https://support.irembo.gov.rw/en/support/solutions/articles/47001261985-how-to-apply-for-provisional-authorization-for-registration-of-health-facilities) is the first step for anyone setting up a new facility. The facility must be registered in Rwanda and have a TIN, and you upload four documents:

- the managing director's criminal record certificate;
- the managing director's CV;
- a business plan for five years;
- the business registration certificate from the RDB.

The authorization is valid for one year. Use that year to fit out the premises and apply for the inspection.

### Step 2: the inspection

You [apply for the inspection](https://support.irembo.gov.rw/en/support/solutions/articles/47001261986-how-to-make-an-application-for-a-health-facility-inspection) once you hold the provisional authorization. The application asks for the facility's licence number and the requested attachments, and you are then notified of the inspection date.

The date depends on the calendar. The [2022 instructions](https://www.moh.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Moh/Publications/Laws/new_law/MINISTERIAL_INSTRUCTIONS_2022_compressed.pdf) hold licensing inspections in four rounds a year, in February, May, August and November, and the Ministry sends the inspection plan to the district and to insurers at least 30 days ahead. A joint team carries out the inspection, with at least five members present, among them at least two Ministry staff, people from the district hospital, the head of the local health centre and insurance representatives. If the team finds gaps, the re-inspection comes no sooner than 30 days and no later than 60 days after the first.

### Step 3: the licence to operate

After a favourable inspection recommendation, you apply for the [licence to operate a new health facility](https://support.irembo.gov.rw/en/support/solutions/articles/47001261980-how-to-apply-for-a-license-to-operate-a-new-health-facility). The application asks for the director's details and your staff list, with the director's documents attached. It also needs two arrangements in writing: a contract for incinerating the clinic's waste, and an ambulance contract, which the yellow card for a vehicle of your own can replace.

The [2022 instructions](https://www.moh.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Moh/Publications/Laws/new_law/MINISTERIAL_INSTRUCTIONS_2022_compressed.pdf) also list an occupation permit from the City of Kigali or the district, and a work permit for any foreign staff, and they say the licence is issued within 30 days of the inspection. Sort out the permits and the two arrangements before the inspection, so the licence application can go in the day the recommendation arrives.

## Prepare for the inspection

The IremboGov guides do not say what the inspectors check. [The 2022 instructions](https://www.moh.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Moh/Publications/Laws/new_law/MINISTERIAL_INSTRUCTIONS_2022_compressed.pdf) put the standards for each category in their annex, so read the part for your category. The 2017 service-package document adds what each type should offer, with the staff and equipment that go with it. Walk through the clinic as an inspector would, room by room, and switch on every piece of equipment yourself before they arrive.

Check the paperwork as well as the rooms. Every doctor's RMDC licence should be current on the day, and the incineration and ambulance arrangements should be signed.

## Set your fees from the national tariffs

Since 1 July 2025, [Ministerial Instruction No 20/0001 of 23/06/2025](https://moh.gov.rw/index.php?eID=dumpFile&f=133711&t=f&token=71831006d84ff4dd43bd157a91218f49151f46e4) has set the tariffs for health services in private facilities as well as public ones. Private facilities have five tariff categories, set by the patient's insurance and nationality, and the instruction says all health facilities and insurers must comply. The Ministry and the National Health Insurance Council audit compliance. The tariff amounts are not in the instruction itself, so get the current list for your facility level before you write the business plan.

## Keep the licence once you are open

The [2022 instructions](https://www.moh.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Moh/Publications/Laws/new_law/MINISTERIAL_INSTRUCTIONS_2022_compressed.pdf) make the licence to operate valid for five years, renewable, and ask for the renewal request three months before it expires. Renewal is also [an application on IremboGov](https://support.irembo.gov.rw/en/support/solutions/articles/47001261987-how-to-apply-for-the-renewal-of-the-operating-license). It needs your current licence number, a completed inspection, and four attachments: the director's documents (the guide lists a CV, practising licence, work permit, criminal record certificate, degree and certificate of services rendered), the incineration contract, the ambulance contract or yellow card, and an occupation permit. Under the 2022 instructions, secondary and tertiary facilities such as polyclinics and specialized clinics must also reach level 1 of accreditation by their first renewal and level 3 from their second, and they pay for the accreditation assessments.

The same instructions list what a licensed facility must keep doing. Among them:

- report its health data to the Rwanda Health Management Information System, immediately, weekly and monthly, and use HMIS software to record patient data, since renewal asks for proof of five years of HMIS reporting;
- tell the Ministry within 7 days about every new health professional it hires, full time or part time, and about key senior managers;
- send the Ministry the results of a patient satisfaction survey every 6 months;
- report suspected medicine quality problems, side effects and medication errors to the Rwanda Food and Drugs Authority straight away.

Changes need an application too, each made with a valid operating licence number: moving the clinic, changing its management, adding services, upgrading it to another category and closing it.
