Many clinics in Nigeria run on two timetables: the patients' and the grid's. A cloud EMR adds one more thing that has to stay up, the internet line, and it only earns its place if the clinic has planned for the hours when the power or the line is gone. This post is that plan, from the UPS under the desk to the paper register beside it.

## Why a cloud EMR stops when the router does

In a cloud EMR the patient records sit on servers outside the clinic, and the screen at the desk is a browser window onto them. For that window to work, two things have to be true inside the clinic: the router and the computer in use have power, and at least one internet line is up.

The router is the part people forget. The receptionist's laptop keeps running on its battery, but the router on the wall goes dark the moment the grid does, and the laptop has nothing to connect to. A clinic can spend on a good generator and still lose the EMR for the time it takes to start, if the router is not on a UPS.

Cloud has one real advantage in a place where the power is unreliable. Nothing is stored only on the machines in the clinic, so a surge that kills a hard disk, or a laptop stolen from the desk, does not take the records with it. Paper has its own advantage: it works in a blackout, by daylight or by torch, and it needs no line at all.

## Write down when the power and the line go

Before you buy a UPS or a second line, find out what you are planning for. For two weeks, have the receptionist note each time the power goes off and comes back, and each time the internet drops, with the time of day. The log shows how long the backup has to last, and whether the gaps fall in clinic hours or at night.

## Put the router on backup power first

A UPS covers the gap between the grid going off and the generator taking the load. An inverter with batteries, charged from the grid or from solar panels, covers the longer outages. If the clinic has a generator, the UPS is the part that saves the EMR. If it has none, the inverter has to carry the whole outage.

What goes on the backup, in this order:

1. The router, and the box the internet line comes in on.
2. The computer at the front desk, where patients are registered and billed.
3. The computers in the consulting rooms, unless the doctors use laptops that can run on their own batteries for a while.
4. The printer last. During an outage, the patient can take away a handwritten prescription and a receipt from the receipt book.

Then test it. Pick a quiet hour, switch off the mains, and check that the desk can still open a patient's record and the doctor can still write in one. If it fails at 11 in the morning with the waiting room empty, it will fail at 5 in the evening with the waiting room full.

## Keep a second internet line on a different network

One internet line is one fault away from a day on paper. The simplest second line is a mobile data hotspot: a phone or a mobile router with a SIM on a different network from the main connection, so that a fault on one network leaves the other working.

A few habits keep the second line useful:

- Keep the bundle loaded. A hotspot with an expired bundle is not a backup.
- Keep it charged and in a fixed place at the desk, so it is not in someone's pocket on the day it is needed.
- Check its data counter after its first full day as the clinic's only line, so you know what a day on it costs.
- Write down how to switch the desk computer over to it, and tape the note beside the screen.

## When the power and the line are both gone

Some minutes will be lost however well you plan. The clinic keeps seeing patients on paper for those minutes, and the EMR catches up afterwards.

- At the desk, a paper register with each patient's name, phone number, time of arrival and what they paid, backed by the receipt book.
- In the consulting room, the note on paper and a handwritten prescription for the patient to take away.
- If the line drops in the middle of a note, finish that visit on paper and check the record once the line is back, rather than assume that what was typed was saved.
- Before the desk closes, one named person types the outage visits into the EMR and matches the receipts against the bills.

Keep the register pages with the day's receipts. On the day the EMR and the register disagree, the paper is how you find out why.
