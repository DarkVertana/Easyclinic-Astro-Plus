faq:
  - question: Does a small clinic in Kenya have to register with the ODPC?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Businesses with a turnover under KES 5 million and fewer than ten employees are exempt from
      registering, but the exemption does not cover processing for health administration and patient care,
      so a clinic registers whatever its size ([regulation 13 and the Third
      Schedule](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-REGISTRATION-OF-DATA-CONTROLLERS-AND-DATA-PROCESSORS-REGULATIONS-2021.pdf)).
      The fee runs from KES 4,000 for the smallest tier to KES 40,000 for the largest, and the certificate
      lasts 24 months ([ODPC FAQs](https://www.odpc.go.ke/faqs/)).
  - question: Do I need the patient's consent to keep their medical record?
    answer: >-
      Yes, unless the service is provided for public health under the Public Health Act, because the
      Digital Health Act requires a healthcare provider to have consent to process sensitive personal data
      ([section
      31](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf)). Keep a
      record of it: the Data Protection Act puts the burden of proving consent on the clinic, and the
      patient can withdraw it at any time ([section
      32](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).
  - question: How fast must a clinic report a data breach in Kenya?
    answer: >-
      Within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, where someone unauthorised has accessed or acquired personal
      data and there is a real risk of harm to the patient ([section 43 of the Data Protection
      Act](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). The
      clinic then tells the affected patients in writing, and the ODPC takes reports through a [form on its
      website](https://www.odpc.go.ke/report-a-data-breach/).
  - question: What are the penalties under Kenya's Data Protection Act?
    answer: >-
      The Data Commissioner can fine a clinic up to KES 5 million or 1% of the previous financial year's
      annual turnover, whichever is lower, under [section
      63](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf) of the
      Act as the ODPC publishes it. A patient who suffers damage can also claim compensation, and an offence
      with no specific penalty carries a fine of up to KES 3 million or up to ten years in prison, or both
      ([sections 65 and
      73](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).
  - question: Can we keep patient records on software hosted outside Kenya?
    answer: >-
      Not without checking two rules first: sensitive personal data may be processed outside Kenya only
      with the patient's consent and confirmation of appropriate safeguards ([section
      49](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)), and data
      processed to provide primary or secondary health care must go through a server and data centre in
      Kenya, or keep at least one serving copy in a data centre in Kenya ([regulation
      26](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)).
      The Digital Health Act also says personal health information may only be shared with anyone outside
      Kenya for health tourism ([section
      47](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf)), so ask a
      lawyer how these apply and ask every vendor where its servers are.
  - question: Does using clinic software make my clinic compliant?
    answer: >-
      No, because the clinic stays the data controller, and the ODPC's registration guidance says a data
      controller is responsible for the compliance of the processors it contracts ([guidance
      note](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Registration-of-Data-Controllers-and-Data-Processors.pdf)).
      The Act also requires a written contract saying the vendor acts only on the clinic's instructions
      ([section
      42](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).
  - question: Does the Data Protection Act cover paper records?
    answer: >-
      Yes, the Act covers personal data recorded by hand when it forms part of a filing system, which means
      data readily found by reference to the person, such as patient files kept by name ([sections 2 and
      4](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).
  - question: Can a patient ask for a copy of their records?
    answer: >-
      Yes, and the clinic has seven days to give a patient access to their personal data, free of charge
      ([regulation
      9](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)).
      The Digital Health Act adds a right to examine and receive a copy of one's health information on a
      written request to the facility ([section
      36](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf)).

Kenya has had a [data protection law](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf) since November 2019, and since November 2023 a [second law](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf) that deals with health data directly. Both apply to a two-room clinic as much as to a hospital, and this guide sets out what each asks of one.

It quotes the texts published on the websites of the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) and the Digital Health Agency. It is not legal advice, and the texts may have been amended since, so check anything you act on with a Kenyan lawyer.

## The laws that cover patient data in Kenya

The [Data Protection Act, 2019](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf) is the main law. It was signed on 8 November 2019 and came into force on 25 November 2019, as the [front page of the Act](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf) records. It gives effect to Article 31(c) and (d) of the Constitution, and the ODPC's [guidance note on health data](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Processing-of-Health-Data.pdf) describes that constitutional right as including the right not to have information about your family or private affairs unnecessarily required or revealed.

Four things about the Act matter most to a clinic:

- **It covers paper as well as software.** Records kept by hand count when they form part of a filing system, such as patient files kept by name ([section 4](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).
- **It reaches beyond Kenya.** A business outside Kenya that processes the personal data of people located in Kenya is covered too ([section 4](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).
- **Health data is sensitive personal data.** The Act defines health data to include anything collected when a patient registers for or receives health services, and lists health status among the sensitive categories ([section 2](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).
- **Health data stays under a health provider's responsibility.** It may be processed only by or under the responsibility of a health care provider, or by someone bound by professional secrecy under law ([section 46](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).

Two sets of [regulations made in 2021](https://www.odpc.go.ke/data-protection-laws-kenya/) fill in the detail. The General Regulations cover consent, patients' rights, breaches and transfers abroad. The Registration Regulations set out who registers with the ODPC, and registration opened on 14 July 2022 ([ODPC FAQs](https://www.odpc.go.ke/faqs/)).

The [Digital Health Act, 2023](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf) came into force on 2 November 2023. It adds duties aimed at health data, including the healthcare provider's duty to get consent, and it says anyone processing personal data under it must also comply with the Data Protection Act ([section 61](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf)).

Older health laws touch patient privacy too. The ODPC's [health guidance](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Processing-of-Health-Data.pdf) lists them in its Annex A, including the Health Act 2017 and the HIV and AIDS Prevention and Control Act, which makes specific provisions on privacy and confidentiality for HIV test information.

## Who enforces the law

The ODPC oversees and enforces the Data Protection Act ([section 8](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)), and it is the ODPC that issues the certificate of registration (section 19). Your KMPDC registration and licence are a separate matter, covered in our guide to [KMPDC registration](/how-do-i-get-approval-from-the-kmpdc-in-kenya/).

The ODPC can investigate on a patient's complaint or on its own initiative ([section 9](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). A patient can complain orally or in writing, and the Act gives the ODPC 90 days to investigate and conclude a complaint ([section 56](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). It can also audit a clinic's processes and systems ([section 23](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). It publishes its [determinations](https://www.odpc.go.ke/determinations/) on its website, and the lists for 2024 onwards include several against hospitals and other health facilities.

## Registering with the ODPC

No one may act as a data controller or data processor in Kenya without registering with the Data Commissioner, subject to thresholds the Commissioner sets ([section 18](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). A clinic is the data controller for its patients' records, because it decides what is collected and why, and the ODPC's own examples of data controllers include hospitals ([FAQs](https://www.odpc.go.ke/faqs/)).

Small businesses with a turnover under KES 5 million and fewer than ten employees are exempt from registering ([regulation 13(2)](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-REGISTRATION-OF-DATA-CONTROLLERS-AND-DATA-PROCESSORS-REGULATIONS-2021.pdf)). The exemption does not cover processing for "health administration and provision of patient care", so a clinic registers whatever its size ([regulation 13(4) and the Third Schedule](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-REGISTRATION-OF-DATA-CONTROLLERS-AND-DATA-PROCESSORS-REGULATIONS-2021.pdf)). The ODPC's [health guidance](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Processing-of-Health-Data.pdf) says the same: every entity in the health sector must register.

What you receive is a certificate of registration. The ODPC's [public list](https://www.odpc.go.ke/registered-data-handlers/) calls registered controllers and processors "data handlers", and it includes many clinics and hospitals registered as data controllers. Anyone can check it, including your patients.

You apply online through the ODPC website ([regulation 17](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-REGISTRATION-OF-DATA-CONTROLLERS-AND-DATA-PROCESSORS-REGULATIONS-2021.pdf)), with the clinic's establishment documents, such as its business registration certificate, and a description of the patient data you process and why ([regulation 5](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-REGISTRATION-OF-DATA-CONTROLLERS-AND-DATA-PROCESSORS-REGULATIONS-2021.pdf)). If the Data Commissioner is satisfied, the certificate is issued within 14 days and lasts 24 months ([regulations 8 and 9](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-REGISTRATION-OF-DATA-CONTROLLERS-AND-DATA-PROCESSORS-REGULATIONS-2021.pdf)). The ODPC asks for a renewal application at least 30 days before the certificate expires, and for any change in your details within 14 days ([FAQs](https://www.odpc.go.ke/faqs/)).

| Tier | Who it covers | Registration (KES) | Renewal every 2 years (KES) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Micro and small | 1 to 50 employees and turnover up to KES 5 million | 4,000 | 2,000 |
| Medium | 51 to 99 employees and turnover of KES 5 million to 50 million | 16,000 | 9,000 |
| Large | More than 99 employees and turnover over KES 50 million | 40,000 | 25,000 |
| Charities and religious bodies | Any size | 4,000 | 2,000 |

The [ODPC FAQs](https://www.odpc.go.ke/faqs/) say an entity moves up a tier only when it meets both the staff figure and the turnover figure, so a clinic with 12 staff and a turnover of KES 8 million pays KES 4,000. You can pay by M-Pesa, card, bank transfer or cheque, and M-Pesa and card payments carry a KES 50 transaction fee (also from the [FAQs](https://www.odpc.go.ke/faqs/)).

Processing personal data without registering, or carrying on after the certificate has expired, is an offence ([regulation 18](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-REGISTRATION-OF-DATA-CONTROLLERS-AND-DATA-PROCESSORS-REGULATIONS-2021.pdf)).

## What the Act asks of every clinic

[Section 25 of the Act](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf) sets eight principles. Personal data must be:

1. processed in line with the patient's right to privacy;
2. processed "lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner";
3. collected for "explicit, specified and legitimate purposes", and not used later in ways that do not fit them;
4. "adequate, relevant, limited to what is necessary" for the purpose;
5. collected with a valid explanation whenever you ask about family or private affairs;
6. accurate and kept up to date, with wrong data corrected or erased without delay;
7. kept in a form that identifies the patient for no longer than the purpose needs;
8. not transferred outside Kenya, unless there is proof of adequate safeguards or the patient's consent.

At the desk, that means a registration form that asks only for what the clinic needs to treat and bill the patient. Before collecting data, the Act expects you to tell the patient, as far as practicable, that it is being collected, why, who else will receive it and what their rights are ([section 29](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). The General Regulations add a written data protection policy that you publish and keep up to date ([regulation 23](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)), and the ODPC's health guidance suggests building the privacy notice into patient registration.

The Act sets no fixed period for keeping health records, as the ODPC's [health guidance](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Processing-of-Health-Data.pdf) points out, so the clinic sets one it can justify in a retention schedule ([regulation 19](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)). The [guidance](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Processing-of-Health-Data.pdf) gives the example of a provider that keeps the records of patients who stop coming, or who have died, for seven years and then destroys or anonymises them.

## Consent and the other lawful grounds

The Data Protection Act does not make consent the only lawful ground. A clinic may also process personal data where it is necessary, for example to perform a contract with the patient or to protect someone's vital interests ([section 30](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).

For health data, the Digital Health Act goes further. A healthcare provider must make sure it has consent to process sensitive personal data, unless the service is provided for public health under the Public Health Act ([section 31](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf)). The patient can withdraw that consent at any time by telling the provider ([section 31(4)](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf)).

Consent to treatment and consent to the use of data are separate things. The ODPC's [health guidance](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Processing-of-Health-Data.pdf) says agreeing to a procedure does not by itself allow the clinic to share or store the data for other purposes, and that providers should keep a verifiable record of consent. The burden of proving consent is on the clinic ([section 32 of the Data Protection Act](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). Consent is not freely given if it is a non-negotiable part of the terms, or if several purposes are merged into one agreement ([regulation 4](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)).

Keep marketing apart from care. The General Regulations allow direct marketing only with personal data other than sensitive personal data ([regulation 15](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)), so a promotional message cannot be aimed at patients by their diagnosis. For a child, the parent or guardian gives consent ([section 33 of the Data Protection Act](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).

## Security and your software vendor

The Act asks every data controller to build data protection into its systems from the start ([section 41](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). The measures it asks you to consider include encryption and the ability to restore access to the data quickly after an incident ([section 41(4)](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). The Digital Health Act adds that a data controller must control who may use sensitive personal data, and must verify the identity of each person before they use it ([section 33](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf)).

In a clinic that comes down to habits: one login per person, each seeing only what their job needs, no shared password taped to the front-desk screen, and staff trained on their duties when they join.

Your EMR vendor is usually a data processor, because it handles the records on the clinic's behalf, and the ODPC's [health guidance](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Processing-of-Health-Data.pdf) names EHR vendors as an example. The Act requires a written contract saying the processor acts only on the clinic's instructions and is bound by the clinic's obligations ([section 42](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). The ODPC's [registration guidance](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Registration-of-Data-Controllers-and-Data-Processors.pdf) says the controller is responsible for the compliance of the processors it contracts, so choosing software does not hand the duty to the vendor.

The General Regulations list processing sensitive personal data among the operations that need a data protection impact assessment before processing begins ([regulation 49](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)), and the Act says the report is submitted 60 days before the processing ([section 31(5)](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). Ask a lawyer how that applies to a clinic that is already open.

## Sending patient data outside Kenya

Sensitive personal data may be processed outside Kenya only with the patient's consent and confirmation of appropriate safeguards ([section 49](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). The General Regulations go further for health care: data processed to provide primary or secondary health care to someone in Kenya must go through a server and data centre in Kenya, or at least one serving copy must be kept in a data centre in Kenya ([regulation 26](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)). The Digital Health Act says personal health information may only be shared with anyone outside Kenya for the purposes of health tourism ([section 47](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf)).

Ask a lawyer how these rules apply to cloud software hosted abroad. Ask every vendor where its servers are, and whether a copy of your data is kept in Kenya.

## Breaches and the 72-hour rule

Where personal data has been accessed or acquired by someone unauthorised and there is a real risk of harm to the patient, the clinic must notify the Data Commissioner without delay, within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach ([section 43](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). A late notice must give the reasons for the delay ([section 43(2)](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). The clinic then tells the affected patients in writing within a reasonably practical period, unless it cannot identify them ([section 43(1)(b)](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).

The General Regulations treat a breach as carrying a real risk of harm when it joins data that identifies the patient to data on a list in their Second Schedule. The list includes the diagnosis or treatment of sexually transmitted diseases, HIV, mental disorder, and substance abuse and addiction ([regulation 37 and the Second Schedule](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)).

If your software vendor finds the breach, it must tell you without delay and, where reasonably practicable, within 48 hours ([section 43(3) of the Act](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). The ODPC takes reports through a [form on its website](https://www.odpc.go.ke/report-a-data-breach/).

Under the Digital Health Act, losing health data or sharing sensitive personal data with an unauthorised party is a criminal offence. The general penalty is a fine of up to KES 1 million or up to 15 years in prison, or both, and where the offence involves sensitive personal data the penalties in section 73 of the Data Protection Act apply ([section 35](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf)).

Write the plan before you need it. Decide who judges whether a breach is notifiable, who files the report with the ODPC, who writes to the patients and who talks to the vendor. The Act also asks the clinic to keep a record of every breach and the action taken ([section 43(8)](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).

## What patients can ask for

A patient has the right to be told how their data will be used, to see it, to object to its use, and to have false or misleading data corrected or deleted ([section 26](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). The General Regulations give the clinic seven days to answer a request for access, free of charge, and 14 days to make a correction it agrees is needed ([regulations 9 and 10](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)). The Digital Health Act adds the right to examine and receive a copy of one's health information on a written request ([section 36](https://nhts.dha.go.ke/api/file-download/?filename=Digital+Health+Act+15+of+2023.pdf)).

A patient who thinks the clinic has broken the rules can [complain to the ODPC](https://www.odpc.go.ke/file-a-complaint/), so decide who at the clinic answers requests before a patient asks for their file at the desk.

## What happens when a clinic breaks the rules

The ODPC can serve an enforcement notice that tells the clinic what to fix, and it must give at least 21 days to do it ([section 58](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). Ignoring one is an offence, with a fine of up to KES 5 million or up to two years in prison, or both ([section 58(3)](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).

It can also issue a penalty notice. In the Act as the ODPC publishes it, the maximum is KES 5 million or, for a business, 1% of the previous financial year's annual turnover, whichever is lower ([section 63](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). The clinic can appeal to the High Court ([section 64](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)).

A patient who suffers damage, including distress, can claim compensation from the clinic ([section 65](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). An offence under the Act with no specific penalty carries a fine of up to KES 3 million or up to ten years in prison, or both ([section 73](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). Beyond the law, a patient whose diagnosis has leaked has little reason to come back.

## Privacy checklist for a clinic in Kenya

1. Register the clinic with the ODPC as a data controller, and put the renewal in the diary 30 days before the certificate's 24 months run out.
2. Publish a data protection policy, and give patients a privacy notice when they register.
3. Record each patient's consent to the use of their data, separately from consent to treatment.
4. Give every member of staff their own login, limited to what their job needs.
5. Encrypt stored records, and keep backups you have tested restoring.
6. Sign a written contract with each vendor that handles patient data, saying it acts only on your instructions.
7. Find out where each vendor stores your patients' data, and ask a lawyer whether that meets the rules on sending data outside Kenya.
8. Carry out a data protection impact assessment before you process health data in a new way.
9. Write a breach plan that gets a report to the ODPC within 72 hours.
10. Set a retention period for records and review it on a schedule.
11. Name the person who answers patients' requests, within seven days for access and 14 for corrections.
