Patients choose a clinic partly on how easy it is to get to. A clinic on the third floor with no lift, down a lane with nowhere to park, asks a lot of an elderly patient or a parent carrying a feverish child, however good the doctor is. This post is a way to choose a site in India that you can do yourself, with a notebook and a few visits, before you sign a lease. The registrations come after, and they are in our guide to [starting a clinic in India](/clinic-in-india/).

## Start with the patients your specialty serves

Before you look at a single shop front, write down who you expect to treat and what you will charge them. Then look for the area where those people already live or work.

- **General practice** needs a dense residential area, where people can walk in on the way to work or on the way home.
- **Paediatrics** belongs near family housing and schools, and not in an industrial area where few families live.
- **Gynaecology** suits a quieter residential street, where patients can come and go privately.
- **Orthopaedics and physiotherapy** need the ground floor and parking, because patients arrive on crutches or in pain.
- **Dermatology and aesthetic clinics** sit well in commercial or mixed-use areas, where patients are already out to shop and spend.
- **Psychiatry** needs a discreet entrance away from a crowded market.

Match your fees to the area as well. A consultation fee that suits a gated colony can keep patients away in a lower-income neighbourhood.

Look at where the area is going. New apartment blocks nearing possession and a metro line under construction both mean more people nearby in a few years. An area that is still filling up usually has lower rent and fewer clinics, but it also has fewer patients in the first year, so budget for a slow start.

## Count the clinics already there

Competition depends on who else is on the street, and nearby doctors can either take your patients or send them to you.

Having several doctors in your own specialty within walking distance means the patients are split before you open. Doctors in related specialties nearby can help you instead: a pain specialist next to a physiotherapist, or a paediatrician near a diagnostic lab, send patients to each other.

Being near a big hospital helps a specialist more than a general practitioner. Some patients leaving the hospital want a second opinion or follow-up closer to home. A general practitioner next door to a hospital is competing with its outpatient department.

To count the competition:

1. Walk every street within easy walking distance of the site and note each doctor's board, with the specialty and the hours.
2. Search online maps and doctor directories for the same streets, and read what patients say about the clinics you find.
3. Ask the nearest chemist which doctors' prescriptions come in most often.
4. Talk to one or two doctors already practising there. You will learn who refers to whom, and sometimes that a senior doctor is about to retire.

## Check the building before you look at the rent

A cheap unit that patients cannot reach costs more than it saves. Go through these before you negotiate.

**The floor and the lift.** The ground floor gets walk-ins and spares older patients the stairs. If the clinic has to be upstairs, take it only in a building whose lift works, and check it again on your second visit.

**Parking and transport.** Look for space for two-wheelers and cars outside, and for an auto stand or a bus or metro stop within a short walk. A patient who cannot stop outside may go to a clinic where they can.

**Being seen.** A board that can be read from the main road does some of the work that advertising would otherwise do. You pay for that position in rent, so decide what it is worth to you before you compare prices.

**The neighbours.** A clinic next to a mechanic's workshop or a garbage point looks unclean before the patient reaches the door. Stand outside at the busiest hour and see what the patient sees.

**Whether a clinic is allowed there.** Ask the municipal office whether a clinic can run from that address, and ask the owner for the building's approvals. The registrations you need are in the licensing checklist of the [India start-a-clinic guide](/clinic-in-india/).

## A metro or a smaller town

No list of best cities fits every specialty and every budget. What changes between them is how many patients live within reach, and what you pay in rent to be near them.

In a metro, more patients live within reach, and so do more doctors. The rent is higher too, and the [Mumbai cost guide](/how-much-does-it-cost-to-open-a-clinic-in-mumbai/) shows what a small clinic there costs line by line. In a smaller city the rent is usually lower and there may be fewer specialists, but check the doctors' boards yourself rather than trusting that a town is underserved.

In a small town or a village with few facilities, a general practice can fill a real gap. The patients may pay less and trained staff can be harder to find, so plan the running costs before you plan the building.

## Visit, compare, then sign

1. Write down your specialty, the age of the patients you expect, what they will come in for and the fee you plan to charge.
2. Shortlist at least two areas where those patients live or work.
3. Visit each site on a weekday and at the weekend, in the morning and in the evening, and count the people passing.
4. Count the clinics in your specialty and in related ones, and talk to the chemist.
5. Check the floor, the lift, the parking and whether the board can be seen from the road.
6. Ask the municipal office whether a clinic is allowed at the address.
7. Set the rent against the number of patients you can realistically see, using the set-up and running costs in the [India guide](/clinic-in-india/).
