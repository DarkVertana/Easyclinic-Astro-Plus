A test gets most of a clinic's attention at the start: choosing it and getting the sample to the lab. The risk sits at the other end, between the result arriving and someone acting on it. The patient safety network of the US Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality lists the lack of a reliable system for following up test results among the things that make a diagnostic error more likely in outpatient care ([AHRQ PSNet](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/diagnostic-errors)). This post sets out that system for a clinic without a lab manager.

## Where results go missing

Once the lab has done its work, a result can still go missing in four places before it reaches the patient:

- **The test that never comes back.** The sample is lost or rejected, or the report goes to the wrong clinic. A missing result makes no noise, so nobody notices until the patient asks.
- **The result nobody reads.** It arrives in an inbox or on paper while the doctor is seeing patients, and it is filed with the normal ones.
- **The abnormal result that is read but not acted on.** The doctor sees it and means to call the patient, and then the next patient walks in.
- **The patient who is never told.** "We'll call if there's a problem" means a lost result and a normal one sound the same to the patient: silence.

The first two are the clinic not knowing. The last two are the clinic knowing and nothing happening. Each needs its own check.

## Keep a list of tests waiting for a result

The test that never comes back is the easiest gap to close, because it only needs a list. For every test sent to the lab, write down the patient, the test, the lab, the date the sample went and the date the result is due. The list can be a register at the desk or the orders in your clinic software.

Check it every working day. Anything past its due date gets a call to the lab that day, and if the sample has to be taken again, the patient gets a call too. When a result arrives, mark it received, not closed. It is closed when someone has read it and the patient has been told.

## Decide who reads each result, and by when

Every result needs a named reader. Usually that is the doctor who ordered the test, and a rule such as "by the end of the next working day" stops results waiting for a quiet afternoon that never comes.

Name a cover for each doctor. When a doctor is on leave or off duty, their results go to a colleague who has agreed to read them, and the desk and the lab both know who that is. Without a named cover, a result for an absent doctor waits until the doctor returns.

Agree critical results with your lab. Some results cannot wait for the next working day. Ask the lab which values it treats as critical, and which number it calls when one comes through. Write the answers down and keep them at the desk, because the person who takes the call at six in the evening may not be the one who asked.

## What to do with an abnormal result

An abnormal result is closed when the doctor has decided what to do and the patient knows. Write the decision against the result: repeat the test, change the treatment, bring the patient in, refer them, or take no action and say why. A note that says "seen" does not tell a colleague what was decided.

Then reach the patient. Call first. If the call does not connect, try again later the same day, then send a message asking the patient to call the clinic, and note each attempt with the time. If a patient with a serious result cannot be reached, the doctor decides the next step and writes it down.

## Tell every patient, including the normal ones

If the clinic calls only when something is wrong, the patient whose result was lost hears nothing and takes it as good news. Tell every patient, even with a one-line message that the result was normal and nothing needs to change. Then silence always means something is missing, and patients learn to ask.

Say what the result means in the words the patient uses. A report on its own can alarm a patient about a value that does not matter, or reassure one about a value that does. On Easy Clinic's Premium and Enterprise plans the report can go to the patient on [WhatsApp](/features/whatsapp/), and a repeat test booked as an appointment gets an SMS or email reminder on every plan. The doctor's explanation still has to go with the report.

## Results from outside labs

Many clinics send some tests out, and those reports arrive as a PDF by email or WhatsApp, or as a printout the patient brings to the next visit. The day a report arrives, match it to the order on your pending list and attach it to the patient's record. Then give it to the doctor who ordered the test. A report the patient carries in has still not been read until a doctor reads it.

Be careful about typing values from an outside report into the record by hand. Each value typed is a chance to get it wrong, so keep the original report on the record beside anything typed, and have the doctor read the original.

In Easy Clinic, a report from an outside lab can be uploaded to the patient's record as a file, beside the results from your own lab. A direct feed from an outside lab's own system into Easy Clinic would be integration work, scoped as a project, so plan for a person to read every outside report.

## A daily routine for a small clinic

1. First thing in the morning, check the pending list and call the lab about anything overdue.
2. Through the day, each result is read by the doctor who ordered it, or by their cover.
3. For each abnormal result, the decision is written against it and every attempt to reach the patient is noted.
4. For each normal result, the patient gets a short message.
5. Once a week, look for any result marked received but not closed, and close it.
