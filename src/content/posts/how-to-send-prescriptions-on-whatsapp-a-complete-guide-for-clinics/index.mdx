To **send prescriptions on WhatsApp**, save the patient’s WhatsApp number at registration, complete the visit, and send the prescription as a PDF from your clinic software. This guide covers the steps, what else to send across the visit, what the front desk should say, privacy checks, and how to handle the situations that come up every week.

## Key points

Send prescriptions as a PDF from clinic software, not as photos from staff phones. Confirm the number and ask the patient once. Check for shared phones. Then add booking confirmations, reminders, invoices and lab reports on the same number.

## Why patients want their prescription on WhatsApp

Most patients already use WhatsApp every day. A prescription in the same app is easy to find and hard to lose. That is why more clinics now **send prescriptions on WhatsApp**.

- **It stays on their phone.** A paper copy gets lost. A PDF in a chat does not.
- **It works at any pharmacy.** The patient shows the screen or forwards the file.
- **Family can help.** Children or carers who manage medicines can read it too.
- **It is there at the next visit.** The patient can show what they were given, even if they forget the paper.

## Photo of the paper vs sending from clinic software

Many clinics send a photo of the handwritten prescription from a staff phone. It is quick, but it creates problems the clinic cannot see until something goes wrong.

| **Area** | **Photo from a staff phone** | **PDF from clinic software** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quality | Often blurred, tilted or cut off | Clear, printed layout |
| Sender | A staff member’s personal number | The clinic system |
| Record | No record of what was sent or when | Saved with the patient visit |
| Readability | Handwriting is just as hard to read | Typed text, logo and digital signature |
| Staff changes | Chats leave with the staff member | Nothing depends on one person’s phone |

## How to send a prescription on WhatsApp, step by step

1. **Save the right number at registration.** Ask which number is on WhatsApp. Confirm it by reading it back digit by digit.
2. **Ask once if they want it on WhatsApp.** Most will say yes. Note it on the record if they say no.
3. **Complete the visit as usual.** Complaints, diagnosis, medicines, advice and follow-up date.
4. **Click send on WhatsApp.** The prescription goes as a clear PDF with your clinic details.
5. **Ask the patient to check their phone.** Do this before they leave the room, so a wrong number is caught at once.

## What to send on WhatsApp across the whole visit

Once you **send prescriptions on WhatsApp**, the same number can carry the rest of the visit. Each message removes a phone call or a question at the desk. Reminders alone can help [reduce patient no-shows](/reduce-patient-no-shows/).

| **Moment** | **What to send** | **Why it helps** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When the appointment is booked | A confirmation with date, time and clinic name | The patient has the details in writing |
| Before the appointment | A reminder | Fewer missed appointments and follow-ups |
| Before the visit | A self-registration link, if your system supports it, in place of paper [patient intake forms](/patient-intake-forms/) | Details are filled in at home, not at the desk |
| After the doctor | The prescription PDF | Clear instructions the patient keeps |
| When tests are ready | The lab report | No second trip just to collect a report |
| After payment | The invoice | A record for the patient and for claims |

## What the front desk should say

Short, clear lines work best. Use these as a starting point and adjust them to your clinic.

- **At registration:** “Is this number on WhatsApp? We send your prescription and reminders there.”
- **If the phone is shared:** “Is this your own phone, or does someone else use it? We can send it to another number if you prefer.”
- **After the visit:** “Please check your WhatsApp. Your prescription should be there now.”
- **If they say no:** “No problem. We will print it for you as usual.”

## Privacy and consent checks

A prescription is personal health information. Treat WhatsApp like any other channel that carries it, and avoid common [patient data privacy mistakes](/patient-data-privacy-mistakes/).

- **Ask before you send.** Do not assume every patient wants messages.
- **Check shared phones.** Some patients use a family member’s phone. Ask before sending anything sensitive.
- **Send only what is needed.** Prescription, advice and reports the patient expects. Not internal notes.
- **Use one clinic channel.** Do not send from staff personal phones.
- **Follow local rules.** Check the data protection rules that apply to patient information in your country. See our guides to [patient data privacy laws in India](/patient-data-privacy-laws-in-india/) and [data privacy laws in Kenya](/data-privacy-laws-in-kenya/).

## Common situations and how to handle them

| **Situation** | **What to do** |
| --- | --- |
| The patient did not receive it | Check the saved number with them. Correct it and send again. |
| The patient has no WhatsApp | Print the prescription. Offer email if your system supports it. |
| An elderly patient needs a carer to see it | Ask the patient which number to use and note it on the record. |
| The patient asks for it again weeks later | Find the visit in the patient record and send it again. |
| The patient changed their number | Update the number on the record before sending anything. |

## How to tell if it is working

Track a few simple things for the first month. Count how many patients agree to WhatsApp. Note how often the front desk gets calls asking for prescriptions or appointment times. Watch whether more patients turn up for follow-ups after reminders start. You do not need exact numbers. A tally sheet at the desk is enough to see the trend.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it safe to send a prescription on WhatsApp?

It is safe to **send prescriptions on WhatsApp** with good practice. Confirm the patient’s number, ask if they want it on WhatsApp, and check if the phone is shared. Send from a clinic system rather than a staff phone, so there is a record of what was sent.

### Does the patient need an app to open it?

No. The prescription arrives as a PDF inside WhatsApp. It opens on any smartphone. The patient can also forward it to a family member or show it at a pharmacy.

### Can I still give a printed copy?

Yes. Many clinics print the prescription and send it on WhatsApp too. The printed copy is for the visit. The WhatsApp copy is for later.

### What else can a clinic send on WhatsApp?

Appointment confirmations, reminders, invoices and lab reports work well on WhatsApp. In Easy Clinic, patients can also receive a self-registration link, so they fill in their details before they reach the clinic.

### What if a patient gives the wrong number?

Ask them to check their phone before they leave the room. If nothing arrived, read the saved number back to them, correct it on the record and send again. This catches most mistakes on the same day.

### Should staff send prescriptions from their own phones?

No. Personal phones leave no clinic record, and chats leave when the staff member leaves. Send from a clinic system so every message is linked to the patient visit.
