Your appointment book can be full all month and the bank balance still tight at month end. A few patients say they will pay next time. A course of physiotherapy is paid for half. A small balance is written off because nobody wants to ask twice, and the drawer and the UPI app never quite match the bills.

The clinic has earned that money. Some of it has not arrived yet, and some of it will not arrive at all. This post is about that gap, written for clinics in India, with a short section for clinics where patients pay by mobile money.

## Billed is not banked

Billing is raising the right charge for the work done. Cash flow is that money reaching the bank, on time and in full. A clinic can do the first well and still fall short on the second, because collecting is a separate job with leaks of its own.

An owner who watches only the billing total sees a good month. The number to watch is the difference between what was billed and what was collected, and how old the unpaid part is.

## Where the gap opens

Each leak is small on its own.

- **Pay next time.** A patient leaves without paying, meaning to settle at the next visit. If the next visit is months away, or never comes, the balance ages until someone writes it off.
- **Packages paid in part.** A course of physiotherapy or dental work is paid in instalments, and nobody tracks which instalment is due at which session.
- **Takings that do not match.** Patients pay by cash, card, UPI or bank transfer, and sometimes split one bill across two of them. A UPI payment written down as cash leaves the drawer short against the system and the UPI app holding money no bill accounts for, and by morning nobody remembers which patient it was.
- **Treatment put off.** A family cannot pay the full amount up front, so a procedure the patient needs is postponed, and the income moves to a later month or to another clinic.
- **Balances written off by default.** A few hundred rupees here and there are dropped because chasing them feels awkward, without anyone deciding to drop them.

## Collect before the patient leaves

The easiest balance to collect is the one that never opens.

- Tell the patient the amount before the service. A patient who knows what the dressing and the test cost before they are done expects to pay for them.
- Bill at the desk as the consult ends, with the tests and procedures on the same bill, so the patient pays once.
- Take an advance when a procedure is booked, and record it against the bill.
- Make paying quick. A UPI QR code at the counter means a patient who came without cash can still pay before leaving.

In EasyClinic, the desk bills and takes payment from the appointment screen in one click, and the tests and procedures the doctor ordered arrive on the invoice as lines. On the Premium plan, online payments let a patient pay without coming to the counter, for example when a video consult is booked.

## Follow up what is still owed

Some balances will stay open, and each one needs someone responsible for it. Give one person the list of open balances every week, oldest first, with the patient's phone number and what the balance is for. A short message with the amount and a way to pay is easier on both sides than a call three months later.

Decide the write-off rule in advance. For example, a balance under an amount you choose is written off after two reminders. Then balances are written off on purpose, by someone you allowed, and not by forgetting.

In EasyClinic, the balance shows on the patient's bill as soon as it is opened, so the desk sees it when the patient comes back. Write-offs go through authorisation, so only someone the owner allows can write a balance off.

## Packages and big treatments

A package paid over several sessions is a loan from the clinic to the patient until the last instalment arrives. Treat it like one.

- Put the schedule in writing when the package is sold, with the amount due at each session.
- Agree up front what happens if the patient stops halfway.
- Collect each instalment before the session it pays for.
- If a patient needs longer to pay than the course of treatment lasts, write that agreement down separately and review it monthly.

In EasyClinic, package treatments come with Premium and Enterprise. A package can be billed in one visit or across several, with its payment spread over the visits. Professional does not include packages.

## Match each payment mode to the bills every evening

At closing, check each payment mode on its own.

- **Cash:** count the drawer against the cash recorded in the system.
- **UPI:** check the credits in the clinic's UPI app or bank account against the UPI payments recorded.
- **Card:** match the card machine's settlement slip against the card payments recorded.
- **Bank transfer:** check the bank statement against the transfers recorded.

A payment recorded under the wrong mode shows up as one mode short and another over by the same amount. That is a recording mistake. On the evening it happens it is easy to find, and at month end it may never be found.

In EasyClinic, the till is balanced across payment modes with an audit trail of every transaction, and the end-of-day report gives a snapshot of the day's cash transactions to count the drawer against. Access to the cash register is set by role. Tracing a gap between the drawer and the report is covered on the [revenue management page](/features/revenue-management/).

## If your patients pay by mobile money

The same rule holds where patients pay by mobile money, such as M-Pesa in Kenya or MTN Mobile Money in Uganda. The money lands on the clinic's phone or till statement, and the bill is at the desk. Match each transaction reference to a bill the same evening. Otherwise a payment a relative sent from another town sits on the statement as a credit nobody can explain.

In Kenya, a patient can pay at the EasyClinic desk by M-Pesa or in cash, and the payment is recorded on their bill, which shows what is still owed. The [Kenya demo page](/kenyademo/) covers EasyClinic for Kenyan clinics.

## The owner's four weekly numbers

1. What was collected in the week, as a share of what was billed.
2. Open balances, split by age: this week, this month, one to three months, and older.
3. Package balances still to be collected.
4. The cash gap for each counter, day by day.

These start from the bills and payments at the desk. In EasyClinic, the end-of-day financial reports run for the whole clinic, and reports export to Excel, where the weekly sheet can be built.

## What software does not fix

Software records the money and shows the gap. It does not have the conversation about money before a procedure, and it cannot make a treatment affordable for a patient who cannot pay.

EasyClinic also needs the internet to record a payment, because it does not run fully offline today. Keep a paper slip at the desk for the minutes the line is down, and enter those payments when it comes back.
