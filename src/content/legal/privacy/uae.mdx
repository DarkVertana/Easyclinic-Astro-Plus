## What this page adds

This page covers only what is different for clinics in the UAE. What EasyClinic collects and how it protects that data are in the [EasyClinic privacy policy](/privacy/), which applies here too. Where the two differ, [which document prevails for UAE clinics].

Two federal laws apply. The first is [Federal Law No. (2) of 2019](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1209) concerning the use of information and communications technology in health fields, called the ICT Health Law below. The second is [Federal Decree-Law No. (45) of 2021](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972) on the protection of personal data, the PDPL. The Dubai Health Authority (DHA) and the Department of Health in Abu Dhabi (DoH) add their own rules.

## Which law applies to patient data

The PDPL does not apply to personal health data that has its own legislation regulating its protection and processing ([Article 2(2)(e)](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972)). It also does not apply to companies in free zones that have their own data protection laws ([Article 2(2)(g)](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972)). The ICT Health Law, by contrast, applies to every use of information and communications technology in health in the UAE, including the free zones ([Article 2](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1209)).

[Which parts of the PDPL apply to EasyClinic and to UAE clinics alongside the ICT Health Law].

Where the PDPL does apply, data about a person's health, including information about the healthcare they received that reveals their health status, is sensitive personal data ([Article 1](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972)). It may be processed without consent where that is needed for diagnosis or treatment under UAE law ([Article 4(4)](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972)).

## Health data stays in the UAE

Health data and information about health services given in the UAE may not be stored, processed, generated or moved outside the country, except in cases set by a resolution of the health authority in coordination with the Ministry ([Article 13](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1209)). Breaking Article 13 carries a fine of AED 500,000 to AED 700,000 ([Article 24](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1209)).

The DHA's [health data protection policy](https://dha.gov.ae/uploads/082022/Health%20Data%20Protection%20and%20Confidentiality%20Policy_EN2022810559.pdf) names the instrument that sets the exempt cases as Decision No. 51 of 2021 (sections 4.2.3 and 4.15.1). In Dubai, that policy says protected health information should not be transferred outside the UAE except within those exemptions, and that DHA approval must be granted under its Health Information Assets Classification Policy (section 4.15.1). In Abu Dhabi, the DoH says health data that relates to health services provided in the country may not be stored or transferred outside the UAE ([ADHICS FAQ](https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/Feature/Aamen/ADHICS-FAQ.ashx)).

## Confidentiality and who may see records

Anyone using technology in health must keep health data confidential and protect it from unauthorised change or deletion. They must also keep it available to those authorised to see it ([Article 4](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1209)).

Whoever handles information about patients must keep it confidential and use it only for health purposes unless the patient approves in writing ([Article 16](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1209)). The law lists five exceptions:

- A health insurer, or anyone funding the care, reviewing or verifying what it must pay for.
- Scientific and clinical research that does not reveal who the patients are.
- Preventive or curative public health measures, or protecting the patient or someone in contact with them.
- A request from the competent judicial authorities.
- A request from the health authority for monitoring and inspection, or to protect public health.

## How long records are kept

Health data must be kept for at least 25 years from the date of the patient's last health procedure ([Article 20](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1209)). In Abu Dhabi, the DoH repeats the 25-year minimum for healthcare entities in its [Data Storage and Retention Standard](https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/FB7CE4A318974DE2B955F3FC4E3D7A72.ashx) (section 3.2.2.1).

## Dubai and Abu Dhabi

The DHA's [Policy for Data and Health Information Protection and Confidentiality](https://dha.gov.ae/uploads/082022/Health%20Data%20Protection%20and%20Confidentiality%20Policy_EN2022810559.pdf), issued in August 2022, applies to every healthcare entity under DHA jurisdiction. It also covers the information systems those entities buy or use, and the suppliers and vendors who access their health information (sections 3.4 and 3.5).

The DoH's Abu Dhabi Healthcare Information and Cyber Security Standard (ADHICS) is mandated for all healthcare entities in Abu Dhabi. Any entity that handles health information from Abu Dhabi must meet the controls that apply to it, and the DoH says that includes a small clinic of four staff with one doctor. Meeting a minimum set of ADHICS controls is a condition of connecting to Malaffi, the Abu Dhabi health information exchange ([ADHICS FAQ](https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/Feature/Aamen/ADHICS-FAQ.ashx)).

## Duties under the personal data law

Where the PDPL applies, a controller must report a personal data breach to the UAE Data Bureau set up by Federal Decree-Law No. 44 of 2021, within the period set by the PDPL's Executive Regulations ([Article 9(1)](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972)). A processor that becomes aware of a breach must tell the controller as soon as it does ([Article 9(3)](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972)). A controller and a processor must appoint a data protection officer in the cases Article 10 lists, which include processing a large volume of sensitive personal data ([Article 10(1)](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972)). Personal data may be transferred outside the UAE only in the cases Articles 22 and 23 allow, and for health data Article 13 of the ICT Health Law also applies.
