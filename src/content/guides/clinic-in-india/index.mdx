This guide follows the order you will meet things in: the area and the kind of clinic, then the plan and the money, then the premises and the people, and finally the paperwork. The licensing steps are gathered into one numbered checklist further down, next to the fees this guide quotes in rupees. Fees vary by state and they change, so check each one with the office that issues it before you budget.

## Why open a clinic in India

Most outpatient care in India is already private. According to the National Health Policy 2017, private providers deliver about 70% of it, and long waits at public hospitals are a large part of the reason. With about 0.9 doctors for every 1,000 people ([WHO, 2022](https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/indicators/indicator-details/GHO/medical-doctors-%28per-10-000-population%29)), the gaps run in both directions: rural areas lack facilities, while the cities carry a growing load of lifestyle diseases such as diabetes that need regular outpatient follow-up.

Patients also stay with a clinic they trust. A small clinic can offer what a large hospital struggles to: a doctor they know, shorter waits, follow-ups that feel personal and costs they can see up front. That loyalty turns into repeat visits and word-of-mouth referrals, which is how a clinic's appointment book fills.

The hard parts are the paperwork and the money you spend before the first patient. Plan both carefully, and look at a bank loan or an investor early if your savings will not cover the set-up.

## Start with a market survey

Before you sign a lease, find out who the area already serves and who it misses. Public hospitals take the advanced cases, and community health centres cover much of rural India. Outpatient care is where private clinics lead, and it is where you will compete.

Demand differs a lot from place to place. Maternal care is badly needed in rural Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, where facilities are scarce and midwives are overstretched. In [Mumbai](/how-much-does-it-cost-to-open-a-clinic-in-mumbai/), diabetes care is growing, and in Delhi so is the demand for diagnostics. In the cities, competition is intense, which leaves room for a speciality nobody nearby offers.

Use Ministry of Health data where it covers your district, then do your own survey. Visit the area several times, on different days and at different hours, and count the clinics on the street. A local survey of patients will tell you more about what they are missing than any national report.

## Decide what kind of clinic you are opening

The type of clinic decides who you hire and what you buy, so settle it first. There are four common options:

- A consultation clinic offers outpatient care.
- A dispensary adds medicines to the consultation.
- A short-stay clinic keeps patients under observation for up to 8 hours.
- A diagnostic clinic focuses on tests, such as X-rays.

Open in the field you trained in, or partner with a specialist to cover the gap. Then match the type to what the survey showed the area needs.

Start small. A small clinic costs ₹5 lakh to ₹15 lakh to set up, and that lets you test the location before you commit more money to it.

## Write a business plan

A business plan makes you answer the questions a bank or a partner will ask anyway. Keep it to five parts:

- Purpose: who the clinic is for, whether that is underserved patients in a rural area or a speciality such as cardiology.
- Market: local demand and the competition, and what will make patients choose you.
- Money: set-up costs of ₹5 lakh to ₹20 lakh and running costs of ₹2 lakh to ₹5 lakh a month, set against revenue from a patient count you can defend.
- Operations: how a patient moves from check-in to billing, and how the records are kept.
- Risks: regulatory delays are the usual one, and a consultant who knows your state's offices can shorten them.

## Choose the premises

The location decides your patient numbers more than anything else you control. Before you sign, check four things:

- Transport links and parking, away from congested lanes that patients and staff will struggle to reach.
- Nearby homes or offices, so the people you plan to treat live or work within easy reach.
- Footfall and competition. Visit the site several times before you commit.
- Room for the layout below, within your state's building codes.

## Fit out the clinic

Plan the rooms around privacy and patient flow:

- A consultation room of at least 100 sq ft, with a door that closes.
- A waiting area where patients are comfortable.
- Storage for medicines and supplies.
- A procedure room, if you will do procedures.

Then build for safety and hygiene. Put in fire exits and waste bins, and apply for the fire clearance certificate. Keep surfaces easy to clean and the rooms well ventilated, because, where the Clinical Establishments Act applies, a clinic must meet minimum standards for its facilities and services (section 12). Air conditioning and bright lighting make the rooms usable in summer and for examinations.

Budget ₹2 lakh to ₹8 lakh for equipment. Start with the basics, such as stethoscopes and blood pressure monitors, and decide how you will keep records, on paper or in software, before you open.

## Hire registered staff

Every clinician you hire must be qualified and registered. Doctors register with the State Medical Council. Nurses register with the State Nursing Council, and pharmacists, if you dispense, with the State Pharmacy Council. Check each registration before the offer letter goes out.

Salaries come out of the running budget every month. Budget ₹50,000 to ₹1.5 lakh a month for a doctor and ₹20,000 to ₹50,000 a month for a nurse. Offer health insurance if you can, because a good nurse who leaves in the first year is expensive to replace.

Train the team before you open, on the records system and on emergency protocols, and repeat the sessions as rules and routines change. As patient numbers grow, plan to build the team to 4 to 6 people.

## Register and license the clinic

The paperwork is where most of the waiting happens. Start it as soon as you have the premises.

### Choose the business structure

A proprietorship is the simplest structure. A private limited company costs ₹10,000 to ₹50,000 to register and separates your personal liability from the clinic's. Choose based on how much risk you carry and how far you plan to grow.

### Register the clinic with the state

The Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010 applies only where a state has adopted it. The [Act's site](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/) lists Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Mizoram, Rajasthan, Sikkim, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and every union territory except Delhi. Elsewhere, ask the state health department which law registers clinics.

Where the Act applies, each state sets its own fee: the [fee table on the Act's site](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-06/46311.pdf) lists ₹100 for a clinic's provisional registration and ₹500 for permanent registration in Chandigarh, and ₹500 for a clinic in Jharkhand.

### Check every doctor's council registration

Doctors need State Medical Council registration to practise. The [National Medical Commission](https://www.nmc.org.in/) regulates medical practice nationally.

### Get the local licences

Most clinics also need a fire safety certificate (₹10,000 to ₹30,000) and a biomedical waste licence (₹20,000 to ₹50,000). A Shop Act licence (₹5,000 to ₹15,000) is needed where your area requires one.

### Work out whether you need GST

You need GST registration when you make taxable supplies, such as medicines sold over a pharmacy counter or cosmetic procedures, and your aggregate turnover for the year crosses ₹20 lakh, or ₹10 lakh in some states (section 22 of the CGST Act). A clinic that supplies only exempt health care services does not have to register (section 23(1)(a)). If you do need it, budget ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 to get it done, and read the rules on the [CBIC GST site](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/).

From planning to the first patient usually takes two to four months. How quickly the licences come through and how ready the premises are decide where in that range you land.

## Licensing checklist

1. Choose the business structure. A proprietorship is simplest; a private limited company costs ₹10,000 to ₹50,000 to register.
2. Check that every doctor holds State Medical Council registration. The [National Medical Commission](https://www.nmc.org.in/) is the national regulator; our [NMC registration guide](/how-to-get-approval-from-the-medical-practitioners-and-dentists-council-in-india-nmc-dci/) covers the steps for doctors and dentists.
3. Check that nurses are registered with the State Nursing Council and pharmacists with the State Pharmacy Council.
4. Lay out the premises to your state's building codes, with a consultation room of at least 100 sq ft and fire exits.
5. Find out which law registers clinics in your state and register under it: the Clinical Establishments Act in the 19 states and union territories that have adopted it, or the law your state health department names. Pay the fee your state sets (see the fee table linked above).
6. Get the fire safety certificate, at ₹10,000 to ₹30,000.
7. Get the biomedical waste licence, at ₹20,000 to ₹50,000, and have the waste bins in place before the first patient.
8. Get a Shop Act licence if your area requires one, at ₹5,000 to ₹15,000.
9. Work out whether you need GST registration: it applies when you make taxable supplies, such as pharmacy counter sales, and turnover crosses ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in some states); a clinic supplying only exempt health care services does not register. The [CBIC GST site](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/) has the rules.
10. Put every renewal in the diary, including each doctor's council registration on the cycle their State Medical Council sets, and keep the certificates filed where an auditor can find them.

## Market the clinic

Marketing is easiest to start before you open and hardest to start once patient flow has slowed. What each channel costs:

- A website costs ₹50,000 to ₹1.5 lakh, and Facebook ads ₹10,000 to ₹30,000 a month.
- Flyers and tie-ups with local pharmacies cost ₹20,000 to ₹50,000, and they reach the families who live around the clinic.
- A logo and signage cost ₹30,000 to ₹80,000, so people can find you from the road.
- Health camps in the neighbourhood bring in the first patients and introduce you to the community.

The waiting room is marketing too. Comfortable seating and a receptionist who greets people by name bring patients back.

## Billing and payments

- Put your payment policy where patients can see it, and take UPI as well as cash, because patients in towns and villages pay differently.
- Keep a record of every bill, so disputes are rare and your GST records hold up if you are registered.
- Decide in advance who gets a discount or a waiver, such as low-income patients, so the desk is not deciding case by case.
- Check income against expenses every month, and find out where any gap comes from.

## Patient communication and follow-up

- Give patients printed information on common conditions such as hypertension, so they can manage their own health between visits.
- Call or send an SMS reminder when a follow-up is due, especially for patients with chronic diseases.
- Speak your patients' language. In Chennai that may be Tamil and in Lucknow Hindi, and hearing it at the desk makes a difference.
- Ask for feedback with a short survey or a suggestion box, and act on what comes back.

National health days are a good reason to run an awareness session, and they build the clinic's name in the area.

## Running the clinic once you are open

The licence is the start. What keeps a clinic open is steady attention:

- Track patient satisfaction and revenue every month, and look for the numbers that are moving the wrong way.
- Keep up with regulatory changes through NMC workshops and updates.
- Train staff every year on new protocols.
- Offer teleconsultations for follow-ups where they suit the patient. Telemedicine is permitted under national guidelines, and it reaches rural patients who cannot travel.

Watch the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission ([ABDM](https://abdm.gov.in/)), the national programme for digital health records. As it grows, clinics that keep digital records should find it easier to work with insurers, labs, pharmacies and referring doctors.

## Where new clinics go wrong

Many first-time owners spend heavily on interiors and equipment and overlook the day-to-day running. The same mistakes come up again and again:

1. Choosing a location without studying patient footfall.
2. Underestimating monthly running costs, salaries and consumables above all.
3. Ignoring marketing until patient flow becomes uneven.
4. Putting off digital records, and then moving a busy clinic off paper later.
5. Missing compliance renewals, and not being ready for an audit.

Clinical skill gets the clinic open. Running it as a business, with a plan and a monthly budget, is what keeps it open. A well-run clinic typically breaks even in 12 to 18 months, and avoiding these mistakes early saves the money you would otherwise spend fixing them.
