If you are a doctor or dentist planning your own clinic in Kenya, your registration with the Kenya Medical Practitioners and Dentists Council (KMPDC) comes before the lease and the software, because you cannot legally work at a private clinic without it. This guide covers that registration: who qualifies, the six steps, the documents, and the extra checks for doctors trained outside the East African Community (EAC).

## What KMPDC registration means for your clinic

The KMPDC regulates the practice of medicine and dentistry in Kenya. It registers practitioners, and it also registers and licenses health institutions, so you and your clinic answer to the same council.

Without KMPDC approval you cannot legally work at a private clinic or any other health facility in Kenya. Practising without a licence is an offence, and the council regularly publishes lists of unlicensed practitioners. Being on one of those lists can bring penalties or the closure of the clinic.

Patients care about it too. Registration tells them the doctor in front of them is qualified, and a new clinic with no reputation yet needs every bit of that trust.

## Who can register with the KMPDC?

The route in this guide is written for Kenyan citizens and citizens of other EAC countries, such as Uganda or Tanzania. Practitioners trained outside the EAC have extra requirements, covered further down. Before you apply, you need:

- A medical or dental degree the KMPDC recognises: an MBChB for doctors or a Bachelor of Dental Surgery (BDS) for dentists.
- A completed internship in Kenya or an EAC country, with the internship qualifying exams passed.
- A pass in the KMPDC's pre-registration exam, which tests whether you are ready to practise safely.
- Good moral standing, with no history of professional misconduct.

## How KMPDC approval works, step by step

There are six steps. The first three are about your training, and the last three are the application itself.

### 1. Check your eligibility

Confirm that the KMPDC recognises your degree before you spend money on anything else. If it does not, you will have extra steps to take before you qualify, and it is far better to find that out before you have signed for premises.

### 2. Finish your internship

Complete a recognised internship in Kenya or another EAC country and pass the internship qualifying exams. The KMPDC schedules these exams and announces them on its website.

### 3. Pass the pre-registration exam

This is the council's own test of whether you can practise safely. The schedules are posted on the KMPDC website, often well in advance, so build them into your plan early.

### 4. Gather your documents

Collect certified copies of everything on the document list below. Missing or incorrect documents can delay approval.

### 5. Pay the fee and submit

Download the application form from [kmpdc.go.ke](https://kmpdc.go.ke/) or collect it from the council's office in Nairobi. Pay the prescribed fee; the current amount and how to pay it are on the council's website. Then submit the form with your documents, either online through the [KMPDC Online Services Portal](https://osp.kmpdc.go.ke) or in person at the office.

### 6. Wait for the assessment

The council reviews your application. It may ask you to sit additional exams, and if you trained abroad it may require them. Once the application is approved, you receive a certificate of registration. To practise you also need a current practice licence, which the KMPDC issues every year through its [Online Services Portal](https://osp.kmpdc.go.ke).

## Extra steps if you trained outside the East African Community

If your degree is from outside the EAC, the council needs more evidence before it will assess you:

- Your medical school must be listed in the [World Directory of Medical Schools (WDOMS)](https://www.wdoms.org).
- You provide proof of registration from your home country or the country where you trained.
- Your medical diploma, transcript and certificate of good standing are verified with the Educational Commission for Foreign Medical Graduates (ECFMG) through the Electronic Portfolio of International Credentials (EPIC).
- You may have to sit KMPDC assessment exams to show your competence.

If this is you, start with the WDOMS listing and the EPIC verification early, because the ECFMG verification form is one of the documents your application needs.

## Documents the KMPDC asks for

Have certified copies of these ready before you open the application form:

- Your final medical diploma: the MBChB, BDS or equivalent degree certificate.
- Your final medical school transcript.
- Your internship completion certificate.
- A certificate of good standing from your current or previous jurisdiction, issued within the last six months, confirming you have no disciplinary issues.
- Letters of reference on your professional conduct and competence.
- A completed peer review data form setting out your professional experience.
- If you trained abroad, the ECFMG verification form from EPIC.

Watch the six-month limit on the certificate of good standing. If you request it first and the rest of the pile takes longer than planned, it can expire before you submit.

## Licensing checklist

Work through these in order. Where the council has its own page for a step, the link goes there.

1. Confirm that the [KMPDC](https://kmpdc.go.ke/) recognises your MBChB or BDS. If you trained outside the EAC, check that your medical school is listed in [WDOMS](https://www.wdoms.org).
2. Complete your internship in Kenya or an EAC country and pass the internship qualifying exams. Exam dates are on [kmpdc.go.ke](https://kmpdc.go.ke/).
3. Pass the KMPDC pre-registration exam.
4. If you trained outside the EAC, get proof of registration from your home country and verify your diploma, transcript and certificate of good standing with the ECFMG through EPIC.
5. Collect certified copies of every document on the list above, with a certificate of good standing issued within the last six months.
6. Download the application form from [kmpdc.go.ke](https://kmpdc.go.ke/) and pay the prescribed fee through the channels the council lists there.
7. Submit the form and documents through the [KMPDC Online Services Portal](https://osp.kmpdc.go.ke) or at the council's office in Nairobi, never through a third party.
8. Sit any assessment exam the council asks for, then collect your certificate of registration.
9. Register and license the clinic itself, since the KMPDC regulates health institutions as well as practitioners. The steps for the premises are in our [guide to starting a clinic in Kenya](/the-ultimate-guide-to-starting-a-clinic-in-kenya/).

## What slows an application down

### Leaving the degree check until last

If the KMPDC does not recognise your degree, everything else waits while you sort that out. Check it first, before the internship paperwork and well before you look at premises.

### Treating the exams as a formality

The KMPDC sets the dates for the internship qualifying exams and the pre-registration exam, often well in advance. Put them in your plan as soon as they are announced and study for both.

### Sending papers that are incomplete or uncertified

Double-check that every document is complete and certified. Missing or incorrect documents delay approval, and if you have already signed a lease, every week of delay is rent on a room you cannot use.

### Using an agent

Apply to the KMPDC directly, online or at its office. Going through a third party adds the risk of fraud and delays.

### Working from old information

The KMPDC posts exam schedules and fee changes on [its website](https://kmpdc.go.ke/). Check it before each step rather than relying on a guide, this one included, because the requirements can change too.
