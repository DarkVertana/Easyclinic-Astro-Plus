If you are a doctor or dentist planning your own clinic in India, your registration comes before the lease and the software, because you cannot legally see patients without it. This guide covers who registers you, the steps for doctors and for dentists, the documents, the approvals the clinic needs on top, and the rules that apply once you are open. For the local licences and what they cost in rupees, read our [guide to starting a clinic in India](/clinic-in-india/).

One point on names first. There is no "Medical Practitioners and Dentists Council" in India; that name belongs to Kenya's regulator. Here doctors and dentists answer to different bodies, and the dentists' national regulator was replaced in March 2026.

## What registration means for your clinic

The code of ethics the NMC publishes, the [Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002](https://www.nmc.org.in/page/rules-regulations-rules-regulations-of-erstwhile-mci-code-of-medical-ethics-regulations-2002), is blunt about it: only a doctor with a recognised qualification who is registered with a medical council may practise modern medicine. Practising without registration can lead to penalties or the closure of your clinic.

For doctors, two bodies are involved. The State Medical Council of the state where you practise registers you. The National Medical Commission (NMC), through its Ethics and Medical Registration Board, keeps the [National Medical Register](https://www.nmc.org.in/page/about-nmc-autonomous-boards-ethics-medical-registration-board), which holds every entry from the state registers.

For dentists, the State Dental Council registers you. Above it sits the National Dental Commission, which replaced the Dental Council of India (DCI) on 19 March 2026, according to the [notice on the commission's website](https://dciindia.gov.in/).

Registration also tells patients who you are. The NMC runs an [Indian Medical Register search](https://www.nmc.org.in/information-desk/indian-medical-register) of doctors registered with the State Medical Councils, and a new clinic with no reputation yet needs every bit of that trust.

## Who can register

Before you apply, check four things:

- A recognised degree: an MBBS for doctors or a BDS for dentists, from an institution the regulator recognises.
- A completed internship, for doctors. You do a one-year internship after the final MBBS exam, and the NMC's [internship regulations](https://www.nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/Lpv9Va5WsAzpQmISmwAMWmsefK2nOEroAdNZkVJC.pdf) rule out permanent registration without it.
- Good standing, with no history of professional misconduct.
- Your citizenship and where you trained. Under the NMC's [2023 registration regulations](https://www.nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/yU2SAi2t2zlfbXoTSXbrFzYcG8eRMTBsriQyaGPA.pdf), a foreign citizen who is registered as a doctor in their own country can get temporary registration for a set purpose, such as a postgraduate course or voluntary clinical service, and only when someone in India sponsors them. Indian citizens who qualified abroad follow the NMC's Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate Regulations, 2021.

## How doctors register, step by step

The State Medical Council handles both registrations. The NMC's part is the national register your entry ends up on.

### 1. Get provisional registration for the internship

Once you pass the final MBBS exam, apply to the State Medical Council for provisional registration. Under the NMC's [Registration of Medical Practitioners and Licence to Practice Medicine Regulations, 2023](https://www.nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/yU2SAi2t2zlfbXoTSXbrFzYcG8eRMTBsriQyaGPA.pdf), provisional registration is granted for internship training. It is not a licence to open your own clinic.

### 2. Complete the internship

Complete your one-year internship. The NMC's [internship regulations](https://www.nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/Lpv9Va5WsAzpQmISmwAMWmsefK2nOEroAdNZkVJC.pdf) say no medical graduate is eligible for permanent registration without it, so keep the completion certificate somewhere safe.

### 3. Apply for permanent registration with the State Medical Council

Apply for permanent registration to the council of the state where the clinic will be, such as the Maharashtra Medical Council. This is the registration that lets you practise on your own, and the [2023 regulations](https://www.nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/yU2SAi2t2zlfbXoTSXbrFzYcG8eRMTBsriQyaGPA.pdf) call it a licence to practise medicine. They set out an online route for the application. You pick the state on a portal run by the NMC's Ethics and Medical Registration Board, and the portal sends the application to that state's council. Once the council approves it, your name appears on the State Medical Register and on the National Medical Register.

The same regulations say the existing system carries on until they are fully in force. The [NMC's home page](https://www.nmc.org.in/) links to the National Medical Register portal, so ask your council which route it uses before you start.

## How dentists register now the DCI has been replaced

The Dental Council of India was dissolved on 19 March 2026, and the National Dental Commission took over from the same day, according to the [notice on the commission's website](https://dciindia.gov.in/). The commission still uses dciindia.gov.in while its own website is built, and it may use the DCI's name in digital communication during the changeover.

Dentists register with their State Dental Council, such as the Karnataka State Dental Council. The commission publishes a [list of every State Dental Council](https://dciindia.gov.in/ListofSDC.aspx) with each registrar's email and phone number, which is the place to start. Its [FAQ on moving a registration between states](https://dciindia.gov.in/TransferRegistration/NOC%20FAQ%20FINAL.pdf) confirms that when you move, the new state's Dental Council makes the final decision on registering you.

## Documents to gather

Have certified copies of these ready before you open the application form. Your council's own list is the one that counts, so check it first.

- Your MBBS or BDS degree certificate.
- Your internship completion certificate, if you are a doctor.
- Marksheets and transcripts from your medical or dental college.
- Identity proof: a government-issued photo ID, such as an Aadhaar card or passport.
- Proof of good standing: a certificate confirming no professional misconduct.
- The receipt for the registration fee.

## Approvals the clinic needs as well

Your registration covers you. The clinic needs approvals of its own. The business structure, the Shop Act, GST and what each licence costs are in our [guide to starting a clinic in India](/clinic-in-india/).

### Clinical establishment registration

The Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010 is a central law, but it applies where a state has adopted it. The Ministry of Health's [Clinical Establishments Act site](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/) says 19 states and union territories have adopted it: Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Mizoram, Rajasthan, Sikkim, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and every union territory except Delhi. If your clinic is in one of them, register it under the Act, following the rules your state has published, which the same site carries. If it is not, ask your state health department which law registers clinics there. Each state has its own rules either way.

### Bio-medical waste

Get the State Pollution Control Board's approval for the bio-medical waste the clinic produces, such as sharps and soiled linen. Depending on your bed count, you may also need the board's consent to establish and consent to operate.

### The building

If you own the building, you need the occupancy certificate and the completion certificate. Check too whether your state's fire rules require a fire NOC for it. If you rent, make sure the owner holds these approvals before you sign, and keep copies with your lease.

### X-ray machines and medicines

If the clinic has an X-ray machine, register it with the [Atomic Energy Regulatory Board](https://www.aerb.gov.in/english/). If you sell medicines, you need a licence under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.

## Rules to follow once you are registered

### The code of ethics

Doctors follow the 2002 [code of medical ethics](https://www.nmc.org.in/page/rules-regulations-rules-regulations-of-erstwhile-mci-code-of-medical-ethics-regulations-2002), which covers patient confidentiality and the quality of care among much else. Dentists have their own code, so check the National Dental Commission's site for the current version.

### Records

Keep records of the treatment you give. The 2002 code also asks every doctor to keep a register of the medical certificates they issue, with a copy of each certificate.

### What to display

The same code asks every doctor to display the registration number the State Medical Council gave them in four places: the clinic, prescriptions, certificates and the money receipts given to patients. Put the clinical establishment registration certificate on the wall as well.

### Staff

Every doctor you hire needs State Medical Council registration, and nurses and pharmacists need registration with their own nursing or pharmacy council. Check each one before the offer letter goes out. For doctors, the [Indian Medical Register search](https://www.nmc.org.in/information-desk/indian-medical-register) is a quick first check, though the NMC notes on that page that the data is still being updated. Run a background check on everyone you hire.

## Licensing checklist

Work through these in order. Where a regulator has its own page for a step, the link goes there.

1. Confirm that your MBBS or BDS is from an institution the regulator recognises, before you spend anything on premises.
2. Doctors: after the final MBBS exam, apply to your State Medical Council for provisional registration for the internship, as set out in the [NMC's 2023 registration regulations](https://www.nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/yU2SAi2t2zlfbXoTSXbrFzYcG8eRMTBsriQyaGPA.pdf).
3. Doctors: complete the one-year internship required by the [NMC's internship regulations](https://www.nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/Lpv9Va5WsAzpQmISmwAMWmsefK2nOEroAdNZkVJC.pdf) and keep the completion certificate.
4. Gather certified copies of your degree certificate, internship certificate, marksheets, photo ID and certificate of good standing.
5. Doctors: apply to the State Medical Council of the state where the clinic will be for permanent registration, and ask whether it takes applications through the National Medical Register portal linked from [nmc.org.in](https://www.nmc.org.in/). Keep the fee receipt.
6. Dentists: register with your State Dental Council. The [National Dental Commission's list](https://dciindia.gov.in/ListofSDC.aspx) gives each council's contact details.
7. Find out which law registers clinics in your state. The [Clinical Establishments Act site](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/) lists the 19 states and union territories that have adopted the Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010. If yours is one, register the clinic under the Act and your state's rules; if not, ask your state health department.
8. Get the State Pollution Control Board's approval for bio-medical waste, and its consent to establish and operate if your bed count calls for it.
9. Check the building's occupancy and completion certificates, and whether your state's fire rules require a fire NOC.
10. Register any X-ray machine with the [Atomic Energy Regulatory Board](https://www.aerb.gov.in/english/), and get a drug licence under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, if you will sell medicines.
11. Check that every clinician you hire is registered with their council, and run a background check on all staff.
12. Display your registration number in the clinic and on prescriptions, certificates and money receipts, and put the clinic's registration certificate on the wall.
13. Put every registration and renewal fee in the diary and pay it on time.

## What slows registration down

### Leaving the degree check until last

If your degree is not recognised, everything else waits. Check it before the internship paperwork and well before you look at premises.

### Sending an incomplete file

Gather and certify every document before you apply, because a missing one delays the approval. If you have already signed a lease, every week of delay is rent on a room you cannot use.

### Going through an agent

Apply to your council yourself and use the official sites, [nmc.org.in](https://www.nmc.org.in/) for doctors and [dciindia.gov.in](https://dciindia.gov.in/) for dentists. A third party adds the risk of a scam.

### Missing a renewal

Pay the registration and renewal fees on time, so your practice is not interrupted.

### Working from old information

Each state has its own rules for medical and dental council registration, and the national rules have moved recently: the NMC issued new registration regulations in 2023, and the National Dental Commission replaced the DCI in March 2026. Check with your state council before each step rather than relying on a guide, this one included.
